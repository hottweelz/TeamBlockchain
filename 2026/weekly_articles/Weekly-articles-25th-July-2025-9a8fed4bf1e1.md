**Weekly articles 25th July 2025**

**1.** [**10x bigger than Bitcoin, Tether, and Gold: China's Central Bank Digital Currency will dwarf them all**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2YldACOBJ8)**-** This video presents a thought-provoking view: rather than directly challenging the U.S. dollar, China might repurpose its massive dollar reserves by tokenising them mirroring the stablecoin model. Observing how Tether operates at scale, backed 1:1 by dollars and facilitating trillions in global transactions, China envisions a digital-dollar platform built and regulated from Hong Kong. This could overhaul cross-border finance by enabling programmable, blockchain-native dollar liquidity leveraging China’s reserves to assert financial influence without dethroning the dollar. By adopting a dollar-pegged digital asset under Chinese governance, Hong Kong could emerge as a major global stablecoin hub potentially rivalling U.S. financial systems. This evolutionary model equips China with a new tool to reshape dollar liquidity flows while avoiding direct confrontation with U.S. currency hegemony.

**Broader implications**

* Signals a shift in the reserve currency dynamic: the power of dollar reserves could extend digitally, not just geopolitically.
* Encourages private-sector innovation in programmable, globally settled money via stablecoin-style solutions.
* Speeds up the global pivot toward regulated digital currencies—and may inspire other reserve holders to explore similar strategies.

China isn’t battling the U.S. dollar it’s reengineering it. The question now is whether Washington will respond by embracing tokenised reserves or risk ceding financial architecture to Beijing.

[**2. The stablecoin race-**](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/econographics/the-stablecoin-race/) America’s debt crisis is reaching new heights, with over $36 trillion owed and more than $14 trillion coming due soon much of it requiring refinancing at sharply higher rates. Annual deficits aren’t shrinking; in fact, recent tax cut extensions and bipartisan spending promises could push the national debt-to-GDP ratio from 120% today toward an astonishing 166% within 30 years if left unchecked. Interest alone could soon consume $300 billion more per year, tightening the squeeze on government budgets and forcing painful choices about future investments in growth. Against this backdrop, stablecoins are emerging as a surprising potential ally. Not only can stablecoins offer a faster more efficient method to make payments, especially cross border payments but potentially a significant buyer of US government debt, becoming the third-largest purchaser of short-term Treasury bills after JPMorgan and China. For a future Trump administration, harnessing this demand could help absorb record bond issuance and ease upward pressure on interest rates.

Yet the picture is deeply paradoxical. While stablecoins may buy time, underlying US fiscal policy is still adrift, with neither party ready to tackle the tough adjustments needed for sustainability. History shows Congress can implement austerity and restore balance—but only after markets force its hand. If confidence in US finances falters, even stablecoin demand may not be enough to prevent borrowing costs from spiking, potentially triggering a fiscal crisis with global repercussions. In the race to stabilise America’s balance sheet, the embrace of stablecoins is as much a sign of political desperation as it is of digital-age innovation.

[**3.The rise and risk of stablecoins (FT)**](https://archive.is/jx7tf#selection-2179.0-2231.248)**-** The passage of the **GENIUS Act** in the US marks a seismic shift in the evolution of money, giving regulatory legitimacy to privately issued, US$ stablecoins, Washington has thrown open the doors to a new era of digital payments, positioning the greenback to dominate global finance and extend the longevity of the US$ as the world’s reserve currency. But beneath the surface of this bold innovation lies a tangle of unresolved risks and geopolitical tensions.

The GENIUS Act aims to ensure Stablecoins, though marketed as safe and simple, will be deeply embedded into traditional financial markets forcing them to acquire short-term Treasuries and bank deposits. This potentially creates fragile linkages that could transmit shocks at unprecedented digital speed. A run on stablecoins during market stress could reverberate through money markets, banks, and sovereign debt. History offers a sobering parallel in the 19th-century “free banking” era, where privately issued currencies led to chaos and forced governments to intervene. The US bet on private stablecoins contrasts sharply with Europe and China’s pursuit of central bank digital currencies (CBDCs), reflecting competing visions for monetary sovereignty and global influence. The looming question isn’t just about financial stability; it’s about which model private innovation or public issuance will define the future of money.

As the world races toward digitised payments, we are hurtling into uncharted territory where financial innovation and geopolitical ambition collide. Will stablecoins accelerate efficiency or become the flashpoint for the next financial crisis? And in this high-stakes game, can Europe afford to remain cautious or is its sovereignty already slipping away?

**5.** [**Western Union CEO Says Stablecoins Are a Store-of-Value Opportunity, Not a Threat**](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/western-union-ceo-stablecoins-store-of-value-opportunity-not-threat/) **-** Western Union CEO Devin McGranahan’s recent remarks signal a powerful shift in how legacy financial institutions are reimagining their roles in a world increasingly influenced by blockchain technology and digital assets. Rather than resisting the rise of stablecoins, McGranahan embraces them not as a disruptive force, but as a strategic opportunity to enhance Western Union’s global reach. His message is clear: **speed, access, and stability** are the real currencies of value in a fragmented global financial system. Stablecoins promise to accelerate cross-border payments something Western Union has been facilitating for nearly two centuries and McGranahan sees blockchain as an enabler, not a competitor. He notes, for instance, that while stablecoins aren’t yet accepted for buying something as simple as a Coca-Cola, Western Union can step in to solve that real-world usability gap by acting as a **conversion bridge** between stable value digital currencies and local fiat, especially in hard-to-bank regions.

What’s more compelling is his acknowledgment of stablecoins as **a lifeline for customers in volatile economies.** In many parts of the world, preserving wealth in local currency is like trying to hold water in a sieve. Stablecoins offer these users a refuge — and for Western Union, a new role: not just remittances, but also **digital safe haven** provider. Despite fears of a shrinking customer base, he insists Western Union’s clientele is rooted, established, and integral to the US economy. The real growth story, he implies, lies **outside American borders**, where demand for dollar stability and frictionless payments continues to grow.

Ultimately, this moment feels like a tipping point. Western Union is no longer just in the money transfer business it’s positioning itself as a **global on-and-off ramp between the digital and fiat worlds,** leveraging its trust, scale, and compliance infrastructure to meet the future of money on its own terms. **The real question now is not whether stablecoins will disrupt incumbents but whether those incumbents can transform quickly enough to stay relevant.** Western Union, it seems, intends to try.

[**7.The UK bank Starling looks to list on NYSE-**](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46293/starling-eyes-nyse-listing?ct=t(email_campaign_7_19_2025_13_16)&mc_cid=3a2d2d2f65&mc_eid=562a6d0508) **-** Once a poster child for London’s post-Brexit ambition to remain a global financial innovation hub, Starling may now become the latest in a growing list of firms **disillusioned with the UK's capital markets.** Despite former statements asserting London as its “natural home,” Starling’s CFO Declan Ferguson’s recent comments to the Financial Times suggest a **sobering reassessment of market realities**. The bank is now openly weighing a **New York IPO** due to better **valuation prospects**, deeper investor pools, and heightened tech-sector visibility in the US. If Starling proceeds, it will echo Wise’s move last month to shift its primary listing from London to New York. For the UK government and especially new Chancellor Rachel Reeves the implications are stark. The fintech sector was meant to be a **growth engine and post-Brexit success story**, with a thriving IPO market as its crown jewel. Instead, the IPO pipeline risks being redirected across the Atlantic.

Starling’s transatlantic aspirations aren't just financial they are strategic. With **expansion plans in the US**, including a potential acquisition of a US bank, listing on a US exchange could provide more than just capital: it would offer **regulatory familiarity, investor alignment, and brand legitimacy** in the world’s most important financial market. More broadly, this moment reflects the **increasing gravitational pull of the US financial ecosystem.** UK FinTechs like **Monzo and Revolut** are also looking westward, not just for customers, but for capital, scale, and relevance.

If London can no longer retain its brightest fintech stars, it raises urgent questions:

* Is the UK’s regulatory, tax, and listing environment aligned with growth-stage innovation?
* Can a global financial centre thrive without anchoring the companies of the future?
* And more critically, what structural changes are needed to **reclaim London’s appeal?**

As the fintech sector matures, it’s not just about who can innovate, but **where that innovation chooses to live.** If London wants to be more than a historical financial centre, it must confront the uncomfortable reality that it is **currently losing the future it helped build.**
