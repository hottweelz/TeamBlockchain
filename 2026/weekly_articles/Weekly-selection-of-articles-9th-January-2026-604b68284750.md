[**Citi bank extols the virtues of banking using blockchains**](https://www.pymnts.com/blockchain/2025/citi-pulls-blockchain-into-the-banking-core/)Blockchain’s long-promised disruption of finance is finally arriving — but not as a revolt against banks. Instead, it is being absorbed into the plumbing of global markets. As Citi’s Biswarup Chatterjee argues, the technology’s value lies less in crypto-native instruments than in its ability to deliver always-on, programmable and data-rich infrastructure that aligns financial services with the realities of 24/7 global commerce. The shift reflects a structural mismatch that has grown impossible to ignore. Trade, platforms and supply chains now operate continuously across borders, while banking systems remain constrained by batch processing, cut-off times and fragmented data. Blockchain addresses this gap by producing natively digital, standardised records that can move liquidity and information in real time. Crucially, large banks are deploying it quietly — embedding blockchain into existing systems rather than forcing clients to adopt new rails. For financial markets, the implications are profound. Settlement cycles compress, reconciliation costs fall and liquidity management becomes more precise. Tokenised deposits, securities and collateral can coexist with legacy instruments, provided interoperability and custody standards are robust. This integration-first approach signals that blockchain is becoming infrastructure, not ideology. The result is a financial system evolving toward continuous markets, where speed, resilience and data transparency are no longer optional, and where competitive advantage will increasingly hinge on who can industrialise blockchain at scale.
2. [**I lost £172,000 while buying my home**](https://archive.ph/BBbH2)The quiet plumbing of the payments system is becoming a source of growing friction in modern commerce. Nowhere is this clearer than in the reliance on lawyer-controlled client accounts to intermediate transactions that could, in theory, settle instantly peer-to-peer. Designed for an analogue era, client accounts were built to manage trust where real-time settlement and transparent ownership did not exist. Today, they increasingly represent delay, opacity and concentrated operational risk. High-profile failures and freezes of client accounts have exposed a structural weakness: funds legally belonging to clients are routinely trapped in pooled accounts, subject to human error, insolvency risk and slow judicial processes. Settlement timelines stretch from minutes to weeks; disputes require lawyers to resolve problems created by lawyers’ infrastructure. The result is cost; uncertainty and liquidity drag across property, M&A and commercial transactions. Peer-to-peer payment, whether via instant payments, tokenised deposits or regulated stablecoins, offer a cleaner alternative. They allow value to move directly between parties, with settlement finality, auditability and programmable controls embedded in the payment itself. Crucially, they reduce the need for trusted intermediaries whose role is administrative rather than economic. For financial markets, the implication is stark. Trust is shifting from institutions to infrastructure. As payments become atomic and transparent, the rationale for holding client money “in trust” weakens. The future of settlement is not better escrow; it is no escrow at all.

[**Banks and regulators coming to terms with digital payments**](https://www.omfif.org/2026/01/outlook-2026-stablecoin-fretting-will-calm-down/?mc_cid=d24ae3a35a&mc_eid=8fd65fb32f)The grand stablecoin drama of 2025 has arrived at a curious denouement. Far from the "bloodbath" of a Macbethian tragedy, the rise of digital assets is concluding with a mere shuffling of the financial nobility. Traditional finance, bolstered by protective regulators, has moved to co-opt the challengers rather than succumb to them. In the UK and EU, the strategy is one of "state-sponsored containment." By mandating high reserve requirements at central banks, effectively turning stablecoin issuers into "regulated e-money platforms", authorities have neutralised the threat to the "singleness of money." For commercial banks, this is a reprieve. While stablecoins apply healthy competitive pressure on payments, they lack the structural advantages of the banking license: the ability to pay interest, provide deposit guarantees, and access the lender of last resort. The real threat to banks, deposit flight, is being managed through "mutually beneficial co-habitation." New models see banks converting stablecoins into deposits for customers, reconciling redemptions in the background. This allows banks to capture the efficiency of distributed ledger technology without losing their grip on the customer relationship. Ultimately, if banks successfully deploy tokenized deposits, they will likely outgun non-interest-bearing stablecoins. In developed markets, the "hour of strutting" for stablecoins may indeed signify a shift in technology, but the balance of power remains firmly with the incumbents, at least for now!

[**Will stablecoins preserve dollar dominance?**](japantimes.co.jp/commentary/2026/01/05/world/will-stablecoins-preserve-dollar-dominance/?__cf_chl_tk=wVzQ5SqcKfaeRx_vacKLTVxVTWVu_JKJ.ni68b7Gvyo-1768650672-1.0.1.1-d8hcWwUrzKzONIalYvLgInBzc1uKf.BIm06vp5Ipd7U)The GENIUS Act of 2025 has unleashed a stablecoin boom, with US Dollar-pegged tokens projected to hit $1.6 trillion by 2030, backed 1:1 by Treasuries and cash. Proponents argue this cements U.S. dollar dominance, creating new global payment rails faster and cheaper than SWIFT. Foreign users holding stablecoins boost demand for dollar assets, potentially lowering U.S. interest rates by 40 basis points via increased Treasury demand, acting like a modern "global saving glut." Yet, this bet carries risks. Stablecoins could erode bank deposits by $1-2 trillion, shrinking lending capacity and weakening monetary policy transmission. The Fed's rate hikes might not curb stablecoin growth, as they offer no yield but provide digital convenience. Unlike interest-bearing bank accounts, stablecoins sidestep deposit insurance, heightening run risks, evident in 2023's USDC de-peg. Ultimately, stablecoins may preserve US Dollar hegemony short-term but threaten banking resilience. A hybrid approach, regulating stablecoins while exploring a digital US Dollar, could balance innovation and stability. Reduced bank deposits from stablecoin adoption would shrink lending capacity, pushing banks to raise deposit rates or tighten credit. This could exert upward pressure on short-term interest rates and borrowing costs economy-wide, especially if the Fed resists monetising debt. Conversely, sustained US Dollar demand via stablecoins might ease long-term Treasury yields by supporting foreign reserve holdings. The net effect remains uncertain, hinging on adoption scale and regulatory containment of deposit flight.

[**Instant Settlement: Treasury’s Shiny New Toy – Or Expensive Distraction**](https://treasurymastermind.com/on-chain-settlement-beyond-the-hype-and-into-the-messy-middle/)**-** In 2025, the corporate treasury landscape has split into two parallel realities. While "instant settlement" is the industry’s most intoxicating buzzword, the operational impact on CFOs is a mix of high-velocity opportunity and integration friction. For CFOs, on-chain settlement offers the "holy grail" of liquidity: reducing cross-border transfer times from days to seconds and slashing costs by up to **70%** in expensive corridors. However, the "last mile" remains a significant hurdle. Most existing Treasury Management Systems (TMS) still operate on batch-processing cycles, creating a mismatch where money moves in real-time while the General Ledger updates at end-of-day.

### Key Impacts on Treasury Managers

* **Intraday Liquidity Pressure:** The shift to 24/7 settlement requires managers to maintain larger, more active liquidity buffers to support round-the-clock operations.
* **Reconciliation Complexity:** Automating the reconciliation of fragmented data across disparate systems remains a major challenge for **60%** of treasurers.
* **Shift to "Hybrid Finance":** Leading firms are adopting a multi-modal approach—using stablecoin rails for urgent intercompany moves (emergency repatriation or just-in-time funding) while keeping standard suppliers on traditional rails.
* **Asset Safeguarding:** Treasurers are now assuming "custodian-like" responsibilities; the absolute nature of on-chain finality means losing a private key or sending to a wrong address is often irreversible.

The pragmatists of 2025 are not replacing their entire stack. Instead, they are building integration layers that treat on-chain settlement as a specialized tool for high-value, time-sensitive use cases.
