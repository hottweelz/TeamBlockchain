[**Coinbase Earnings Show Crypto Is Eating Wall Street**](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/30/coinbase-shares-rise-on-third-quarter-earnings-beat-.html)Coinbase’s latest results underline how far crypto has come from its outsider roots to the heart of global finance. Founded in 2012 as a bitcoin on-ramp, the company now commands the scale and profitability of a major financial institution. Its latest quarterly earnings showed **$1.87 billion in revenue, $433 million in net income**, and an astonishing enough to rank among the top ten U.S. banks by deposits. What’s striking is not only Coinbase’s size but its cultural reach. Coinbase’s CEO, Briam Armstrong’s, impromptu reference to a **Polymarket prediction market** during the earnings call—sparked by a team link shared mid-meeting, signalled how crypto’s participatory ethos is bleeding into corporate finance itself. Prediction markets, long niche curiosities, are becoming feedback loops for real-world sentiment, where the prediction markets are offering investors a new tool to gauge crowd sentiment. Coinbase’s trajectory encapsulates the convergence of traditional and decentralised finance. Once dismissed as a speculative exchange, it now sits at the centre of digital asset custody, payments, and tokenisation infrastructure. As regulation stabilises and institutional adoption accelerates, Coinbase looks less like a crypto exchange and more like a **universal financial platform** built for the tokenised economy. Wall Street isn’t being disrupted, it’s being absorbed.

[**McKinsey & Company Global Banking Annual Review**](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-banking-annual-review) **global** banks are posting record-breaking profits and intermediating vast sums of assets, yet investor scepticism remains deeply entrenched as average price-to-book ratio sits nearly 70% below that of other industries. Between 2019 and 2024 the banking system’s assets grew by some 40% and revenues after risk exceeded $5.5 trillion in 2024 yet the sector still trades well below its cost of capital. The report argues that banks have ridden favourable tailwinds high interest rates, low risk costs and surging global wealth but those tailwinds are fading. Meanwhile the structural forces reshaping finance platform-based competition, AI-driven entrants and balance-sheet light business models are beginning to bite. For banks that cling to “scale for scale’s sake”, the message is blunt: size alone no longer confers advantage. Instead, McKinsey emphasises a new formula for success precision in strategy, technology, capital deployment and customer experience. In essence, banks are at an inflection point. They must transition from riding the wealth-and-rates wave to mastering the business of pinpointing where value truly lies. Those that do may well reclaim investor trust; those that don’t may simply see the earnings stunt as an interlude before the real value challenge begins. The report notes in the “agentic AI” chapter, McKinsey estimates that **costs** in banking could be cut by as much as 70% via AI, though net cost-base reductions may more realistically be only 15-20% after offsetting technology investments. McKinsey also highlights that despite banks spending roughly **$600 billion a year on technology**, labour productivity growth has been “mixed”, suggesting that scale of tech investment alone has not delivered commensurate productivity gains.

[**1,000x Faster Than Silicon: The Rise of Photonic Chips**](https://mail.google.com/mail/u/0/#inbox/QgrcJHrjCssJvdtwcJXgHgZWKNRmxjnLFRB?projector=1) We had a lot of interest in the new synthetic diamond chips that use less power than traditional silicon chips. This YouTube video examines how light e.g. photonics could be a solution. Photonic chips represent a tectonic shift in computing’s evolution, poised to overcome the fundamental physical limits of silicon-based electronics. As transistors approach atomic scales, electrons face barriers of heat and leakage, threatening the decades-old promise of Moore’s Law. Photonics, using photons or light waves instead of electrons offers not only blistering speed and efficiency but also a radically different architecture where data flows uninterrupted without electrical resistance or collision delays. Yet the key obstacle was memory: light can transmit and compute swiftly but cannot easily remember states. Recent breakthroughs embedding phase-change materials into microscopic resonators solve this, anchoring information in photonic circuits and enabling simultaneous memory and processing on the same chip. This convergence dismantles the energy and latency bottlenecks of traditional separate compute and memory units, potentially unleashing unprecedented computing power at dramatically lower energy footprints. For artificial intelligence, whose rapidly escalating energy demand threatens sustainability, photonic chips offer a lifeline processor fuelled by the power of a single bulb running at “the speed of light.” Beyond AI, these chips promise transformative advances in scientific simulations, climate modelling, and finance, shrinking years of computation to moments. However, significant engineering challenges remain before photonics fully supplants electronics. Component size, material durability, and ecosystem readiness raise obstacles. Nonetheless, a global race has begun.

[**Wharton Study: 75% Companies using AI see positive results**](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2025/wharton-finds-82-of-leaders-use-gen-ai-weekly/)According to a new study by Wharton School in the USA 82% of enterprise leaders now use generative AI (Gen AI) at least weekly, a dramatic jump from 37% in 2023. The shift marks Gen AI’s journey from lab-experiment to operational staple, with 75% of the 800 companies surveyed reporting positive returns. Use cases that once sat on the fringe data analysis, content creation, summary generation have become embedded workflows. Yet the rapid rise raises a paradox: even as tools proliferate, human skill-atrophy looms. Some 43% of leaders fear that employees’ core abilities may degrade as reliance on Gen AI increases. The message for firms is clear: the frontier is no longer “if we deploy AI” but “how we embed it while preserving human capital and governance.” Mere adoption is insufficient what matters now is disciplined measurement, responsible use, and talent readiness. Those organisations that build deep fluency with Gen AI and simultaneously guard against workforce erosion may well set the agenda for the next decade of value creation.

[**Imperial College London, How Stablecoins can Secure the UK’s Financial Future**](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/business-school/news/mind-the-gap-how-stablecoins-can-secure-the-uks-financial-future/). Stablecoins have transcended their early experimental phase to become critical infrastructure in the evolving architecture of digital finance, marrying the stability of fiat currency with the efficiency and programmability of blockchain technology. Yet, their rise exposes a vexing policy paradox for regulators: how to integrate these private digital monies into the mainstream financial system without compromising monetary sovereignty or financial stability. A central dilemma lies in their reserve compositions. Unlike the U.S. Treasury market, smaller markets such as UK gilts lack liquidity to absorb large-scale stablecoin issuance unless the UK government finances its huge borrowings with shorter term debt instruments. This underscores the need for regulator-imposed collateral standards that balance market capacity with the imperative to maintain peg credibility under stress. Moreover, regulatory fragmentation threatens to leave national economies at the mercy of foreign-denominated tokens, risking “digital dollarisation” and the erosion of domestic monetary control. Regulators face the unsettled question of whether to treat stablecoins as payments instruments, narrow banks, or distinct financial utilities altogether. A nuanced ‘same risk, same rules’ approach is vital to prevent arbitrage and preserve competitive innovation, especially as tokenised money market funds and deposits blur traditional distinctions. The promise of programmable digital cash could catalyse a more inclusive, resilient, and efficient financial ecosystem, but realizing that potential demands urgent clarity, international coordination, and a balanced policy toolkit that embraces innovation while safeguarding systemic integrity. The stakes for economic leadership and monetary sovereignty could not be higher.

[**Standard Chartered CEO expects blockchain to ‘eventually’ power nearly all global transactions**](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/03/standard-chartered-ceo-believes-nearly-all-global-transactions-will-move-to-the-blockchain-eventually.html)**.** Standard Chartered’s CEO Bill Winters believes within the next decade, nearly all cross-border transactions will migrate to blockchain-based platforms, with money in fully digital form. Speaking at Hong Kong FinTech Week, Winters described this evolution not as incremental change but as a “complete rewiring of the financial system”, a shift echoing the optimism of other industry leaders such as BlackRock and Robinhood. Yet, Winters concedes that the precise contours of this transformation remain uncertain, highlighting a period of experimentation in regulatory and technological frameworks, particularly in hubs such as Hong Kong.​ Standard Chartered’s growing emphasis on digital asset custody, crypto trading integration with mainstream FX systems, and partnerships to pilot HKD-backed stablecoins signals that institutional banking is now at the vanguard of this transition. Winters referenced Hong Kong’s deliberate approach to licensing and tokenisation as a blueprint for global adoption, calling HKD stablecoins a potential catalyst for international commerce on blockchain rails.​ If Winters’ vision materialises, traditional intermediaries may face existential pressures as programmable, transparent, and frictionless digital assets supplant cash and legacy settlement networks. The revelation is not just technological, but strategic, forcing banks, regulators, and corporates to rethink their role as blockchain converges with global economic infrastructure.​
