1. [**EU to make transactions over €10,000 illegal. Will this fuel demand for Peer to Peer € stablecoins?**](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2025/11/13/no-brussels-didnt-just-criminalise-cash#:~:text=%22Every%20euro%20above%20%E2%82%AC10%2C000,EU%20hasn't%20banned%20cash.)From July 2027, the European Union’s Anti-Money Laundering package imposes a bloc-wide cap of €10,000 on cash payments in business transactions. All cash deals above that threshold will simply be prohibited for businesses, though the actual use of cash, saving, withdrawing, or holding, remains entirely legal.​ What does this mean in practice? Buying a car in cash at a dealership for more than €10,000 will be illegal, but buying from a private seller falls outside the law’s remit. Everyday commerce, groceries, rent, and sundries, won’t be affected, nor do the rules ban cash in private transactions unless a specific country already imposes stricter limits. Member states can still set lower national caps, [as Italy (€5,000)](https://stripe.com/gb/resources/more/understanding-the-2025-cash-payment-limit-italy#:~:text=In%202025%2C%20the%20limit%20for%20cash%20payments,or%20debit%20cards%2C%20or%20checks%20are%20mandatory.) and [Spain (€1,000)](https://www.europe-consommateurs.eu/en/shopping-internet/cash-payment-limitations.html#:~:text=Payments%20in%20connection%20with%20property,Greece) have done, making regulatory harmonisation partial at best.​ The real intent? To tighten Europe’s grip on illicit flows that too often course through untraceable banknotes, estimated at nearly €2 trillion globally for money laundering each year. The cap forces large deals into digital or bank-traced channels, heightening transparency and shrinking criminal opportunity. Sensational posts may stoke old “war on cash” paranoia, but the new AML rules signal a calculated pivot to auditability not the abolition of physical money itself. But in doing so will AI fuel demand for Digital € or other € stablecoins?

[**Why the Future of Blockchain Payments Could Stay Narrow**](https://www.pymnts.com/blockchain/2025/why-future-blockchain-payments-could-stay-narrow/) This article in Payments argues blockchain’s future in payments is unlikely to resemble the sweeping, universal platforms long promised by its most vocal evangelists. Instead, we will see a narrower, more pragmatic trajectory defined by specialist deployments as opposed to a unified global settlement layer. The core issue is structural, payments do not behave like digital platforms. Their network effects stem from liquidity, compliance, trust, and deep integration with existing financial rails, not from developer ecosystems or token-driven incentives. This is why crypto-native “platform-first” strategies have failed to achieve mass payment adoption. The abstraction is too broad and the incentives misaligned. By contrast, real-world progress is emerging in tightly bounded, high-friction financial workflows: cross-border corporate payouts, loyalty settlement, supply-chain finance, invoice reconciliation and VAT refunds. These are domains where blockchain technology is not competing with retail payment networks but with spreadsheets, batch files and ageing ERP systems. NH NongHyup Bank’s stablecoin-based VAT refund pilot with Avalanche, Mastercard and Worldpay is emblematic. It targets a specific pain point with measurable efficiency gains, automation, instant settlement and reduced friction. Visa’s Mark Nelsen echoes the logic: stablecoins are most powerful not in consumer payments but in cross-border niches where traditional rails remain slow or unreliable. Crucially, incumbents, not crypto firms, are driving these deployments. Their incentives centre on operational efficiency, compliance and customer experience rather than token circulation. If blockchains are to scale, it will likely do so through a constellation of specialised systems rather than a single, all-encompassing network. The revolution may be quieter, but far more durable.

[**Saudis and Trump to tokenise luxury hotel in the Maldives**](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/dar-global-trump-tokenize-maldives-hotel/) Saudi developer Dar Global and the Trump Organisation are going to tokenise and offer the fractionalisation ownership of a luxury hotel development in the Maldives before a single villa is built. The project, an 80-villa Trump-branded resort slated for completion in 2028, will allow investors to buy tokens representing exposure to the development phase itself, a model its promoters describe as a “global first”. The move signals how fast tokenisation is shifting from proof-of-concept to premium-asset fundraising. Deloitte forecasts that real-estate tokenisation could reach **$4tn by 2035,** up from just $0.3tn in 2024, with early growth concentrated in undeveloped land and construction-phase assets. For Dar Global, already experimenting with NFTs via its 2022 Oman AIDA project, tokenisation is becoming a strategic differentiator, a way to globalise access to high-value properties while embedding blockchain-based transaction records into its investment model. Yet the wider implications extend beyond marketing gloss. If successful, the Maldives initiative could reshape how luxury hospitality projects are financed, shifting risk, liquidity and investor participation away from traditional debt-equity structures. It also raises deeper questions: will regulated token platforms mature quickly enough to support cross-border retail participation? And does fractionalisation democratise access, or further financialise prime property markets? **Maldives is under Sharia law, perfect for tokenisation with yield.**
5. **[PRA to increase FSC bank deposit protection to £120,000](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/november/pra-confirms-fscs-deposit-limit-to-be-increased-to-120000-from-1-december?utm_source=Bank+of+England+updates&utm_campaign=48c61f1a75-EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_2025_11_18_09_53&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_-48c61f1a75-113555002).** The last time the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSC) limits were increased was in 2017 from £75,000 to £85,000. If you are wondering why the limit is now £120,000 maybe because according the [Bank of England’s inflation calculator](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/inflation/inflation-calculator)  £85,000 in 2018 after inflation is now £111,490 (up to September 2025). The UK is preparing to strengthen depositor protection with the guaranteed savings limit rising from **£85,000 to £120,000** from December. The Prudential Regulation Authority approved the change signalling a renewed push to reinforce confidence after a period of economic uncertainty. The Financial Services Compensation Scheme will continue to apply its guarantee **per person, per authorised firm**, meaning customers with multiple brands under one banking licence must still monitor aggregated balances. Temporary high-balance protection, covering proceeds from events such as property sales, will also increase from £1million to **£1.4million.** Regulators argue that bolstering deposit security supports both trust and stability. Consumer groups agree, noting the move strengthens confidence without constraining growth but at the expense of the banks that would have to pay as the scheme remains industry-funded through levies imposed on regulated firms.

[**BIS reports daily FX is in excess of $9.6 trillion A DAY!**](https://www.risk.net/our-take/7962354/do-bis-volumes-soar-past-the-trend)The latest BIS Triennial survey reveals an average FX turnover of **US$9.6 trillion a day**, up 28% from 2022. On the surface, this suggests a structurally larger foreign-exchange market. But the devil lies in the data: April 2025 was anything but typical, jam-packed with tariff shocks, trade policy upheaval and spike-driven hedging. As the survey itself notes, such episodic surges may have inflated the headline number. That leads to a subtle but vital question for dealers and risk managers: Is the market genuinely bigger, or simply louder? The distinction matters for models, capital buffers, liquidity plans and strategic infrastructure. Sloppy interpretation risks baking transitory volume spikes into assumptions about steady-state activity. Traders must ask whether the boom is driven by enduring demand or short-term policy shocks. The verdict: treat headline growth with caution, scrutinise the base trends, and prepare systems for elevated, but not necessarily permanent, volume regimes. In any event with traded volume such as this it is no surprise institutions are looking at how tokenisation can improve the efficiency and reduce the costs in global FCX markets. [An ISDA and ANT](https://www.isda.org/2025/07/03/isda-and-ant-international-lead-new-industry-report-on-use-of-tokenized-bank-liabilities-for-fx-settlement-and-cross-border-payments-under-project-guardian/#_ftn1) concluded: *“FX-related risks and costs remain a major hurdle in cross-market and cross-currency payments….On top of limited settlement windows, they also face time zone delays and different settlement assets and platforms. Lessons for future payments and asset management?**](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2025/57percent-united-states-adults-use-gen-ai-millennials-pull-ahead-productivity/)Generative AI adoption is accelerating across the US, but its uptake varies sharply by age, revealing not just a technological divide, but a preview of future market behaviour. According to PYMNTS Intelligence, 57% of US adults, approximately 149 million people now use generative AI. Usage is highest among people born before mid 1990’s (GenZ), where nearly two-thirds rely on AI for personal and professional tasks. Younger workers, see AI as a productivity engine, while older people remain sceptical, citing privacy concerns and unfamiliarity. Paradoxically, Gen Z is both the most enthusiastic and the most fearful of job displacement, with nearly 40% concerned they could be replaced by AI. This generational divergence carries material implications for capital markets. Younger cohorts, who overwhelmingly prefer automated tools over human intermediaries, are poised to inherit an estimated [$124 trillion in assets globally over the coming decades](https://fortune.com/2025/07/23/great-wealth-transfer-124-trillion-bigger-than-ever-millennials-gen-x/). As wealth shifts downward, investment behaviour will shift with it: portfolios will need to be **digitised, programmable, and tradable 24/7**, while payments, asset servicing, and treasury operations must be **AI-native**, not merely digitised versions of analogue systems. The emerging reality is clear: AI adoption isn’t just shaping consumer behaviour; it is pre-wiring future capital flows. Institutions that fail to adapt risk becoming structurally irrelevant to the next generation of wealth holders.
