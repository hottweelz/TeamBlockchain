**Weekly articles 1st August 2025**

**1.** [**McKinsey - The stable door opens: How tokenized cash enables next-gen payments**](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/the-stable-door-opens-how-tokenized-cash-enables-next-gen-payments)**-** Stablecoins are rapidly evolving into a potential backbone of the global payments ecosystem. While they currently facilitate less than 1% of daily global money transfers, 2025 may mark an inflection point as regulatory clarity, technological maturity, and real-world use cases converge. These blockchain-based digital assets, pegged to fiat currencies such as the US dollar, are not just for traders anymore they are being adopted for remittances, B2B payments, treasury operations, and even institutional settlements. Despite their rising utility, stablecoins still face hurdles: they require robust off-ramps, suffer from regulatory fragmentation, and raise complex questions about financial stability. Yet momentum is building. The total value of stablecoins in circulation has doubled to $260 billion in just 18 months, and leading issuers like Tether and Circle now facilitate up to $30 billion in daily transactions. Tokenised money market funds are also emerging as yield-bearing alternatives, blending payments with investment.

With the passage of legislation such as the U.S. GENIUS Act and Europe’s MiCA regulation, and with firms like JPMorgan, BlackRock, and Stripe investing heavily in digital infrastructure, stablecoins are becoming institutional grade. Their ability to offer instant settlement, programmable compliance, and lower costs positions them to challenge legacy rails like SWIFT and Fedwire.

Stablecoins are fast becoming a cornerstone of financial infrastructure, these digital assets backed by short-term U.S. Treasuries and governed by the new **GENIUS Act,** they’re reshaping everything from capital markets to payments. Why does it matter? Because stablecoins don’t just move money faster. They shift where it's held. In emerging markets, they're a store of value. In commerce, they’re a way to **sidestep costly payment rails.** In DeFi, they eliminate the bank altogether. And their reserves held **in US Treasury bills** make issuers such as Tether major players in sovereign debt markets. Tether alone outpaced Germany and Canada as a U.S. Treasury holder in 2024.

But the risks are real. A **de-pegging event** or mass redemptions could mimic a digital-era bank run. And unlike traditional bank deposits, there’s **no FDIC insurance**. Stablecoins may not replace banks overnight, but they are quietly becoming **essential plumbing** in the new financial architecture.

[**3. (FT) The end of the Digital € and the Digital £?**](https://archive.is/75ntY)**-** The European Central Bank has issued a stark warning: the explosive rise of US dollar-backed stablecoins may quietly erode the Euro’s relevance and with it, Europe’s monetary autonomy. ECB official Jürgen Schaaf compares this shift to “digital dollarisation,” where widespread use of dollar stablecoins in the eurozone could undermine the ECB’s ability to control interest rates, manage liquidity, or even conduct effective monetary policy. What’s unfolding isn’t just a tech trend it’s a **currency power shift** ushering in the ability to offer T0 settlement and AI powered programmability with the full backing of the US administration.

As the US cements its regulatory regime through the GENIUS Act and dollar-pegged stablecoins gain legitimacy, the UK and Europe finds itself at a crossroads. Stablecoins now managing over $250 billion in reserves, with 90% US Dollar denominated and largely in US Treasuries, capital could gradually migrate out of Sterling and Euro-denominated assets, raising borrowing costs for the UK and European governments and businesses while tethering the countries closer to US monetary cycles.

This is not just about payments or financial efficiency it’s about **geopolitical leverage and control which if successful will extend the US Dollar as the worlds’ reserve currency**. US Dollar stablecoins, increasingly framed as tools of strategic soft power, could reduce the UK and Europe’s ability to act independently in times of crisis.

Schaaf’s call is urgent: without a credible digital euro, Europe may sleepwalk into a future where monetary sovereignty is no longer its own, meanwhile the Bank of England remains silent. UK and Europe need to respond why not allow issue clear regulatory guidance to enable privately issued GBP and € denominated stablecoins? Initially cap them to say £5billion/€6billion with full KYC/AML functionality and then see who, how and where they are used.

**[4. (FT) Britan needs smart regulation not endless consultation papers](https://archive.is/2ICJc%22%20%5Cl%20%22selection-2139.0-2837.265) -** While the UK’s Financial Conduct Authority opens a 250-page consultation on stablecoins, the rest of the world has already moved on. Japan, Singapore, Abu Dhabi, and most recently the US with the passing of the landmark **GENIUS Act** have not just regulated stablecoins, they've embraced them as foundational to the future of finance. In contrast, the UK dithers. A once-promising opportunity to establish London as the blockchain capital of the post-Brexit world has been squandered by regulatory inertia and bureaucratic risk-aversion. A dinner with Circle’s CEO in 2021 could have sparked action. Instead, it ended with a dismissive remark and years of lost momentum. Meanwhile, the US sees what’s at stake. Investors like Scott Bessent frame stablecoins as tools of **dollar supremacy**, innovation, and financial inclusion. With stablecoins increasingly used for settlement, lending, and payments, the stakes are clear: this is a new infrastructure especially for capital markets.

The UK faces a strategic choice: join the dynamic, open model of private-sector stablecoins, or default to the statist, central-bank-controlled model preferred by the EU and China. Delay means decline. If UK regulators want a lesson in smart policy, they should book flights and go look listen and learn from regulators that are happy to embrace smart regulation.

[**5. FedX to use JP Morgan’s tokenised deposits**](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/fedex-adopts-kinexys-by-jp-morgan-for-blockchain-based-payments/) **-** FedEx’s decision to integrate J.P. Morgan Payments’ Supply Chain Finance solution directly into Oracle’s Cloud ERP marks a significant shift in how large enterprises approach liquidity and supplier relationships. By embedding financing tools natively within their ERP system, FedEx bypasses the traditional complexity of custom integrations, compressing months of development. This not only streamlines internal operations but empowers FedEx’s vendors with choice: wait for payment on extended terms or receive early funding based on FedEx’s own credit profile a strategic move that subtly shifts the balance of working capital optimisation across the supply chain.

Powered by J.P. Morgan’s staggering daily payments volume of over $10 trillion and its blockchain-driven Kinexys platform, already handling more than $2 billion in transactions each day. The scale and fluidity of these systems point toward a future where liquidity is no longer bound by geographic or institutional silos, but is dynamically allocated across time zones and markets, as evidenced by FedEx’s liquidity pooling across Europe and Asia Pacific. At the same time, J.P. Morgan is doubling down on innovation beyond enterprise finance. Its partnership with Coinbase to integrate into consumer banking, including reward point transfers and secure bank-to-wallet links, signals a growing commitment to reshaping how value is stored, moved, and spent.

These developments illustrate a deeper convergence of financial infrastructure, digital liquidity, and programmable capital. Institutions such as FedEx and J.P. Morgan are not just optimising for cost and efficiency, they’re laying the groundwork for a financial system that is faster, more flexible, and increasingly decentralised.
