**Weekly Intelligence Brief 9th October 2026**

This week’s Intelligence Brief examines how AI agents, stablecoins and tokenized assets are converging to create a machine-native financial economy in which software can negotiate, invest and move money. These developments could accelerate capital circulation, reduce operating costs and support economic growth, but they also expose unresolved questions concerning liquidity, digital identity, regulatory responsibility, human oversight and systemic resilience.

[**AI Agents Are Starting to Negotiate With Banks: Sibos Says Payments Are Going Machine-to-Machine**](https://www.fintechfutures.com/ai-in-fintech/sibos-2026-agentic-payments-take-centre-stage-on-day-three)**.** Agentic payments moved from theory towards operational reality at Sibos 2026. Mastercard, Société Générale, Nasdaq, Standard Chartered and State Street argued that adoption depends on verified identity, transferable trust, clear authority, common standards and liability rules. One Mastercard client has already received a call from an AI agent seeking to renegotiate a customer’s credit-card terms, exposing how unprepared existing procedures can be. State Street says an agent used for trade reconciliation achieves 99.5%-99.9% accuracy, versus 80%-85% for human-led processes.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** The wider economic impact could be substantial. Agents could automate onboarding, treasury, payments, purchasing and data acquisition, lower operating costs and enabling machine-speed commerce. They may also intensify competition as agents continuously compare providers and negotiate terms. For businesses, the challenge is therefore how identity, mandates, fraud controls, human oversight and accountability are established before machines can move money autonomously.

[**Tokenized Assets Exceed $34 Billion, but 93% of Trading Comes From Stocks**](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/30/3371933/0/en/dune-report-finds-tokenized-markets-often-diverge-from-the-assets-they-reference.html)**.** Dune Analytics reports that tokenized assets have exceeded $34 billion, but activity is uneven. Tokenized cash represents the largest asset class yet trades infrequently, while tokenized stocks account for 8 percent of market value but generate 93 percent of on chain trading. The findings challenge measures that treat assets issued on blockchain as evidence of active markets. Tokenization may digitise ownership without creating liquidity, demand or utility.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** issuers must focus on distribution, secondary markets and usable collateral rather than simply placing assets on chain. Investors should distinguish locked balances from turnover when assessing adoption and valuation. Efficient markets require interoperable platforms, market makers and settlement assets. If tokenized instruments remain inactive, promised productivity gains will be delayed. If liquidity improves, faster collateral circulation could reduce funding costs and increase the use of capital.

[**SoFiUSD Puts $25 Billion of Card Settlement on Blockchain Rails**](https://www.barrons.com/articles/sofi-stock-stablecoin-crypto-banks-198153fe)**.** SoFi has begun settling debit and credit card transactions over Mastercard using SoFiUSD, becoming the first US bank to apply its stablecoin to card settlement. The program is expected to process more than $25 billion annually, turning a familiar payment rail into a large-scale test of blockchain-based bank money. SoFi also plans conversions between the stablecoin and interest-bearing tokenized deposits, alongside round-the-clock international transfers.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** blockchain settlement could reduce reconciliation work, shorten funding cycles and create liquidity for merchants and payment providers. It may push banks and card networks to develop competing tokenized cash products while preserving regulated access to customer funds. If scaled, the model could lower cross-border costs and improve capital efficiency. It also concentrates operational, reserve and cyber risks inside critical payments infrastructure, making prudential oversight and interoperability essential.

[**AI Agents Could Accelerate Money Velocity: Will Machine-Speed Payments Drive GDP Growth?**](https://pdf.omfif.org/dmi-journal-october-2026)OMFIF’s October Digital Monetary Institute Journal examines how AI agents could move from recommending purchases to autonomously executing them. Agentic payments require machine-readable identity, authority, risk controls and settlement infrastructure capable of operating continuously at machine speed. Faster payments can raise the velocity of money by releasing working capital, reducing reconciliation delays and allowing the same funds to support more transactions. If these efficiency gains increase productive activity rather than simply prices, they can contribute to stronger GDP growth.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** companies could shorten cash-conversion cycles, automate procurement and reach customers through AI agents, while banks and payment providers gain new transaction volumes. However, synchronized agents could also accelerate fraud, liquidity runs and market stress. Sustainable adoption therefore depends on verified mandates, programmable risk data, interoperability, clear liability and human oversight for transactions.

[**US Bank Lawsuit Could Decide Who Controls the Institutional Digital-Asset Economy.**](https://www.reuters.com/world/community-banks-sue-us-regulator-over-crypto-firm-charters-2026-10-02/) The Independent Community Bankers of America sued the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency over national trust charters granted to crypto and fintech firms. These charters permit digital-asset custody and payment settlement but not conventional deposits or lending. Community banks argue that the regulator exceeded its authority and created uneven competition because chartered crypto companies avoid some obligations applied to full-service banks. They also warn consumers may wrongly assume federal deposit protection.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** the case could determine whether digital-asset companies gain a national route around state licensing and how quickly blockchain services enter mainstream finance. A bank victory could slow investment and preserve compliance boundaries; an OCC victory could accelerate competition, consolidation and payment innovation. Either outcome will influence regulatory costs, consumer confidence and the structure of US financial intermediation today.

[**AI and Tokenization Could Rewrite the Economics of Wealth Management**](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/podcasts/thoughts-on-the-market/tokenization-ai-wealth-management-betsy-graseck-michael-cyprys)**.** Morgan Stanley says tokenization and AI could reshape asset and wealth management by changing how products are created, distributed and serviced. Tokenized securities can support fractional ownership, faster settlement and programmable administration, while AI can personalise portfolios and automate advice. Together, the technologies may allow investment products to operate continuously and reach investors excluded by minimum sizes or geographic barriers.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** asset managers could lower distribution and servicing costs, build customised portfolios at scale and turn illiquid assets into accessible products. Banks and advisers may face margin pressure as automated platforms weaken intermediation. Broader participation could mobilise savings and improve capital allocation, supporting investment and growth. Yet fragmented ledgers, unsuitable liquidity, uncertain regulation and algorithmic mis-selling remain risks. Technology may democratise access, but governance will determine whether it also improves investor outcomes.

[**BlackRock Says AI Agents Will Turn Stablecoins Into Machine-Native Money**](https://www.blackrock.com/us/financial-professionals/insights/machine-native-economy)**.** BlackRock argues that artificial intelligence and digital assets are converging into a machine-native economy. AI converts information into tokens machines can process, while blockchains convert money, securities and economic rights into tokens machines can verify, transfer and settle. As agents begin purchasing data, computing power and services, stablecoins and protocols such as x402 could provide programmable payment rails better suited to high-volume micropayments than cards or ACH. BlackRock also identifies computing capacity as a potential tokenized asset class, enabling standardized pricing, financing and hedging. **What this means for business and the wider economy:** companies could automate procurement, monetize APIs and data, reduce settlement costs and create markets for unused computing resources. Faster machine-to-machine commerce could improve productivity and capital efficiency. However, adoption requires agent identity, spending mandates, cybersecurity, legal accountability and liquid markets capable of operating safely at machine speed.
