[**U.S. is considering using stablecoins as a surveillance tool –**](https://crypto.news/u-s-is-considering-using-stablecoins-as-a-surveillance-tool/)The U.S. Treasury Department’s move to monitor **stablecoin transactions** underscores the growing tension between financial innovation, regulation, and privacy. Following the **GENIUS Act** (July 2025), which gives regulatory and legal clarity for stablecoin issuers, the Treasury is seeking public input on methods to detect illicit activity, ranging from **AI-based blockchain analytics** to identity verification systems. This push aligns with global trends as the Bank of International Settlement recently proposed AML “compliance scores” for cryptoassets, raising concerns over creeping surveillance reminiscent of China’s **social credit system.** Supporters argue that deanonymizing transactions and introducing **zero-knowledge credentials** could balance privacy and compliance, enabling regulators to view transaction legitimacy without full exposure of user data. However, critics from the Silicon valley VC,**16z,** warn that even partial deanonymisation erodes the very premise of open, permissionless blockchains. The policy shift also highlights a paradox: earlier in 2025, President Trump banned a **U.S. CBDC** on privacy grounds, yet stablecoins, once framed as private-sector alternatives, are now under a potential surveillance regime. This raises a fundamental question: if digital IDs and compliance scores become mandatory for stablecoin use, will stablecoins truly remain private money, or will they simply evolve into a **CBDC by another name?**

[**Boerse Stuttgart Unveils Pan-European Settlement Platform for Tokenised Assets**](https://www.fstech.co.uk/fst/Boerse_stuttgart_group_launches_pan_european_platform_for_tokenised_assets.php) - Boerse Stuttgart Group has introduced Seturion, a blockchain-powered settlement platform designed to modernise post-trade processes for tokenised assets across Europe. Positioned as the first pan-European digital settlement solution, Seturion directly demonstrates Europe’s intent to lead in tokenised national infrastructures, pledging cost reductions of up to 90% while eliminating cross-border settlement frictions. Its deployment at BX Digital, Switzerland’s FINMA-regulated DLT trading venue, and participation in the European Central Bank’s 2024 blockchain trials underscore its credibility. The announcement represents both a technological leap and a bold regulatory alignment and allows institutions to participate without holding a DLT license. This lowers adoption barriers for banks, brokers, tokenisation platforms, and trading venues that want to expand into digital assets while relying on existing market infrastructure connections. Organisationally, the platform will be led by experienced digital asset executives pending BaFin licensing under the EU’s DLT Pilot Regime, ensuring regulatory alignment. If widely adopted, Seturion could accelerate Europe’s progress toward a unified digital capital market.
3. [**Future of banking with agentic AI. Written by MIT in association with EY**](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/09/04/1123023/imagining-the-future-of-banking-with-agentic-ai/)- Agentic AI is rapidly reshaping the financial services sector, ushering in opportunities for efficiency, automation, and improved decision-making far beyond what rule-based systems such as robotic process automation could deliver. Industry leaders from EY, Citi, and HSBC agree that agentic AI enables banks to automate complex workflows, process large volumes of unstructured data, and navigate ever-changing regulatory and operational landscapes, frequently with minimal human intervention. According to a major 2025 MIT Technology Review survey, nearly three-quarters of banking executives report deploying agentic AI to at least some extent, mostly for piloting use cases (52%) or active deployment (16%). However, most view current technology as not yet appropriate for full autonomy. Priority applications for agentic AI include fraud detection, customer experience enhancement, security, and driving cost efficiencies. Functions such as mortgage underwriting, loan processing, compliance monitoring, and dispute resolution are already benefiting from intelligent automation, which reduces manual workloads while allowing staff to focus on high-value tasks. AI’s strength lies in its capacity to learn and improve with feedback, delivering fewer errors, faster performance, and earlier identification of risks within dynamic data environments. Despite its promise, agentic AI adoption faces significant hurdles: governance and risk management complexities, technical skill gaps, data quality issues, and trust deficits. Executives stress the need for robust frameworks, incremental implementation, and intensive workforce reskilling. Looking forward, the technology’s transformative potential will depend not only on continued innovation, but also on responsible adoption strategies, ethical oversight, and a relentless focus on building trust. Those institutions redefining processes with agentic AI will likely outpace those resistant to change, as the sector transitions from automation to true digital autonomy

[**Binance and Franklin Templeton join forces on tokenisation ventures-**](https://www.franklintempleton.com/press-releases/news-room/2025/binance-and-franklin-templeton-to-develop-digital-assets-initiatives-and-products) The world’s biggest crypto exchange, Binance’s partnership with Franklin Templeton, with [$1.68 trillion](https://www.franklinresources.com/annual-report) undermanagement, represents a watershed in the convergence of crypto markets and traditional finance. Franklin Templeton, was one of the first issuers of a U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF, brings institutional credibility and regulatory know-how. Binance, meanwhile, offers unmatched liquidity, processing roughly $22 billion in daily trades. The collaboration is focused on the tokenisation of securities integrating Franklin Templeton’s Benji Technology Platform with Binance’s global trading infrastructure, the initiative seeks to make settlement faster, collateral management more efficient, and portfolio construction more dynamic. If successful, tokenised securities could reduce friction in capital markets by removing intermediaries, offering instant settlement, and widening investor access. This move comes as tokenisation accelerates across financial markets. Nasdaq is exploring tokenised stock listings, while Kraken has launched tokenised securities trading in Europe. The Binance–Franklin tie-up underscores a broader trend: institutional finance no longer treats blockchain as an existential threat but as infrastructure for modernisation. The question is whether tokenisation can navigate compliance, liquidity fragmentation, and investor trust to deliver on its promise of transparent, programmable, and global capital markets.

[**Mastercard Survey: 90% of SMEs That Accept Digital Payments in Puerto Rico Report Significant Business Growth**](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/paytech/mastercard-survey-90-of-smes-that-accept-digital-payments-in-puerto-rico-report-significant-business-growth/) **–** What lessons can other economies learn from this? Mastercard’s new study on Puerto Rican SMEs underscores a decisive shift: digital payments are no longer optional but existential for business survival. Of the surveyed owners and directors, **90% of SMEs already accepting digital payments credit them with significant growth**, while nearly **80% said their businesses could not function without them**. This suggests that digitalisation has transcended convenience, embedding itself as core infrastructure in the island’s economic ecosystem. Adoption is not just about sales. SMEs use digital payments to **pay suppliers (68%)**, improve **customer experience (76%),** and enhance **data analytics and compliance (75%).** In effect, payments have become an operational backbone, extending well beyond consumer-facing transactions. Non-adopters recognize the stakes: **60% report losing customers weekly** for not offering digital options, a stark signal of competitive disadvantage. Trust emerges as the central attribute in choosing providers. **86% rank trustworthiness as essential,** above provider size or brand. Customer support (83%), security (81%), and scalability (76%) rank high, reflecting SME needs for resilience, not just functionality. Importantly, SMEs also engage with secondary services such as credit facilitation (43%), highlighting how digital platforms double as financial access points. The lesson is clear: in Puerto Rico, digital payments are no longer a competitive edge but a **structural necessity**. The open question is whether providers can keep pace with SMEs’ demand for trust, security, and scale in an increasingly digital economy.

[**The Psychology of Spending: How Digital Payments Shape Our Habits**](https://www.deeside.com/the-psychology-of-spending-how-digital-payments-shape-our-habits/) **-** The way we pay is reshaping the way we think about money. With taps and swipes replacing tangible cash, spending feels increasingly abstract, and that abstraction has consequences. Behavioural research shows people spend more when the payment is frictionless because the transaction doesn’t “hurt” in the same way. Prepaid tools such as **Neosurf vouchers and repaid cards** restricts users to a fixed balance. That balance acts as a psychological guardrail, forcing a pause before every purchase. For gamers and digital natives, this offers not only privacy and flexibility but also discipline in a landscape full of impulse triggers and subscription traps. In an economy built on instant consumption, prepaid solutions aren’t old-fashioned; they’re revolutionary. They blend autonomy with accountability, reminding us that financial control doesn’t mean restriction, it means awareness. In a world of invisible money, sometimes the smartest move is setting limits.

[**Sibos 2025: How blockchain is unlocking borderless transactions**](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/sibos-2025-how-blockchain-is-unlocking-borderless-transactions) - As the global financial industry turns its focus to Sibos 2025, blockchain's role in revolutionising cross-border payments is increasingly clear. Originating from the rise of cryptocurrencies, blockchain technology now stands as a foundational innovation, enabling faster, more cost-efficient, and transparent international transactions. Its decentralised architecture eliminates multiple intermediaries and reduces traditional frictions inherent in multi-currency, multi-jurisdictional transfers. Industry leaders emphasise blockchain’s capacity to reduce transaction costs dramatically, often bringing fees down to mere pennies via peer-to-peer transfers on shared ledgers. This not only accelerates settlement times, from days to minutes, but also ensures 24/7 availability, a crucial factor for global commerce. Stablecoins further enhance this system by addressing volatility concerns, while smart contracts automate payment triggers, integrating compliance and operational efficiencies. Beyond efficiency, blockchain fosters greater financial inclusion by democratising access to global markets, especially for underbanked populations and gig economy workers who traditionally face prohibitive remittance costs. Europe’s digitally native workforce, for instance, stands to gain significantly from reduced fees and faster payouts, narrowing the inclusion gap. However, adoption hurdles remain prominent. Regulatory ambiguity across jurisdictions, interoperability issues between legacy and new systems, and scalability constraints slow widespread implementation. Collaborative efforts among financial institutions, FinTechs, and regulators are critical to harmonising standards and building trust. Real-world innovations, such as Standard Charterer’s blockchain settlements between Hong Kong and Singapore, showcase promising progress.
