[**AI to ‘unlock $370billion a year in additional bank profits**](https://www.thebanker.com/content/99e80fd3-6c6c-4250-835b-950a591cb3b3?xnpe_tifc=4.euOkP_xIblh.LsbuHjOypsafeWaeiWhFW_huULRkUcqoB9afeWaG8.adJSOFbjbuPN4uPNbIVXbuxd4zTT&utm_source=exponea&utm_campaign=BKR%20-%20Wednesday%20-%20Newsletter%20-%2005.11.25&utm_medium=email)AI could unlock a $370 billion annual profit windfall for retail banks, according to Boston Consulting Group (BCG), which estimates large-scale deployment could cut costs by up to 40%. Yet BCG warns that AI’s potential extends far beyond efficiency. Banks that fully integrate “agentic” AI embedding it into decision-making, customer engagement and workflow redesign will redefine banks economics. Only about 5%of institutions are expected to achieve true AI maturity, but those that do could shift the industry’s competitive balance, rendering slower rivals “increasingly irrelevant”. BCG identifies three stages of AI evolution, **deploy, reshape and invent**, noting that genuine transformation arises only when banks scale AI across end-to-end processes and reinvent business models. “*Too many banks are stuck in deploy mode*,” says Bharat Poddar, BCG senior partner. “Automation isn’t transformation.” Singapore’s DBS offers an early glimpse of the disruption: it plans to cut 10%of its workforce as AI efficiencies bite. As margins compress and fintech challengers accelerate, the report argues, AI-first banking may determine not just profitability, but survival.
2. [**A new tool to help investors? Google to integrate Kalshi and Polymarket predictions into its finance AI tools**](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/google-kalshi-polymarket-prediction-markets-rcna242670)**.** Google’s decision to integrate **Kalshi** and **Polymarket** data into Google Finance marks a turning point: prediction markets, long confined to the crypto fringe, are entering the financial mainstream. By displaying “event contract” odds directly in search results, from GDP forecasts to elections — Google effectively legitimises crowd-sourced probability as a new class of financial signal. Platforms such as Polymarket run on blockchain infrastructure, meaning that the same rails used for digital asset trading could soon underpin data feeds for investors, economists, and AI models. Tokenised prediction markets transform human sentiment into measurable liquidity, a step toward “information as an asset.” As decentralised finance (DeFi) matures under regulatory frameworks like MiCA, these platforms could blur the lines between capital markets and crowd intelligence. Yet this visibility invites scrutiny. Regulators still see a gambling veneer; critics fear manipulation or misinformation loops, especially in politics and markets. But as institutional capital and Big Tech converge, the question is no longer if prediction markets will shape finance, but how fast. In a world where every forecast can be tokenised, crypto’s next frontier may not be speculation, it’s the monetisation of collective foresight and threat to some security analysts.

[**Stablecoins, effective bridge between fiat and crypto, or a bridge too far?**](https://www.lseg.com/en/ftse-russell/research/stablecoins-effective-bridge-between-fiat-and-crypto-or-a-bridge-too-far) The FTSE Russell report provides an in-depth analysis of stablecoins, often described as the missing link between fiat money and crypto assets arguing they are more than mere bridge currency; they are emerging as **quasi-money instruments** with, distinct collateral structures and important implications for reserve currency dynamics. Crucially, the report links stablecoin growth to macro-monetary expansion: when US M2 rises, so does stablecoin issuance, suggesting they operate as on-ramp liquidity vehicles in broader money supply cycles. The report highlights different types of stablecoins, including fiat-collateralised, crypto-backed, and algorithmic models, noting their varying volatility and risk profiles. Regulatory developments, such as the US GENIUS Act and EU MiCA framework, aim to bolster market stability. The report also examines stablecoins’ potential impact on financial stability and the US dollar’s dominance as a global reserve currency, underscoring ongoing challenges around reserve asset quality, arbitrage mechanisms, and regulatory divergence. Overall, stablecoins stand as a pivotal innovation bridging traditional finance but require careful governance to mitigate risks and maintain trust. Adopting them as monetary equivalents requires legal clarity, reserve proofing and stress-testing conventions to mature.

[**Bank of England November systemic stablecoin consultation**](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/november/boe-launches-consultation-on-regulating-systemic-stablecoins) The Bank of England’s (B of E) consultation on regulating systemic sterling stablecoins marks a pivotal moment for UK payments in particular for wholesale financial markets. By proposing robust backing assets, (60% gilts 40% bank deposits held by the B of E, transitional liquidity support, and strict holding limits, the Bank aims to mitigate risks to monetary stability amid rapid digital money evolution. Although it is unclear how one is able to practically limit the holding of a stablecoin as questions how popular it would be to even try restricting their use. Crucially, the framework illustrates regulatory nimbleness: joint oversight with the FCA and B of E, phased asset caps, and analysis of deposit outflows all signal a future of adaptive rule making. The FCA and B of E currently have [joint oversight to regulated financial market infrastructures](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability/financial-market-infrastructure-supervision/what-do-we-do/the-bank-of-englands-approach-to-financial-market-infrastructure-supervision#:~:text=FMI%20supervisors%20co%2Dordinate%20with,%2Dthe%2Dcounter%20financial%20markets.) (FMIs) such as central counterparties (CCPs), recognised investment exchanges (RIEs), and recognised central securities depositories (RCSDs). Using a MoU these two regulators aim to avoid duplicating effort while providing effective regulatory monitoring. This regime not only seeks to anchor public trust but also tests Britain’s ability to harmonise innovation and safety, raising the question of whether digital money’s promise can truly be reconciled with the realities of financial system resilience.

[**Blockchains’ role in taming Agentic AI**](https://coingeek.com/blockchain-crucial-role-in-taming-agentic-ai/)Artificial intelligence may soon power an agent/ bot to act as our financial planner, personal assistant, or even autonomous business manager, but its growing independence exposes a deeper problem: trust. As [agentic AI](https://www.oracle.com/uk/artificial-intelligence/agentic-ai/) systems evolve beyond chatbots to perform real-world tasks, they demand mechanisms for accountability and verification. That’s where blockchain technology can potentially help. “*Blockchain will be critical to every AI model moving forward*,” said FICO’s Chief Analytics Officer, Dr Scott Zoldi, at the London Blockchain Conference 2025. His firm already uses blockchain to audit AI model development, embedding transparency and ethical oversight into code itself. Proponents argue immutable ledgers can document AI decisions, track data provenance, and prevent “black box” opacity. Yet adoption remains slow, few developers have integrated on-chain safeguards into AI pipelines. The challenge is not technological but cultural: merging the decentralised ethos of blockchain with AI’s drive toward autonomy. As autonomous agents begin managing assets and making decisions in real time, the question is no longer whether they can act, but whether we can prove why they did. Blockchain may become AI’s conscience: a system of record ensuring that intelligent machines, however independent, remain accountable to human-defined truth.

[**JP Morgan, USDC and Coinbase blockchain, Base, to enable bank to trade deposit tokens**](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/j-p-morgans-jpm-coin-deposit-token-goes-live-on-public-blockchain-base/)J.P. Morgan’s USD deposit token, JPM Coin (JPMD), on Coinbase’s Base Ethereum Layer 2 public blockchain, ushers in a new era for institutional digital payments. For institutional clients, the move promises near-instant, low-cost, 24/7 settlement, a significant step forward from legacy correspondent banking. Integration with major partners such as Coinbase, B2C2, and Mastercard broadens the use case for bank-issued, blockchain-native money, improving liquidity and automating complex financial workflows via smart contracts.​ On the other hand, the shift to public blockchains raises key questions around security, privacy, and compliance, areas where banking and blockchain cultures sometimes diverge. The convergence of regulated bank deposits and open networks tests interoperability, scalability, and the industry’s ability to uphold regulatory standards under real-time settlement pressures. Ultimately, the JPM Coin initiative highlights both the transformative potential and the operational complexities of digital money in mainstream finance. Success will hinge on whether such projects can harness the efficiencies and programmability of blockchain while satisfying institutional risk, legal, and compliance demands, potentially redefining the boundaries between traditional finance and the public digital asset ecosystem
