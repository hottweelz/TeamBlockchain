**Weekly articles 10th October 2025**

1. **[Tokenised stock market dominated by just platforms](https://crypto.news/tokenized-stock-market-dominated-by-only-two-players/)** The market for tokenised stocks is expanding at an extraordinary pace, yet its growth is remarkably concentrated. Animoca Brands Research reports that as of September, just two issuers, Backed and Ondo Global Markets command a 95% market share of on-chain tokenised stock market. Together they represent nearly the entirety of a sector now valued at $127 million (or $342 million including EXOD shares), a fourteen-fold increase since the start of the year. Tokenised stocks replicate the economic exposure of traditional equities enabling trading 24/7 and appeals to crypto-native investors seeking access to equities such as Apple, Tesla, and ETFs without the friction of conventional intermediaries. However, most current offerings, are synthetic in nature, tracking stock prices without conferring underlying ownership rights. Although, experimental models such as Superstate Opening Bell’s tokenised Galaxy Digital shares aim for true ownership, potentially bridging blockchain infrastructure with the protections of traditional securities law. Concentration raises uncomfortable questions. Is the dominance of two platforms a sign of efficient first-mover advantage, or does it risk replicating centralisation in a space meant to democratise finance? Equally, the emergence of tokenised pre-IPO shares on Solana through PreStocksFi suggests another frontier, where retail traders gain exposure to illiquid private firms like SpaceX or OpenAI, but without the clear investor safeguards of public markets. But at least PreStocksFi is offering a way for smaller investors to gain access to IPOs The promise of tokenised equities is undeniable: greater access, efficiency, and innovation. But their legitimacy hinges on whether legal frameworks, investor protections, and genuine decentralisation can keep pace with rapid adoption.

[**Visa Direct will prefund payouts with stablecoins**](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250930875370/en/Visa-Direct-Taps-Stablecoins-to-Unlock-Faster-Funding-for-Businesses?utm_source=substack&utm_medium=email) Visa’s decision to allow businesses to pre-fund accounts with stablecoins such as USDC and EURC is more than a technical upgrade, it is a structural shift in how global liquidity may be managed. By treating tokenised $ and £ as equivalent to “money in the bank,” Visa converts idle pre-funded capital into liquid assets enables cross border payments in minutes rather than days. The efficiency gains are obvious: instant settlement, reduced reliance on legacy correspondent banking, and fewer intermediaries clipping fees from every transfer. Yet beneath the headline of faster payouts lies a deeper signal: Visa is normalising stablecoins as functional deposits, collapsing the long-standing distinction between balances held in commercial banks and balances recorded on blockchains. IN EFFECT TREATING STABLECOINS AS CASH This convergence has profound consequences. For multinational corporates, it offers operational agility and cost savings. But for regional and mid-sized banks, the threat is existential. Stablecoin rails strip them of both liquidity and lucrative fee income derived from slow-moving cross-border transactions. If Visa’s model scales, could it accelerate the migration of financial flows onto blockchain-based settlement layers, bypassing the traditional banking hierarchy. The real story, then, is not the pilot itself but what it represents: the redefinition of “money in the bank” in an era where digital tokens can rival deposits for speed, trust, and utility.

[**Will UK’s major banks tokenised deposits snuff out £ stablecoins**](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/tokenised-sterling-deposits#:~:text=What%20is%20the%20GBTD%20project,traditional%20sterling%20commercial%20bank%20money.) **–** Barclays, Lloyds, NatWest, Santander and Nationwide have joined a project to create Great British Tokenised Deposits (GBTD) to enable transactions of tokenised sterling deposits, bridging the gap between traditional commercial bank money and programmable digital payments. Unlike unregulated stablecoins, these deposits are fully backed by sterling in regulated UK banks, maintaining the same trust and legal protections while unlocking new efficiencies. The project builds on the UK’s Regulated Liability Network (RLN), extending its work into real-world use cases such as fraud-resistant person-to-person payments, instant remortgage settlement, and digital asset exchanges. Crucially, the pilot signals a shift from theory to implementation, showcasing how programmable, tokenised deposits could underpin a multi-money ecosystem that coexists with central bank money, stablecoins, and tokenised assets. This experiment is more than a technical test, it’s a live case study in how tokenisation could redefine monetary infrastructure, recasting banks as the custodians of programmable value and laying the groundwork for a digitally native sterling economy.

[**Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), vests $2billion in a Polymarket, a DeFi prediction platform why?**](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/nyse-owner-dives-into-prediction-markets-with-2-billion-polymarket-investment-db34302b?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Making investment decisions is all about market knowledge, information and data. In 2024 2024 Polymarket saw an100x increase in volume and now offers investors insight as to what hundreds or thousands of people think as opposed to a few analysts. ICE, parent of the New York Stock Exchange, is to invest up to $2 billion in Polymarket, valuing it at $8 billion, is a watershed moment for prediction markets and tokenised finance. At a strategic level, the investment signals that Wall Street is hedging on the mainstreaming of event-driven markets. By becoming a global distributor of Polymarket’s “event-driven data,” ICE gains direct access to a new layer of market intelligence, sentiment-based signals that could complement traditional price data. But the move is not risk-free. From a competitive perspective, ICE’s investment could pressure exchanges, data providers, and tokenisation platforms to adopt or integrate prediction-based products. The move underscores that data about events themselves, not just financial instruments, is becoming a tradable asset. If successful, the deal could mark the moment prediction markets transition from fringe crypto experiments into regulated pillars of the institutional data ecosystem. But whether regulators, institutions, and users will embrace that future fear its implications remains to be seen.

[**McKenzie 2025 global payments report**](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-payments-report?cid=cl4c-cml&sid=CL4&mkt_tok=NDczLVpZRS01MjIAAAGdYfJR8uYyJWyO4WulFBzkmhs-mBbfTqU1Tq8VmZU8xq_cctD5v3g1ZSYW2f6MyCdNSPrNvcdcrSh_owM68F9VI56YtiPN5o-tz4uG2pKKSpmBSIP2Bg) McKinsey’s report argues we’ve crossed a threshold: payments are no longer about mere efficiency gains but about who shapes the rails of value. Once viewed as a back-office utility, payments now offer huge revenues and can smash the once cosy bank monopiles. McKinsey identifies three tectonic shifts driving disruption:1) fragmented rails, 2) digital assets, and 3) AI-native commerce. Globally payments generate over $2 trillion of annual revenue moving $2 quadrillion around the world and remains the largest engine in financial services. Hence the explosion in the number of FinTech firms globally looking to grab a slice of the action. Banking margins are under pressure as interest income softens, fee arbitrage escalates, and alternative payment rails vie for dominance. In this new landscape, winners will not be fastest but most trusted and agile. Platforms that bind payments, value, credentials and AI agents into a coherent stack hold a decisive edge. McKinsey warns that players locked into legacy models will find themselves marginalised or simply replicated by more nimble challengers. The shifting foundations of money tokenised deposits, stablecoins, CBDCs are transforming rails into programmable layers. But innovation alone isn’t enough; architecture, governance, and trust will define who leads. In the decade ahead, how money moves may prove more consequential than how much moves. Indeed, will we look back and say this was the Kodak moment for many banks?
7. [**Walmart to offer crypto to its 150 million weekly shoppers. Are digital wallets set to replace bank accounts?**](https://www.statista.com/statistics/818929/number-of-weekly-customer-visits-to-walmart-stores-worldwide/#:~:text=Walmart:%20weekly%20customer%20visits%20to,weekly%20average%20a%20year%20earlier.)Walmart-backed OnePay is taking a decisive step into digital assets, positioning itself at the intersection of retail, payments, and crypto finance. According to CNBC, the FinTech soon enable Bitcoin and Ether trading and custody, signalling that crypto is not an afterthought but a core layer of its financial ecosystem. The move transforms OnePay’s “everything app” ambition—already spanning savings, lending, cards, and telecom, into a gateway for digital money management. With over 150 million weekly Walmart shoppers and app rankings surpassing JPMorgan, Chime, and Robinhood, OnePay has the potential to mainstream crypto through scale rather than hype. This development also reflects a broader policy and market realignment. If executed effectively, OnePay could become America’s first mass-market “super app,” seamlessly linking spending, saving, and digital assets under a regulated, consumer-trusted brand. Yet, it also raises strategic questions: will Walmart’s financial reach blur the lines between retail commerce and monetary sovereignty? And can FinTechs democratise crypto access without replicating the systemic risks of traditional banking?
