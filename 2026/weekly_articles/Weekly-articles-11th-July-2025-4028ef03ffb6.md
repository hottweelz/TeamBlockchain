**Weekly articles 11th July 2025**

**1.** [**Seems like the Chinese don’t want US$ backed payments too**](https://www.tradingview.com/news/coinpedia%3A58e921058094b%3A0-china-s-big-tech-wants-yuan-stablecoin-to-break-usdt-s-lead/)**.** As US$ stablecoins cement their dominance in global digital trade, China’s tech giants JD.com and Ant Group are pressing for a yuan-based stablecoin to be launched in Hong Kong. This move underscores Beijing’s growing anxiety over the yuan’s slipping share in international payments and the unstoppable rise of USDT and USDC as default currencies for cross-border programmable digital money methods of settlements. While China has championed its e-CNY for domestic use, its strict crypto bans and capital controls have hamstrung yuan internationalisation efforts. Now the stakes are higher. Hong Kong’s progressive crypto regulations present a potential launchpad for a yuan stablecoin that could align with China’s Belt and Road ambitions and offer an alternative to American centric financial rails. But time is not on Beijing’s side. The U.S. has surged ahead with regulatory clarity under the GENIUS Act, fuelling an ecosystem where private dollar stablecoins are already reshaping global money flows. Unless China rethinks its digital asset strategy, its dream of a “digital yuan zone” risks being eclipsed by an American-led stablecoin order.

**2.** [**The future of money is undoubtedly digital, but as recently as the last decade, the road to cashless was expected to run through existing banking rails.**](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/state-of-play-stablecoins)The future of money is programmable, decentralised, and breaking out of the traditional banking rails faster than anyone imagined. A decade ago, the road to a cashless world was expected to run through Visa, SWIFT, and the central banks. Today, it’s stablecoins not banks that are winning the race. Stablecoins are everywhere. Stripe’s $1.1 billion acquisition of Bridge this February made Wall Street sit up. This wasn’t just another FinTech play it was a strategic acquisition based on the belief stablecoins will be the backbone of global payments. Stripe, once a Bitcoin sceptic, now sees stablecoins as the key to a faster, cheaper, and more inclusive financial system. They aren’t alone. Circle is racing to secure a national trust bank charter in the USA. Visa is building tokenised asset networks to make crypto and fiat interoperable. Fiserv’s new FIUSD stablecoin is primed to flow through existing banking channels. Suddenly, stablecoins aren’t the sideshow they’re the main event.

But here’s the uncomfortable truth: most people still underestimate what’s coming. Stablecoins aren’t just digital cash they’re a new monetary operating system. They don’t just move dollars; they rewrite the logic of money itself. Imagine a world where cross-border payments settle instantly for less than a cent, where businesses run on 24/7 programmable cash flows, where even the poorest countries have access to global capital without begging for correspondent banking relationships. That’s the stablecoin future.

But there’s a catch. For now, stablecoins handle $15.6 trillion annually tiny next to SWIFT’s $5.3 trillion daily. Citi predicts stablecoins could hit $1.6 trillion in supply by 2030, but the real battle isn’t supply its trust. Can they avoid being co-opted by the same banks they were meant to disrupt? And most importantly: who will own this future? Will it be private issuers like Circle and Stripe, or central banks racing to catch up with CBDCs?

[*“Digital currencies shouldn’t just be about rebuilding our economy; it should be about creating those economies which don’t exist yet.”*](https://www.fintechfutures.com/blockchain-crypto-digital-assets/state-of-play-stablecoins)

This isn’t just about faster payments. It’s about sovereignty, power, and who writes the rules for the global economy. Stablecoins could democratise money or they could just become sleek, digital leashes controlled by the same old gatekeepers.

One thing is clear: we’re past the point of no return. The question now isn’t if stablecoins will reshape money. It’s whether they’ll do it in the image of crypto’s original promise—or in the image of the institutions they’re quietly replacing.

**3**[**. Robinhood faces regulators over tokenised stock offering-**](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/robinhood-faces-regulatory-scrutiny-over-tokenized-private-company-stock/)Robinhood’s push into tokenised stocks for European retail investors feels like a watershed moment but also a high-wire act. On one hand, it promises to break down decades-old barriers, giving everyday investors access to U.S. equities and even private giants like OpenAI and SpaceX. On the other, it raises questions as these tokenised shares aren’t real shares but derivative wrappers, offering no voting rights, no dividends, and little regulatory protection. OpenAI’s immediate stance to distance itself highlights a deeper tension when innovation runs ahead of governance, who protects the investor? Robinhood argues tokenisation solves liquidity and accessibility issues for private markets, but critics see a dangerous sleight of hand an asset masquerading as ownership. Regulators in Europe are now asking tough questions, and their response may determine whether this is the dawn of a new financial frontier or not.

At its core, Robinhood’s move challenges us to ask: is democratizing access enough if the product being offered isn’t what users think it is? Or are we watching the early stages of a system where financial rights are abstracted away entirely in favour of raw exposure and liquidity? The answer could redefine what “ownership” means in the digital age. No doubt others such as E-Toro and Kraken will be looking closely to see how this unravels.

[**4. Ex ECB board member urges EU to embrace stablecoins**](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/socgen-chair-ex-ecb-board-member-urges-eu-to-embrace-stablecoins/) **-** Lorenzo Bini Smaghi, Chairman of Société Générale and former ECB board member, plea is a wake-up call for Europe: stablecoins aren’t a threat to monetary sovereignty they may be the only way to preserve it in a rapidly digitising global economy. As U.S. dollar-backed stablecoins surge and Asia experiments boldly with tokenisation, Europe lags, hamstrung by cultural conservatism and a banking sector unwilling to invest in the future. Smaghi frames stablecoins not as “unsound money,” as the BIS recently claimed, but as a revolutionary infrastructure for instant, borderless, low-cost transactions something the Eurozone, with its single market, should be leading, not fearing. His warning is clear: if European regulators and bankers don’t embrace innovation, they’ll cede the field to American and Asian players, ironically undermining the very sovereignty they seek to protect.

Yet his advocacy is also self-interested. Société Générale, which he chairs, is one of the few European banks with a live € stablecoin (albeit at a tiny scale). This raises a provocative question: is Europe’s hesitation about prudence or about protecting incumbents who fear disruption? And more critically, can the euro survive as a global currency if it misses the next wave of financial infrastructure?

[**6.Virtual card adoption expands penetration in the UK.**](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/sap-taulia-virtual-card-research)**-** According to SAP virtual payment cards are quietly becoming one of the biggest shifts in corporate finance, redefining how global businesses manage supplier payments and working capital. Over 90% of procurement leaders are either using, planning to adopt, or actively exploring virtual cards. What’s driving the surge? Agility and control. Virtual cards allow companies to onboard suppliers faster, reduce friction in payments, and tighten risk management areas where traditional systems often choke growth. Europe appears to be leading the charge, with firms allocating significant spend to these tools, while US counterparts move more cautiously.

But there’s a deeper undercurrent here. As procurement functions embrace digital transformation, virtual cards represent more than convenience they’re an early glimpse into a future where finance is real-time, decentralised, and programmable. For early adopters, the payoff could be significant: leaner operations, smarter cash flow, and competitive advantage. For laggards, the risk is being stuck in legacy systems as the next wave of financial innovation passes them by.

[**7. Swift and ISO 20022: The impact on payments**](https://www.fxcintel.com/research/reports/swift-iso-20022-impact-payments-explained) **–** The global Cross border market for payments is expected to grow from $194 trillion in 2024 to over $317trillion by 2032. Arguablythe financial plumbing of global payments is undergoing a seismic shift as Swift’s migration to ISO 20022 gathers pace. Promising richer data, transparency, and smoother cross-border transactions, the new standard could redefine how trillions move across borders. Yet, behind the headline of progress lies a messier reality. Many banks are still grappling with legacy systems, creating a patchwork where half of global messages remain stuck in the old Message Type (MT) format. This hybrid state threatens to blunt the very benefits ISO 20022 promises more structured data, faster settlements, and fewer compliance bottlenecks.

So, will this then enable a new era of programmable, AI-driven finance? But sceptics warn that uneven adoption, data truncation and costly tech overhauls could mean years of disruption before the system delivers on its potential. The stakes are high: in a world where real-time payments, sanctions compliance, and financial crime prevention are non-negotiable, ISO 20022 may be less about innovation and more about survival. Will the global banking industry rise to the challenge or be slow to innovate and leave the door open for FinTechs to continue capturing market share in a more open and fragmented payments landscape?
