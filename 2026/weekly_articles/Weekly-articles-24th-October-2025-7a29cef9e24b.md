[**IMF Warns on Financial Stability Risks From Stablecoin Market**](https://archive.ph/lJ5iE#selection-1147.0-1147.61)The International Monetary Fund’s latest warning positions the $305 billion stablecoin market as a potential catalyst for financial disruption. Once hailed as the most stable corner of crypto, these fiat-backed tokens now sit at the crossroads of traditional and digital finance. The IMF cautions that a run on major stablecoins could spark fire sales of Treasuries and bank deposits, jolting the very markets they rely on for backing. The risk extends further: widespread adoption could dilute monetary sovereignty, undermining central banks’ control over liquidity and interest rates while accelerating "digital dollarization" in fragile economies. Yield-bearing stablecoins also threaten to drain bank deposits, eroding lending capacity and deepening systemic fragilities. Yet paradoxically, the same institutional embrace driving stablecoin maturity, such as Goldman Sachs, Santander, and Citi exploring reserve-backed digital currencies, heightens these interconnections. As the line between money-market funds and blockchain tokens blurs, stablecoins exemplify a deeper dilemma: innovation that enhances efficiency while amplifying systemic risk. The IMF’s warning is less an obituary for stablecoins than a challenge to regulators, develop frameworks that harness their utility without allowing them to destabilize the architecture of global finance.

[**Ripple pay $1billion to but US based corporate treasury business**](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46775/ripple-targets-corporate-treasury-with-1bn-gtreasury-acquisition?ct=t(email_campaign_10_18_2025_7_21)&mc_cid=afa65e9e21&mc_eid=562a6d0508) - Ripple’s acquisition of Chicago-based GTreasury marks a pivotal moment in the convergence of blockchain and corporate finance. GTreasury’s has over 1,000 clients across 160 countries now offers Ripple direct access to the multi-trillion-dollar corporate treasury market, a space long dominated by legacy siloed systems. The move signals Ripple’s ambition to evolve from cross-border remittances into a **full-stack liquidity network for global enterprises,** enabling CFOs to manage **stablecoins, tokenised deposits, and real-time payments** alongside traditional cash. As corporate treasurers confront fragmented banking rails, Ripple’s integration could unlock **idle capital,** automate liquidity workflows, and deliver the kind of **instant, programmable settlement** that blockchain has promised for years but rarely achieved at scale. CEO **Brad Garlinghouse** hails the deal as an inflection point where blockchain stops being a niche solution and becomes a **strategic backbone** for enterprise finance. Combined with Ripple’s recent acquisitions of **Hidden Road** (digital prime brokerage) and **Rail** (stablecoin infrastructure), GTreasury completes a triangular foundation for **institutional-grade digital money management.** If successful, Ripple won’t just improve treasury operations, it could redefine how **corporates perceive money itself,** transforming liquidity from a static balance sheet entry into a **dynamic, tokenised asset** that moves as fast as information.

[**Visa claims stablecoins are powering new lending opportunities**](https://www.pymnts.com/blockchain/2025/visa-says-stablecoins-are-powering-a-new-lending-space/) – Visa claims stablecoins are no longer just instruments of payment but they are forming the backbone of emerging global credit markets. On‑chain lending powered by stablecoins has generated $670 billion in loans over five years, with $51.7 billion recorded in August 2025 alone, smart contracts now automate rate setting, collateral management, and settlement, eliminating many of the frictions that make bank lending slow and costly. For incumbent banks, this shift could erode their competitive edge, credit creation, once the preserve of balance‑sheet lenders, is migrating to decentralised, programmable markets where liquidity moves in real time and costs fall sharply. Yet for smaller investors and entrepreneurs, this transformation is empowering. Tokenised assets and stablecoin liquidity pools allow individuals to lend or borrow in fractional amounts, creating a peer‑to‑peer credit ecosystem once reserved for institutions. Visa’s move to build the infrastructure rather than compete in lending signals that the rails of finance are being rebuilt for inclusivity and constant access. In this new model, the beneficiaries may not be banks at all, but a growing class of digital lenders and retail participants who can finally compete in global credit markets on equal terms. This mirrors Visa’s historic role in card networks, but with automation and blockchain replacing intermediaries. The fusion of stablecoins and smart contracts could open new forms of credit for both corporates and underbanked populations. Yet, as decentralised markets scale, questions of governance, interoperability, and systemic risk remain. Visa’s move signals the next frontier one where programmable finance blurs the line between DeFi and TradFi, redefining how credit is created, secured, and transmitted across the global economy.​

[**24/7 Markets: How DLT Is Erasing Borders in Global Finance**](https://fintechnews.ch/blockchain_bitcoin/dlt-adoption-accelerates-in-capital-markets-with-growing-focus-on-digital-assets-public-blockchains/78676/?ct=t(EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_10_18_2025_7_21)&mc_cid=afa65e9e21&mc_eid=562a6d0508) Capital markets are embracing blockchain-powered solutions and as tokenised assets rise 300% from $2.1billion in Sept 2024 to over $8.4billion in 2025. Institutions are shifting from pilot projects to real deployment, redefining global capital markets through automation, liquidity, and tokenisation. According to the International Securities Services Association, 36% of market participants now operate live blockchain-powered solutions, up from just 4% in 2020. North America leads adoption, driven by regulatory clarity from the U.S. GENIUS Act, while Europe’s growth has slowed. Investment momentum is equally striking: Blockchain technology funding has tripled since 2020, with North America outspending Europe and Asia-Pacific combined. The focus is shifting from cost-cutting to new product revenue, asset mobility, and collateral efficiency, as digital cash and tokenised assets improve liquidity. Intraday liquidity is being seen as a major driver. Public blockchains now dominate new projects, replacing siloed private networks. Meanwhile, buy-side participation has surged, with one in four investors live on-chain and initiatives like BlackRock’s tokenized BUIDL fund exemplifying real-world utility. Blockchain-powered solutions and assets are being seen as an evolution from cost-efficiency tool to liquidity enabler signals a full digital reboot of capital markets, where asset tokenisation, real-time settlement, and 24/7 accessibility are setting new standards for transparency, efficiency, and investor inclusivity.

[**Central Bank Money Goes Decentralised: The Bank of England’s DLT Moment**](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/speech/2025/october/victoria-cleland-opening-remarks-at-the-boe-bis-dlt-innovation-challenge-showcase)**.** The Bank of England’s DLT Innovation Challenge marks a subtle but significant shift in central banking: exploring how trust in money can be re-engineered across decentralised infrastructures. In partnership with the BIS Innovation Hub, the initiative invites firms to test whether wholesale central bank money can safely move and settle on public blockchains beyond direct official control. This experiment is more than technical curiosity; it’s a policymaking exercise in reconciling institutional oversight with open innovation. By launching its Synchronisation Lab, the Bank is signalling readiness to experiment with atomic settlement across blockchains, bridging RTGS with tokenised assets and programmable payments. Victoria Cleland said in her speech: “*Looking ahead, our vision is for a multi-money ecosystem, where different forms of money— including central bank money, commercial bank money, tokenised deposits, stablecoins—can coexist and interoperate*”. This approach positions the UK at the frontier of hybrid finance, where regulated money can interact with decentralised networks without sacrificing resilience or monetary integrity. If successful, synchronised settlement could act as a foundation for future financial architectures that balance innovation with systemic stability. The challenge’s outcomes will inform the Bank’s ongoing exploration of wholesale digital currency, interoperability standards, and liquidity management, shaping how “central bank money” evolves in a programmable, multi-ledger world.

[**Commercial Real Estate embraces blockchain**](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/21/commercial-real-estate-embracing-blockchain.html)**.** Commercial real estate according to Savills is valued at [$50.8 trillion compared to residential properties at $287.6 trillion](https://impacts.savills.com/market-trends/the-total-value-of-global-real-estate-property-remains-the-worlds-biggest-store-of-wealth.html). Deloitte estimates: “Roughly $4 trillion of real estate will be tokenized by 2035, increasing from less than $300 billion in 2024”. A decade ago, stories of “Bitcoin home sales” mostly involved buyers cashing out digital coins into dollars. Now, platforms such as Propy are taking it further, allowing crypto holders to use Bitcoin and Ether as **collateral** for residential and commercial loans without selling their assets. This lets investors retain upside potential while accessing liquidity for property purchases. Yet the more transformative change lies not in crypto itself but in the **blockchain infrastructure** underpinning it. As **Deloitte** reports, blockchain-based **smart contracts** could streamline every layer of commercial real estate (CRE), from title transfers and leasing to financing and management, potentially halving transaction costs and enabling data-driven city services such as energy billing or smart parking. Blockchain-powered lending according to Figure can accelerates real estate loans to [10 days from industry average 42 days](https://www.ainvest.com/news/blockchain-breakthrough-era-mortgage-lending-public-2509/), property finance could soon become dynamic and transferable, a system where mortgage bonds, interest rates, and ownership records all exist securely, transparently, and perpetually on-chain.

[**A Blockchain and AI powered platform to manage assets**](https://www.reuters.com/press-releases/bluwhale-10m-blockchain-ai-wealth-shift-2025-10-22/) **-** Larry Fink the CEO of BlackRock has been talking about mass customisation of personalised wealth management for a while. As reported by Reuters: *“Over the next 30 years, an estimated $84 trillion wealth transfer from Baby Boomers to Millennials and Gen Z will take place. The number of licensed financial advisors is shrinking, and physical-branch models cannot profitably scale to meet demand”.*  **Enter** Bluwhale who have just raised $10 million offering what could be a glimpse into the future where we see the convergence of artificial intelligence and blockchain finance. Positioned as “Web3’s intelligence layer,” the firm aims to merge traditional banking data with on‑chain assets, creating a unified financial health ecosystem for both institutions and individuals. Its Layer 3 infrastructure orchestrates data, storage, and computing across blockchains, powering thousands of decentralized AI agents that recommend, transact, and score user financial behaviour through its signature “WhaleScore.” With 3.6 million users and onboarding [300,000 a month](https://www.edgen.tech/news/crypto/bluwhale-launches-ai-driven-whale-score-merging-traditional-and-on-chain-financial-data-for-gen-z) new customers Bluwhale has secured backing from UOB Venture Management, SBI Holdings, and Amazon’s head of AI. Bluwhale is betting on a future where wealth management becomes algorithmic, programmable, and personalised. The launch of its native token $BLUAI cements this vision, enabling governance, staking, and payments for queries across decentralized systems. The company’s pitch is audacious: replace opaque financial intermediation with intelligent, user‑owned analytics that span fiat and crypto worlds. In a digital economy redefined by data, Bluwhale’s ambition resembles an AI‑fuelled Bloomberg Terminal for the blockchain era—one that could democratise financial decision‑making or, paradoxically, centralise it under a new algorithmic elite. Either way, the race to embed intelligence into capital markets has begun, and Bluwhale wants to build its neural spine.
