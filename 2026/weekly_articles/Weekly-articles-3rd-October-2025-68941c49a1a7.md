**Weekly articles 3rd October 2025**

1. **[The biggest asset class in the world, derivatives, to be enabled the use of tokenised collateral, including stablecoins.](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9130-25) -** This move recognises that tokenisation, particularly of high-quality digital assets such as regulated stablecoins, can transform the $400 trillion global derivatives by reducing operational frictions, eliminating settlement lags, and unlocking 24/7 liquidity. The program is being launched through a three-year pilot with clearinghouses and futures commission merchants, focusing initially on stablecoins without and underpinned by robust risk and custody safeguards. If successful, the CFTC’s project could serve as the blueprint for tokenised financial infrastructure worldwide The CFTC expects to boost capital efficiency and lower transaction costs, potentially by up to 30%. This opens doors for both incumbents and new entrants: for institutional players, tokenised collateral offers more agile margining and global access to liquidity; for stablecoin issuers, it cements their use case in mainstream, regulated finance. However, critical challenges persist, valuation, custody, segregation, and especially cross-border compatibility must be solved to ensure systemic stability rather than risk amplification. More profoundly, this U.S. “crypto sprint” signals to global regulators that the future of market infrastructure is digital and that public-private innovation, rather than reactive restriction, will define the next wave of financial plumbing. If successful, the CFTC’s project could shift the financial markets to embrace, rather than fear, responsible digital asset adoption.
2. **[Citi Group stablecoins 20230 Web3.0 to Wall Street](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/GPS_Report_Stablecoins_2030.pdf)** - The 2025 Citi GPS report “Stablecoins 2030” reveals explosive growth in stablecoin issuance, with supply reaching $280 billion in September 2025, up from $200 billion at the year's start. Forecasts predict stablecoin issuance could reach $1.6 trillion in the base case and $3.7 trillion in the bull scenario by 2030, reflecting strong institutional adoption and expanding use beyond crypto trading into commerce and real-world payments. Stablecoins enhance 24x7 liquidity, real-time settlement, and programmable finance, promising to revolutionise cross-border payments, especially for small and medium businesses underserved by traditional rails. Key growth drivers include favourable regulation, especially in the US and emerging markets, payment network integration, new regulatory-compliant blockchains, and tokenised financial assets needing a digital settlement layer. Importantly, stablecoins are expected to coexist with tokenised deposits and central bank digital currencies, not replace them, reflecting varied regulatory trust and use-case fit. While stablecoins accelerate dollar dominance globally, bank-issued tokens may surpass stablecoin transaction volumes by 2030 due to industry preferences for trust and privacy. It would appear, stablecoins are transitioning from crypto niche to core financial infrastructure, enabling real-time, low-cost, borderless money movement that could reshape treasury operations, liquidity management, and financial inclusion worldwide.

[**Swift embraces blockchain technology**](https://thepaypers.com/crypto-web3-and-cbdc/news/swift-to-build-blockchain-based-ledger-for-optimal-cross-border-payments)Swift has announced plans to embed a blockchain-based shared ledger into its infrastructure, aiming to deliver real-time, 24/7 cross-border payments. Partnering with Consensys to develop the prototype, Swift is working alongside 30 global financial institutions, including JPMorgan Chase, Deutsche Bank, Bank of America, and BNP Paribas, to co-design the system. The shared ledger will record, sequence, and validate transactions, while smart contracts automate processes and optimise settlement rules. Crucially, Swift will not define the types of digital money users can use e.g. CBDCs, tokenised deposits, or stablecoins—but instead collaborate with commercial and central banks to ensure interoperability and regulatory compliance. With a network spanning 11,500 institutions in over 200 countries, Swift has long been the backbone of global payments. Previous experiments in tokenisation and cross-chain interoperability laid the groundwork for this integration. The initiative underscores Swift’s strategy to extend beyond financial messaging into blockchain-enabled settlement infrastructure, reinforcing trust, security, and resilience in global finance. This move comes as Swift introduces new rules for retail cross-border payments in September 2025, enhancing speed, predictability, and transparency. If successful, the ledger could transform global settlement efficiency, driving adoption of tokenised money and assets.

[**Is Andrew Bailey softening his stance to stablecoins?**](https://www.ft.com/content/eb013c4e-ed53-498b-9d75-2b5d9c7ecc65?accessToken=zwAGQCot5guYkdPrATxO7VNJi9OddStdnH7MZQ.MEYCIQDRDJk1qNoWUwpfhKs9yTTdOf9pMq3dBpjfdAn03Gz_HgIhAK90JCkocUznuHlHXF1ZdhSGwyPsBZMeYsslSyJ8ZzOr&sharetype=gift&token=cee36b8b-9615-492c-95ad-83d6d9f07532)This FT article written by the governor of the Bank of England raises one of the most pressing questions in digital finance: can stablecoins truly function as money, not just as a crypto market tool, but as part of the real economy? The Bank of England’s perspective is both pragmatic and cautionary. On one hand, stablecoins could drive much-needed innovation in payments, especially for cross-border settlement where inefficiency remains entrenched. On the other, their widespread use would demand the same trust, resilience, and clarity that underpin central bank and commercial bank money today. Three key conditions emerge for trust:

A) backing assets must be genuinely risk-free,

B) robust protection against operational failures like cyberattacks is required,

C) redemption terms must be transparent and uniform.

Few stablecoins currently satisfy these. The broader implication is profound. If stablecoins begin to decouple “money” from “credit creation,” they could alter the structure of fractional reserve banking. In theory, banks might lose their monopoly over money creation, with non-banks stepping in to provide credit, reshaping the financial system’s balance of trust, risk, and innovation. The coming UK consultation on systemic stablecoins will be pivotal. Granting them access to Bank of England accounts would elevate their legitimacy but also redefine the contours of modern banking. The question is not whether stablecoins can exist, but how they coexist with traditional credit and money, without destabilising the system they aim to modernise.

[**Barclays bank’s thoughts on banks in a tokenised world**](https://www.omfif.org/2025/10/big-banks-in-a-tokenised-world/?mc_cid=b0d742ff29&mc_eid=8fd65fb32f)Tokenised deposits and central bank reserves could anchor settlement finality while enabling atomic delivery-versus-payment, but the real disruption lies in cross-border payment-versus-payment, a space stablecoins may temporarily bridge until central bank solutions mature. There is a tension between crypto-native ideals and institutional preferences. While DeFi circles valorise stablecoins as safer than bank deposits, traditional treasurers and corporates still trust banks’ balance sheets. This divide illustrates the transitional role stablecoins may play, critical in the short term, but Barclays believe unlikely to displace bank-issued money without regulatory clarity and prudential oversight. Perhaps most thought-provoking is the shifting role of banks. Far from being sidelined, they emerge as the “connective tissue,” providing credit, liquidity, compliance, and interoperability in a tokenised settlement ecosystem. Tokenised money market funds and bank-issued settlement assets will only achieve scale if regulators treat them as eligible collateral, a reminder that innovation cannot outpace rulebooks. Tokenisation is not about replacing banks but redefining them. The key question is whether regulators and industry consortia can build common standards fast enough to prevent fragmentation, ensuring trust and efficiency in a radically reshaped financial system.

[**The shift from bank accounts to wallets and its implication for corporate treasuries**](http://linkedin.com/posts/tony-mclaughlin-7b627a3_corporate-treasury-in-a-world-of-wallets-activity-7379146729658454016-ZLHd)**.** The shift from traditional bank accounts to digital wallets marks a seismic change in corporate treasury management. Wallets don’t just digitise banking they redefine liquidity control by consolidating multiple tokenised assets across blockchains into a single programmable interface. This evolution promises instant settlement, seamless automation, and optimised cash flow 24/7, dismantling existing operational bottlenecks. Yet, the transition demands new skills, regulatory clarity, and technology integration. Forward-thinking treasury teams leveraging hybrid wallet-account models will gain competitive advantage by unlocking trapped liquidity, reducing complexity, and enabling real-time strategic financial management in a rapidly digitising global economy. This transformation is less about replacing banks and more about evolving treasury into a smarter, resilient function poised for the future of finance.

[**The Hidden Infrastructure Powering the Future of Money**](https://fintechmagazine.com/news/payment-technology-the-digital-revolution-in-your-wallet) According to Zeynep Sayin, Head of Visa Direct Partnerships, the evolution of payment technology is not just a technical upgrade, it is a fundamental re-architecture of global commerce. With 35% of all online purchases now flowing through mobile wallets, digital-first payments are no longer an option but the default. Consumers expect speed, invisibility, and control, reshaping financial services into an experience that must feel as seamless as a social app. Behind this simplicity lies an increasingly complex infrastructure. APIs and open banking are driving embedded finance, where payments integrate directly into ridesharing, e-commerce, or even social media marketplaces. Real-time payment networks are dismantling legacy batch-based systems, delivering instant value transfer that aligns with the digital economy’s pace. [Visa’s A2A](https://fintechmagazine.com/articles/visa-launches-a2a-payment-platform-with-card-like-protection) initiative exemplifies this shift, blending real-time bank payments with the security of card networks. [NatWest’s partnership with Prommt](https://www.openbankingexpo.com/news/payit-by-natwest-partners-with-prommt-to-help-businesses-modernise-payments/#:~:text=Payit%20by%20NatWest%20partners%20with%20Prommt%20to%20help%20businesses%20'modernise'%20payments,-Press%20Release%20%7C%20News&text=Prommt%2C%20the%20premium%20pay%2Dby,attractive%20for%20high%2Dvalue%20transactions.) shows how banks are pivoting to offer corporates instant, trackable, and authenticated payments, marrying efficiency with resilience. Yet, this digital leap introduces a new battlefield: security. Fraudsters innovate as fast as FinTechs, forcing advances in biometrics, AI-driven risk detection, and stronger authentication. The paradox is clear: consumers demand frictionless ease, but their trust depends on uncompromising safeguards. The stakes extend beyond convenience. Payment’s innovation is becoming the backbone of financial inclusion, cross-border commerce, and the tokenised economy of stablecoins and digital assets. The real question is not whether payments will go fully digital, but whether providers can build rails resilient enough to secure tomorrow’s economy while invisible enough to empower it.
