[**Mastercard and OMFIF, Future proofing Payments**](https://www.omfif.org/future-proofing-the-architecture-of-real-time-payments/?mc_cid=1310524a87&mc_eid=8fd65fb32f)**.** The OMFIF report "Future-proofing the Architecture of Real-Time Payments" explores how real-time payments (RTP) are becoming foundational to the modern financial ecosystem, enabling faster, more transparent, and efficient money movement globally. Traditional batch-based systems, riddled with delays, costs, and complexity, are being supplanted by RTP infrastructure that reduces settlement risk, enhances liquidity management, and broadens access. The report highlights a global shift toward interoperable, technology-agnostic platforms capable of handling increasing transaction volumes while maintaining security and compliance. Many central banks are expanding RTGS access beyond traditional banks to non-bank payment service providers, fostering innovation and competition. At the same time, cross-border harmonisation efforts aim to smooth fragmented regulatory landscapes and system incompatibilities. Emphasizing flexibility, programmability, and resilience, this modernization agenda positions real-time payments as critical infrastructure to support future digital currencies, tokenised assets, and programmable money. In short, real-time payments are no longer optional; they underpin the financial system's evolution toward greater inclusion, transparency, and speed in a complex global landscape. Indeed, the payments war has shifted from cards to control of blockchain rails. Mastercard is reportedly in late-stage talks to acquire [ZeroHash for $2 billion,](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/mastercard-aims-to-acquire-crypto-and-stablecoin-infrastructure-startup-zerohash/) following **Stripe’s $1.1 billion** purchase of Bridge earlier this year. Together, they’ve spent over **$3 billion** on stablecoin infrastructure in under twelve months. ZeroHash powers fiat-to-crypto conversion, tokenisation, and institutional crypto integration, the connective tissue between traditional and digital money. With stablecoins settling **$27.6 trillion** last year, Mastercard’s bet is clear: it wants to own the pipes of the new financial internet.

[**If stablecoins pay interest it could squeeze lending and smaller banks**](https://openbanker.beehiiv.com/p/stablecoinshock) - The US **GENIUS Act** represents the most consequential monetary reform in decades, embedding stablecoins within the U.S. financial architecture while prohibiting them from paying interest. Congress sought to promote payments innovation without destabilizing the credit engine that fuels Main Street lending. Yet the Act’s safeguard is already eroding. Wallets and exchanges increasingly offer “yield” or “cash-back,” effectively replicating interest-bearing deposits. If left unchecked, this could quietly rewire how credit is created in America. Unlike traditional deposits, stablecoin reserves are ringfenced and protected in custodial megabanks or in government treasuries, as opposed to fractional bankers that transform deposits into loans for households, farms, and small businesses and so incur risks. Academic models suggest that even a 6% deposit displacement could drain **$250 billion** in lending capacity. If stablecoins begin offering yield, the hit could exceed **$1.5 trillion,** a fifth of all U.S. consumer and SME credit. The risk isn’t bank profitability, it’s the **hollowing-out of local credit channels.** The liquidity becomes static collateral rather than working capital. The policy implication is clear: to preserve the flow of credit, regulators must close the yield loophole and treat “rewards” on stablecoin balances as what they are interest. The GENIUS Act’s promise of innovation without disintermediation depends on it. Yes, there is a risk as we have not seen mass stablecoin adoption, but Stablecoins create faster flow of funds will release working capital being tied up by SMES and banks to enable faster payments, help foster innovation and DeFi lending, which is growing fast and already [worth over $25billion](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/the-state-of-crypto-leverage-q2-2025#:~:text=by%20Galaxy%20Research.-,CeFi%20and%20DeFi%20Lending,a%20new%20all%2Dtime%20high.). Maybe this is why the [Bank of International Settlement](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1196.pdf) study found that a 10% increase in the use of digital payments correlates with a 1% increase in GDP per capita growth over two years?

[**Stablecoin startup raises $10million to improve global transfers**](https://fortune.com/crypto/2025/10/29/startup-hercle-raises-10-million-to-build-out-stablecoin-based-global-money-transfers/)**.** In an era where information moves at light speed, but money often crawls, a US company called **Hercle** has just raised $10million, is a telling experiment in how blockchain might finally deliver on its long-promised efficiencies. The firm’s use of US Dollar **stablecoins** offers a pragmatic bridge between traditional finance and the crypto world’s infrastructure, aiming to make cross-border settlements as frictionless as domestic transfers., This fund raise once again demonstrates ongoing interest and commitment as well as institutional confidence in stablecoin-backed payments. The company claims to have processed over **$20 billion** to date, with most transactions clearing in minutes. Such figures hint at both the scale of the problem and the appetite for solutions that bypass legacy banking rails. The US new **stablecoin legislation**, GENIUS, gives regulatory clarity just as corporates and payment providers are experimenting with blockchain settlement. Embedding stablecoins into payment and trade finance networks, could compress working capital cycles and transform liquidity management. Yet, the company still faces Herculean tasks: scaling compliance across jurisdictions and proving long-term resilience If it succeeds, Hercle may exemplify how stablecoins shift from speculative instruments to the **plumbing of global finance**.

[**VISA extends it commitment to stablecoins as volumes quadrupled in the last quarter**](https://www.theblock.co/post/376682/visa-adding-support-for-four-stablecoins-on-four-unique-blockchains-as-spend-quadrupled-last-quarter). Visa’s decision to expand support for four additional stablecoins across four distinct blockchains marks more than a technical upgrade, it arguably signals a structural turning point for the global payments industry. What began as an experiment in digital assets has become a pragmatic response to the inefficiencies of legacy settlement systems. Visa’s data are telling, stablecoin-linked spending on its network quadrupled in the last quarter, underscoring a rapid normalisation of tokenised money in day-to-day commerce. This development shows that stablecoins, oare now edging toward the financial mainstream. By enabling conversion into more than 25 fiat currencies, Visa is effectively bridging the gap between blockchain settlement and traditional money flows. The result is a hybrid model where blockchain speed and transparency meet the regulatory and consumer trust of established payment networks. For banks and corporates, the implications are profound. Stablecoins offer instant cross-border settlement without the frictions of correspondent banking, challenging the economics of foreign exchange and trade finance. Yet the shift also raises questions: will private tokens become the dominant rails of global liquidity, or will regulators reassert control through central bank digital currencies?

[**IBM’s new platform. “Digital Asset Haven”**](https://newsroom.ibm.com/ibm-digital-asset-haven)**.** IBM has stepped into the digital-asset arena by launching a platform designed for financial institutions, governments and regulated enterprises to manage the full lifecycle of digital assets, from custody to transaction to settlement embracing more than 40 public and private blockchains. What stands out is the ambition to provide **institutional-grade infrastructure** for tokenised payments, stablecoins, regulatory compliance and cross-chain settlement under one roof. For the payments industry, this represents a potential seismic shift. The traditional correspondence-banking rails and legacy settlement systems, long criticised for delay and cost, may increasingly be supplanted by a model where tokenised money flows are managed with the same governance, custody and risk controls as conventional assets. IBM’s emphasis on governance, wallet entitlement, multi-party approvals, hardware security modules and even quantum-safe cryptography highlights that the infrastructure won’t be a niche experiment it aims to plug into the heart of regulated finance. But the implications go deeper: if large-scale players adopt tokenised rails and platforms such as IBM’s Digital Asset Haven, we may see a bifurcation of payment systems. On one side: traditional fiat rails with banks, correspondent networks, days-long settlement. On the other: tokenised rails with near-instant settlement, global reach and programmable contracts, yet under the same regulatory umbrella. That means treasury teams, corporates, PSPs should prepare for a world where liquidity lives on chains as much as it lives in bank ledgers. In short, digital-asset settlement infrastructure is migrating from “crypto frontier” into mainstream payments engineering.
