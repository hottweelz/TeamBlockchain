[**Bank of America CEO warns up to $6 trillion in deposits could shift to stablecoins if allowed to pay interest**](https://www.theblock.co/post/385724/bank-of-america-ceo-stablecoin-yields?utm_campaign=website&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=newsletter)**.** The legislative battle over interest-bearing stablecoins has reached a fever pitch, with Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan warning that **$6 trillion in deposits**, roughly 35% of the cash that U.S. commercial look after, could migrate into digital dollars. Assuming banks are earning 1.5% on bank deposits this represents a $90billion loss of revenue for the banking sector p.a. This shift is not merely an operational hurdle for lenders; it represents a profound challenge to the Federal Reserve’s "monetary sovereignty". Stablecoins increasingly act as a digital safe haven, effectively decreasing a central bank’s ability to control the money supply. Unlike traditional bank deposits, which are recycled into the economy via commercial lending, stablecoin reserves are often parked in short-term U.S. Treasuries. This "narrow bank" model can disrupt the transmission of monetary policy by making interest rates less sensitive to central bank key rates, creating a parallel financial system that operates outside the Fed's direct influence.

The "Activity-Based" Loophole: The current Senate compromise, prohibiting passive interest while allowing "activity-based" rewards like staking, seeks to prevent stablecoins from becoming high-yield competitors to checking accounts. However, this distinction has created a significant "reward loophole". Industry players have recently pulled support for the bill, arguing that overly aggressive restrictions on these rewards could stifle innovation. In practice, crypto exchanges are already positioning themselves to circumvent the ban on passive interest by reclassifying yields as loyalty points, transaction rebates, or liquidity incentives. For example, instead of paying "interest" on an idle balance, a platform might reward a user for "maintaining a target liquidity level" or performing a single nominal monthly transaction. This allows platforms to offer returns comparable to bank savings accounts, roughly **5%** in early 2026, while technically complying with the letter of the law. Banking groups warn that this "inducement" model will still trigger a massive deposit exodus, particularly from community banks that rely on local deposits to fund small business and student loans. As stablecoins bridge global payment needs directly to U.S. public debt, they create a persistent demand for "safe assets" that can compress risk-free yields and further erode the efficacy of conventional macroeconomic stabilisation tools.

[**Trillions in Limbo: The Hidden Cost of Global Money Movement.**](https://stabledash.com/news/2026-01-14-why-stablecoins-will-reshape-treasury-management-before-they-disrupt-payments)The global financial system routinely traps trillions in idle capital, not through inefficiency but necessity. [Nostro accounts](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/nostro-account/#:~:text=An%20account%20that%20a%20bank,are%20involved%20in%20international%20trade.), pre-funded bank balances held abroad, exist as buffers against slow, opaque settlement. The same funds viewed from the other side become [vostro accounts](https://legal-resources.uslegalforms.com/v/vostro-account#:~:text=For%20instance%2C%20a%20Mexican%20bank,known%20as%20a%20nostro%20account.), yet the structure remains: replicated balances compensating for timing and counterparty risk. This architecture, born of the 1974 Herstatt failure, forces treasurers to over-provision liquidity across jurisdictions, sacrificing yield and working capital to guarantee payments arrive on time. Cross-border obligations expose the fragility. A U.S. firm with euro payables must convert and route funds, yet time-zone mismatches and RTGS windows can freeze capital for hours or days. Delays trigger compliance holds or exception queues, resolved through phone calls rather than APIs. The result is structural inefficiency: cash sits unproductive to hedge uncertainty. Atomic settlement via smart contracts offers determinism, both legs of a trade execute together or not at all, eliminating Herstatt risk and weekend pricing distortions. Bank-backed deposit tokens (JPMorgan’s JPMD on Base) bring institutional liquidity onto programmable rails, enabling real-time conversion into yield-bearing tokenized assets without traditional buffers. For the wider economy, the impact is profound. Reduced pre-funding releases trapped capital for productive use—boosting investment, consumption, and growth. Lower settlement risk and faster liquidity routing could compress borrowing costs and enhance monetary policy transmission. SMEs, often starved of working capital, stand to gain most: instant cross-border payments and atomic DvP reduce the need for large buffers, freeing cash for hiring, inventory, or expansion. In a high-interest environment, the cost of idle capital is acute; programmable settlement could meaningfully improve SME resilience and competitiveness. The shift is already underway, Partior and Fnality demonstrate live multi-currency corridors, scale depends on regulatory alignment and adoption. If successful, the economy moves from hope-based liquidity management to execution-guaranteed efficiency, unlocking trillions for growth rather than insurance.

Investors gain diversification into previously gated sectors, balancing impact with performance as sustainable finance resets around transition and programmable debt.
