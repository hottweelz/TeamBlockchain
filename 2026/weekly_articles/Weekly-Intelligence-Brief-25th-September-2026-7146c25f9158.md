**Weekly Intelligence Brief 25th September 2026**

**Programmable money and AI are moving into mainstream finance, from interbank tokenised deposits and stablecoin card settlement to round-the-clock share trading and payments made by AI agents. These developments could lower costs and open new markets, but their wider economic value will depend on clear ownership rights, reliable redemption, accountable AI agents and consumer protection.**

[**Could Dollar Stablecoins Redraw the Map of Global Money? The Bank of England’s Warning for Business**](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/speech/2026/september/carolyn-wilkins-speech-at-queens-university-belfast)**.** Bank of England policymaker Carolyn Wilkins argues that stablecoins could reshape both payments and monetary power. Dollar tokens already dominate the market, offering businesses faster, round-the-clock cross-border settlement and potentially cheaper access to international customers. Yet history shows that private money works at scale only when holders trust its reserves, can redeem at par and have clear protections during a crisis. As adoption grows, issuers’ purchases of US Treasury bills could reinforce dollar dominance, while sudden redemptions could transmit stress back into bond markets. Businesses considering stablecoin payments must therefore assess issuer strength, redemption rights and the jurisdictions governing their transactions, alongside the promised savings. For the wider economy, cheaper transfers could improve trade and remittances, but a shift away from bank deposits may affect lending, while fragmented payment networks could complicate oversight. Digital payment rails still depend on credible institutions.

[**BlackRock’s Machine-Native Economy: Why AI Agents Will Need Digital Money, Tokenized Assets and Tradable Compute**](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/literature/whitepaper/the-machine-native-economy.pdf)**.** BlackRock argues that artificial intelligence and digital assets are converging into a machine-native economy. AI provides machine-native intelligence, while stablecoins, tokenised assets and blockchains supply money, ownership records and programmable settlement that autonomous agents can use without human intervention. Emerging protocols including x402, ACP and AP2 could support high-frequency, low-value payments for data, services and computing power. BlackRock also identifies compute as a tokenised asset class, with hyperscaler cloud revenues projected to exceed $1 trillion annually by 2030.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** companies may need systems that machines can discover, verify, purchase from and pay automatically. Banks, payment providers and asset managers face new markets in agent identity, programmable money, tokenized collateral and compute financing. However, adoption depends on authorization, legal accountability, interoperability and controls preventing autonomous systems from spending or contracting beyond their mandates.

[**Beyond Blind Fiat: How On-Chain Provenance and Digital Money Eliminate Trillion-Dollar Banking Compliance Failures**](https://tradersunion.com/news/financial-news/show/3420285-a7-sanctions-evasion-global-banks/)**.** Traditional banks globally are trapped in an endless cycle of multi-billion-dollar regulatory fines because tracking legacy fiat across correspondent accounts is fundamentally broken. Opaque, multi-layered fiat wires force institutions to rely on flawed, retrospective monitoring systems, leaving compliance teams incapable of verifying historical fund origins. Digital money fundamentally resolves this opacity. Built on public or permissioned distributed ledgers, programmable digital assets feature an immutable cryptographic trail, allowing institutions, regulators, and AI agents to instantly trace an asset's exact digital footprint across its previous 5, 10, or 20 owners.

**Impact on the wider economy**. Shifting away from fine-plagued legacy banks toward auditable digital money drastically reduces compliance overheads, eliminates systemic settlement delays, and provides businesses with a transparent, frictionless alternative for global payments.

[**Britain’s Banks Make Tokenised Deposits Interoperable for the First Time**](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/uk-banks-make-first-interbank-transactions-using-tokenised-deposits-2026-09-23/)**.** Britain’s banks have completed what UK Finance describes as the world’s first interbank transfers using tokenised commercial-bank deposits. Lloyds, NatWest and Barclays tested remortgage transactions, while HSBC and other banks simulated a marketplace payment. Programmable funds were locked and released only when contractual conditions were met, linking payment directly to delivery or completion. Unlike privately issued stablecoins, tokenised deposits retain the legal status of money held in a bank account. The project will now develop a rulebook and governance structure, with three digital bonds planned for early 2027.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** tokenised deposits could reduce conveyancing delays, fraud, reconciliation and trapped liquidity while preserving bank-based credit creation. Success, however, depends on interoperability, common liability rules and participation beyond a few large banks; otherwise, new digital rails may reproduce the fragmentation they are intended to remove.

[**SoFiUSD Moves Stablecoins into $25 Billion of Mastercard Card Settlement**](https://www.barrons.com/articles/sofi-stock-stablecoin-crypto-banks-198153fe)**.** SoFi has begun settling card transactions over Mastercard’s network using SoFiUSD, claiming a first for a US bank. The program is expected to process more than $25 billion annually and connects a bank-issued dollar stablecoin with card infrastructure. SoFiUSD was offered to enterprise partners before becoming available to consumers, and SoFi plans conversion into interest-bearing tokenized deposits and round-the-clock international transfers. Mastercard’s participation shows how card networks are adapting their settlement layers rather than waiting to be displaced by blockchain payments. **What this means for business and the wider economy:** stablecoin settlement could improve liquidity, accelerate merchant funding and reduce cross-border treasury friction without requiring consumers to abandon cards. It blurs the distinction between deposits, payment tokens and settlement assets. Banks must manage reserve, redemption and liquidity risks while regulators determine how these instruments interact with deposit protection and monetary policy.

[**NYSE and Blockchain.com Plan 24-Hour Global Trading of Tokenised US Shares**](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nyse-and-blockchaincom-to-explore-selling-tokenised-stocks-4913157)

The New York Stock Exchange and Blockchain.com have agreed to explore global access to tokenised versions of NYSE-listed shares and exchange-traded funds, subject to regulatory approval. The collaboration would connect Blockchain.com users to the NYSE’s planned digital alternative trading system and permit trading beyond conventional hours. Their agreement includes a two-way data exchange: ICE Data Services would distribute Blockchain.com’s crypto analytics, while NYSE and ICE data would appear within Blockchain.com’s platform and AI assistant.

**What this means for business and the wider economy:** tokenised equities could expand international access, fractional ownership and continuous trading while creating new distribution channels for issuers and exchanges. Yet the structure matters. Many stock tokens provide economic exposure without voting, dividend or direct ownership rights. Regulators and investors need clear rules clearly distinguishing genuine digital shares from derivatives or IOUs before liquidity migrates across fragmented markets.

[**Global Banks Warn That AI Shopping Agents Are Outrunning Consumer Protection**](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/banks-warn-ai-shopping-bots-raise-scam-fraud-data-privacy-risks-2026-09-22)

NatWest, Bank of America, ING, Capital One, Commonwealth Bank of Australia and ASB Bank warn that AI shopping agents are developing faster than consumer protections. Agents can select products and initiate payments, but may expose card details, steer users toward weaker payment methods or exceed the customer’s intentions. John Lewis says AI-originated searches rose from 0.3% to 2.5% in a year, showing that agentic commerce already influences retail traffic. The banks propose disclosure when an agent participates, transparency over recommendations, stronger data safeguards, interoperability and clear redress. **What this means for business and the wider economy:** retailers must become discoverable to machines while proving that offers, permissions and payments are authentic. Banks, insurers and regulators must establish liability when an agent makes a mistake. Without trusted identity, mandate and transaction evidence, convenience could convert rapidly into fraud losses and consumer resistance.
