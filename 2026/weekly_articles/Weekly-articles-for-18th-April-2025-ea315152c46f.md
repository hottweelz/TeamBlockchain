[5 takeaways from Finastra Europe Corporate Banking Day: AI, digitisation, and a changing industry](https://www.tradefinanceglobal.com/posts/5-takeaways-from-finastra-europe-corporate-banking-day-ai-digitisation-and-a-changing-industry/)- Digitisation, AI, tokenisation—corporate banking is being pulled into the future by forces it can no longer ignore. The traditional model is cracking under pressure from geopolitical shifts, rising customer expectations, and a wave of new financial technologies. The opportunity? Enormous. The challenge? Alignment. AI is increasingly being used from automating letters of credit to powering autonomous supply chains, it’s becoming less a buzzword and more a backbone. But its true potential lies in blending human insight with machine precision—unlocking speed, accuracy, and sustainability tracking at scale. And then there’s tokenisation. Long theorised, it’s now being tested in trade finance. Smart contracts, risk coins, and tokenised assets could finally remove friction from capital flows. But without industry-wide standards, innovation risks becoming siloed. The sector stands at a crossroad: digitise, decentralise, and automate—or risk becoming irrelevant in a system that no longer waits for manual processes.

[**Visa might be about to change the game for stablecoins.**](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/04/14/visa-is-joining-the-paxos-robinhood-stablecoin-consortium-sources)Its reported move to join the Global Dollar Network (USDG)—a stablecoin ecosystem built by Paxos and regulated out of Singapore—marks the first serious bridge between traditional finance and a new breed of yield-sharing, enterprise-ready digital dollars. Unlike USDT or USDC, USDG doesn’t hoard the yield from its reserves. It shares it with the companies that build liquidity and connections across the network. That’s a structural shift in how value flows in stablecoin economies. Backed by the Singaporean bank DBS and supervised by the Monetary Authority of Singapore, USDG positions itself as both compliant and commercially attractive. Add in Visa’s global rails and upcoming integrations with self-custody wallets like World App, and you’ve got a potential leap in digital money–fiat interoperability. This isn’t just another corporate crypto play. It could be the first real move toward embedding stablecoins into everyday payments—with yield models that reward the ecosystem, not just the issuer. If Visa joins, it’s not just validating USDG. It’s quietly redrawing the lines between digital and traditional finance.

1. [Are Stablecoins a Threat or an Opportunity for Banks?](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/are-stablecoins-a-threat-or-an-opportunity-for-banks/?ct=t(EMAIL_CAMPAIGN_4_11_2025_18_21)&mc_cid=cad7db763d&mc_eid=562a6d0508)- Banks are standing at a fork in the digital road—evolve with stablecoins, or risk being left behind by them**.** Once fringe, stablecoins now sit at the edge of mainstream financial infrastructure, promising faster, cheaper, programmable money. For banks, they represent both threat and opportunity: a potential drain on deposits and a challenge to their role as intermediaries, but also a bridge to new models of custody, compliance, and transaction rails. Banks such as JPMorgan and BNY Mellon, are already building. Others are watching from the sidelines, constrained by regulatory uncertainty and institutional inertia. But the clock is ticking. As the U.S. inches closer to a Federal framework for stablecoins, the groundwork is being laid for a hybrid financial future—where dollars can be tokenised, stored, and settled instantly, and where trust shifts from institutions to infrastructure. The real question isn’t whether stablecoins will change banking? It’s whether banks are ready to change themselves?
