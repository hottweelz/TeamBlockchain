[Revoult looks to speed up Bitcoin payments](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/revolut-and-lightspark-join-forces-to-speed-bitcoin-payments/) – Revolut latest announcement is potentially making Bitcoin an alternative as a payment method for **borderless digital cash** at speed and scale. By integrating the **Lightning Network** and the **Universal Money Address (UMA)** standard, Revolut is transforming Bitcoin from an asset into infrastructure. For years, Bitcoin’s promise as a payment rail was buried beneath speculation and clunky user experiences. Now, Revolut is changing that narrative—not by reinventing money, but by plugging into **a global, open-source monetary grid.** UMA lets anyone, anywhere, send value across wallets and platforms, in any currency, instantly. Bitcoin’s Lightning Network doesn’t just make payments faster it potentially **removes the need for traditional legacy rails altogether**. While banks cling to decades-old architecture, Revolut is quietly building on **permissionless protocols** that settle value at internet speed. The implication? Are we seeing a reshaping what global payments can be. **Money becomes code. Identity becomes an address. And borders become optional.**
2. [The importance of FinTech sector and the role of blockchain technology](https://acropolium.com/blog/blockchain-in-finance-use-cases-transforming-financial-services/#:~:text=The%20global%20fintech%20blockchain%20market,revenue%20increase%20within%206%20months.) - [Boston Consulting Group](https://fintechnews.ch/fintech/global-fintech-revenue-set-to-reach-us1-5t-by-2030/71735/) and QED Investors estimates by 2030, the sector is expected to reach a market size of [US$1.5 trillion in revenue](https://fintechnews.ch/fintech/global-fintech-revenue-set-to-reach-us1-5t-by-2030/71735/), five times the market’s current size. Blockchain is no longer simply an emerging technology in finance it’s becoming the foundation for a new financial operating system. Where FinTech firms once sought to digitise existing infrastructure, blockchain technology is now enabling entirely new rails for value transfer, identity, and financial contracts. Rather than optimizing old workflows, it offers programmable, permissionless systems that operate globally and in real time. The market reflects this shift: over 5,000 payments-focused FinTechs already generate more than $100 billion annually, and the FinTech blockchain market is projected to surge from $3.4 billion in 2024 to $49.2 billion by 2030. By eliminating intermediaries and automating execution through smart contracts, blockchain-powered systems reduce costs, settlement times, and fraud risk. It introduces new efficiencies in lending, insurance, cross-border payments, asset tokenisation, and digital identity. More than just speeding up transactions, blockchain technology redefines who can access the financial system and on what terms lowering barriers for previously excluded individuals and businesses. Yet, the most profound change may be invisible. Blockchains turns finance into infrastructure: open-source, real-time, and trust-minimised. Digital assets settle in seconds, identity becomes portable and user-controlled, and value flows without requiring central intermediaries. Platforms such as Circle, Ripple, Aave, and Uniswap demonstrate how core financial services can operate without banks or clearinghouses, while stablecoins and tokenised assets begin to shift how capital is stored and moved. The challenge ahead is not technical—it’s institutional. Regulatory clarity, legacy integration, and privacy design are critical hurdles. But the direction is clear: blockchain technology is not just improving finance. It is rewiring it. Blockchain is not a layer on top of finance. Increasingly, it is finance.

[Interest grows in the Uk Digital Gilt initiative](https://capitalpioneer.co.uk/support-grows-for-digit/)- [The March 18 announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/chancellor-to-press-on-with-digit-to-boost-investment) represents the UK government’s commitment to modernising its £2.5 trillion ($3.2 trillion) gilt market using blockchain technology UK Chancellor Rachel Reeves emphasised the strategic [importance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/announcement-of-preliminary-market-engagement-exercise-for-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit-pilot/additional-information-and-engagement-on-the-digital-gilt-instrument-digit) of the initiative during a meeting with Fintech CEOs at No. 11 Downing Street, stating: “*The UK is leading the way on digital innovation, and the creation of DIGIT will help to transform our world-leading capital markets sector and drive economic growth.”* The Investment Association’s (IA) endorsement of the UK’s DIGIT Pilot signals more than a policy alignment it marks the beginning of a potential transformation in sovereign debt markets. By backing blockchain-based tokenised gilts, the IA is effectively calling time on legacy infrastructure and opening the door to a new financial architecture where government bonds are programmable and instantly transferable. This shift isn't just about efficiency; it's about rewriting the rules of market access and liquidity. If digital gilts become standard, they could be seamlessly integrated into tokenised money market funds, real-time settlement systems, and AI-managed portfolios—blurring the line between public debt and digital assets. The UK’s ambition here is clear: become the first G7 nation to anchor its sovereign funding on-chain. The question is not whether markets are ready, they are, but whether regulators and institutions can move at the speed of innovation without compromising stability. In a world of fragmented financial plumbing, a digital gilt could become the connective tissue for a truly interoperable capital market.

[The Bankers Association for Trade and Finance- Stablecoins and the Reinvention of Money Movement](https://www.baft.org/policy-news/baft-white-paper-on-the-impact-of-stablecoins-on-payments/#:~:text=%E2%80%9CThe%20Impact%20of%20Stablecoins%20on,regulators%2C%20and%20payment%20service%20providers.) - BAFT’s white paper casts stablecoins not as fringe innovation but as a credible challenge to the core mechanics of global payments. With the potential to enable near-instant, low-cost, programmable value transfer, stablecoins are redefining the infrastructure underlying both domestic and cross-border transactions. But their rise exposes more than inefficiencies—it exposes institutional unpreparedness. This isn’t just about faster payments—it’s about **disintermediating entrenched layers** of banking, FX, and clearing systems that were designed for a pre-digital age. As stablecoins bypass legacy rails, they spotlight the **cost, complexity, and fragility of the status quo.** Yet the path forward is fraught. Liquidity concerns, regulatory fragmentation, and operational risks all loom large. Without clear rules or integration frameworks, stablecoins risk becoming efficient—but **isolated—tools**, unable to scale safely within the broader financial ecosystem. BAFT’s conclusion is clear: the opportunity is transformative, but only if **regulators and financial institutions move from caution to co-creation**. Sandboxes, public-private experiments, and interoperable standards are not just optional—they are essential. Stablecoins offer more than speed—they offer a chance to re-architect trust, access, and functionality in the global financial system.
   The question is no longer whether stablecoins will matter, but **whether the institutions they disrupt will adapt in time.**

[Using the card 'beyond' payments to find the holy grail](https://thepaypers.com/thought-leader-insights/using-the-card-beyond-payments-to-find-the-holy-grail--1273574)- The payments industry has long been trapped in a trade-off: reduce friction, and you invite fraud; tighten security, and you alienate users. For decades, innovation has danced around this dilemma without solving it. The rise of numberless cards and tap-to-authenticate features signals a deeper shift: the physical card is evolving from a passive payment tool into an active digital identity key. When a card can be tapped against a phone to authenticate an online purchase, suddenly the boundaries between “card present” and “card not present” collapse. The card is both—present physically, confirmed digitally. Friction fades, and fraud loses ground. This is more than a UI upgrade. It’s a redefinition of trust in the digital economy where the card becomes the bridge between physical and digital identity, merging familiarity with cryptographic certainty. The very object we’ve carried in our wallets for decades may hold the key to solving a challenge the payments industry has wrestled with for years. Could the future of fraud prevention be no more passwords, firewalls, or friction, but smarter cards and a more intelligent relationship between the user, their device, and their identity? The card that simply paid is becoming the card that proves, that protects, and that prevents. Is revolution in payments may already be in your pocket?
