As the 2025 deadline looms, the question isn’t whether banks will comply, but how many will do so cost-effectively.

**2.**[**Bitcoin Surpasses Amazon: Now the World’s 5th-Largest Asset**](https://www.theblock.co/post/362383/bitcoin-worlds-fifth-largest-asset?utm_campaign=website&utm_medium=referral&utm_source=newsletter) - As Bitcoin surged past **$122,000** this week, it became the **5th largest asset in the world**, eclipsing Amazon, Silver, and Google with a market cap of **$2.4 trillion.** This isn’t just a speculative rally. It’s a signal that the global financial system is quietly pivoting. **Institutional capital is flowing in** via U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs, which have attracted **$16B+** in net inflows, providing deep, regulated liquidity for the first time. Landmark bills such as the **CLARITY Act** and **GENIUS Act** could finally give digital assets legal certainty in the U.S. Bitcoin is behaving like a “high beta monetary asset,” moving in anticipation of rate cuts and dollar weakness.

What’s happening here is profound. Bitcoin is no longer just a speculative “digital gold.” It is morphing into:

* A **non-sovereign monetary base** that functions outside the reach of central banks.
* A **programmable reserve asset** for treasuries and institutions.
* A collateral layer for an emerging on-chain financial system.

As treasurers, allocators, and policymakers weigh these shifts, one thing is clear: **the plumbing of global finance is being re-engineered in real time.** If these trends hold, Bitcoin could plausibly challenge **$150,000** before year-end. But price targets miss the bigger point: this is a rewiring of value itself.

**3.** [**Nvidia the world’s first $4trillion company**](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2025-07-16/nvidia-4-trillion-artificial-intelligence-silicon-valley) - Nvidia’s meteoric rise to a $4 trillion market cap has made it the undisputed king of the AI revolution, eclipsing even Microsoft and Apple. Once known primarily for powering high-end graphics cards for gamers, the company has reinvented itself as the backbone of generative AI and machine learning, with its chips now driving everything from ChatGPT to autonomous vehicles. Its dominance is such that more than 75% of the world’s supercomputers rely on Nvidia’s hardware, and the U.S. government’s decision this week to allow the sale of certain AI chips to China has only strengthened its position, sending its stock soaring. But what comes next. Nvidia’s success has painted a target on its back. Major players such as OpenAI, Google, and Amazon are investing heavily in designing their own custom AI chips, seeking to loosen their dependence on Nvidia’s hardware. Geopolitical tensions add another layer of complexity, with more than half of Nvidia’s revenues tied to foreign markets and its supply chains intertwined with Taiwan.

There is also a looming infrastructural challenge, can the global energy grid sustain the power-hungry demands of AI data centres? The very technology fuelling Nvidia’s rise may hit physical and environmental limits that no amount of silicon innovation can immediately overcome. For now, Nvidia’s dominance appears unassailable. Its ability to innovate at the cutting edge of hardware and software ecosystems gives it an unparalleled moat. But history suggests that no company stays the top dog forever. Will Nvidia evolve beyond being the world’s most advanced chipmaker into the orchestrator of an entire AI-powered platform economy? Or will its own success sow the seeds of disruption, enabling competitors to build ecosystems where Nvidia’s chips are no longer indispensable?

Even a giant like Nvidia must run faster to avoid being overtaken.

**4.** [**Ant’s One Trillion Dollar Bet with Circle to change treasuries in the future**](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2025/07/11/ant-international-usdc-treasury-operations/)**-** Chinese based Ant International is reportedly exploring the integration of Circle’s USDC into its $1T blockchain treasury platform—a move that could redefine global liquidity management. To date, Ant’s platform uses tokenised commercial bank deposits with a closed network of global banks (HSBC, JPMorgan, Deutsche Bank). But adding a regulated, dollar-backed stablecoin like USDC could unlock interoperability beyond this walled garden—connecting treasuries to an emerging ecosystem of CBDCs, tokenized assets, and 24/7 global payments.

Why this matters for CFOs & treasurers:

* Ant claims 30-60% savings in liquidity & FX costs with blockchain-based cash pooling.
* A USDC integration could accelerate real-time treasury operations globally.
* New U.S. stablecoin laws (GENIUS Act) and licensing efforts in Singapore, Hong Kong and Luxembourg highlight the regulatory dimension.
* This is about modernising the core plumbing of corporate finance, moving from fragmented nostro accounts to global, tokenised cash management.

The future of treasury is being built.

[**5. The UK’s Next-Generation Payments Infrastructure: A Bold Vision for Digital Money**](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2025/july/a-new-approach-to-retail-payments-infrastructure) **-**

The UK is reimagining the future of money. With the National Payments Vision, it’s not just upgrading pipes and wires it’s building the foundation for a financial ecosystem where speed, security, and innovation are no longer trade-offs but coexisting imperatives. At its core is an ambitious new model that fuses public sector oversight with private sector dynamism.

By creating a **Retail Payments Infrastructure Board,** chaired by the Bank of England, and a new **industry-led Delivery Company**, the UK is signalling that the next generation of payments will be too complex and too important to leave to a single actor. It’s a recognition that future infrastructure must do more than process Faster Payments or Bacs transactions—it must also anticipate the rise of **tokenised money, stablecoins, and even a digital pound (CBDC).**

This model is not just about rails and settlement systems. It’s about preparing for programmable money, cross-border interoperability, and an economy where payments are embedded invisibly into commerce. As AI and blockchain converge to transform how value flows globally, the UK’s approach offers a blueprint for **predictive governance**—but it also raises hard questions about sovereignty, competition, and resilience.

Will this collaborative model truly deliver the flexibility needed for the era of decentralised finance and tokenised assets? Or will legacy institutions struggle to keep pace with fintech-native innovation? The answers will shape not just the UK’s competitiveness but potentially the global standards for digital payments in the 21st century.

The UK has fired the starting gun on a race where infrastructure is destiny. The rest of the world is watching.

[**6. The USA is set to ensure the Fed is not allowed to issue a CBDC**](https://rollcall.com/2025/07/16/house-adopts-rule-for-crypto-defense-bills/)**.** It is being proposed that a bill is to be passed as part of the national defence bill. As the U.S. Congress edges closer to passing the GENIUS Act (to regulate stablecoins) and considers banning a U.S. central bank digital currency (CBDC), other central banks around the world are watching with unease and with opportunity. If the U.S. formally outlaws a digital dollar, it would be the first major economy to reject a CBDC outright. This would embolden critics of central bank digital currencies globally, particularly in regions like the EU, where the digital euro project is already facing political resistance over privacy concerns. Countries such as Germany, wary of government overreach in payments, could lean harder against CBDCs.

At the same time, U.S. regulatory clarity on stablecoins could make them a de facto private-sector alternative to CBDCs, especially in emerging markets with weak domestic currencies. If dollar-backed stablecoins are seen as safe and well-regulated, they could accelerate dollarization of cross-border payments and undermine efforts by central banks in Africa, Latin America, and Asia to launch their own CBDCs.

For China, the U.S. rejection of a digital dollar would leave the e-CNY (digital yuan) as the leading major-economy CBDC, reinforcing Beijing’s push for a state-controlled alternative to the dollar in global trade.

Central banks now face a stark choice: Double down on CBDCs as tools for monetary sovereignty and payment innovation. Or lean into public-private collaboration, using regulated stablecoins as an overlay on existing systems.

Whatever path they choose, Washington’s decisions could set the tone for the global debate: Is the future of digital money best driven by governments—or by regulated private innovation? Does this mean that if the Bank of England and the ECB issue a CDBC they cannot interact with any US Federal agency?
