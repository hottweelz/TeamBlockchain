**Weekly Intelligence Brief 31st July 2026**

Financial markets are moving toward a 24/7, AI-driven operating model as the London Stock Exchange extends trading, tokenised deposits cross public blockchains, tokenised repo cuts funding costs and digital gilts demand programmable sterling. Meanwhile, autonomous AI, real-time industrial payments and Open USD are reshaping profits, productivity, tax receipts and monetary sovereignty, forcing banks, regulators and businesses to decide who controls digital money, settlement infrastructure and machine-led commerce.

[**London Stock Exchange Goes 24/5: ETPs First, Equities Next, But Are Tokenised Securities the Endgame?**](https://quoteddata.com/2026/07/lse-plans-near-24-hour-etp-trading-from-2027/)The London Stock Exchange plans to launch LSE 24 in the first half of 2027, creating a venue for near-continuous weekday trading. Exchange-traded products will be the first assets available, allowing investors and AI-enabled systems to react to events, access liquidity and manage risk across time zones. LSE 24 will operate overnight alongside the Main Market and connect with LSEG’s digital infrastructure, subject to regulatory approval. Equities are identified as the likely next asset class. Beyond shares, the strategic question is whether tokenised bonds, funds and other digital securities will follow, enabling issuance, trading, settlement and asset servicing on connected digital rails. The initiative shows how cryptocurrency markets have reset expectations around availability. London is no longer extending trading hours; it is preparing for markets in which algorithms and AI agents expect assets, data and settlement to remain continuously accessible.

**[Tokenised Bank Deposits Break Out of Banking’s Walled Gardens](https://layerzero.network/blog/keeta-and-layerzero-bring-tokenized-bank-deposits?utm_source=chatgpt.com" \t "_new).** LayerZero and Keeta have partnered to make tokenised commercial bank deposits transferable across Ethereum, Solana, Base and the Keeta Network. The system uses commercial bank deposits held through Bivo to support regulated digital bank money, with US dollars and eight additional currencies planned. The significance is that tokenised deposits are moving beyond closed institutional networks toward interoperable public blockchains. Banks could eventually settle tokenised securities, treasury transactions and cross-border payments using familiar commercial bank liabilities rather than unregulated cryptoassets. For businesses, this could combine blockchain’s speed and programmability with established banking relationships and compliance controls. However, interoperability introduces questions around legal finality, deposit protection, sanctions screening, cybersecurity and responsibility when money crosses several networks. The commercial battle may shift from whether banks tokenise deposits to which interoperability layer becomes the trusted gateway connecting regulated money with public blockchain markets globally. However, if tokenised deposits are used to settle transactions directly as opposed to via a central bank how soon will it be before different tokenised deposits trade at different rates reflecting the underlying banks’ balance sheet strengths. Surely this would be the death of the Bank of England’s “singleness of money”?

[**AI Adoption Is Accelerating Faster Than Businesses Realise: From Digital Assistant to Autonomous Decision-Maker**](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/insights/ai/as-ai-moves-from-advice-to-authority-who-defines-its-limits)**.** EY’s survey of more than 18,000 people across 23 markets shows that AI is moving from giving advice to acting with authority. and found: “*16% of people globally say they have already used AI systems that act on their behalf*”. The report also found: “*people are not seeking innovation for its own sake. They are looking for faster service (57%), lower prices (57%) and products and services that work better (42%),”* with 11% permitting AI to manage finances or banking tasks. Adoption is therefore advancing faster than many businesses and policymakers believe, often despite continuing public concerns about trust. Convenience, lower prices and better services are encouraging people to delegate decisions before governance frameworks are mature. For companies, the risk is not simply adopting AI too slowly, but failing to define what machines may decide, when humans must intervene and who remains accountable. Organisations should establish authority limits, audit trails, escalation rules and oversight now. The competitive winners will not necessarily deploy the most autonomous AI, but the systems customers trust to act safely on their behalf.

[**Tokenised Money for Banks: How Cutting Repo Funding Costs in Half Could Transform Bank Profits and the Wider Economy**](https://tempo.xyz/blog/tokenized-money-for-banks/)**.** Tempo Research’s guide explains how tokenised money is becoming a core bank product as the cash leg of capital markets is pulled onto faster digital rails. GFMA identifies three key use cases, securities settlement, repo/securities finance, and derivatives margin, where assets and collateral already move faster than traditional bank money. DTCC estimates that intraday repo on tokenised rails could cut funding costs roughly in half while reducing liquidity buffers and capital requirements. Tokenised payments could do far more than speed up transactions. By allowing money, collateral and contracts to move together on programmable rails, they can reduce funding costs, release trapped capital and improve productivity. Higher profits and investment may increase corporation tax, employment taxes and VAT, provided activity remains domestically taxable.

[**AI Turns Steel Sales from Guesswork into Real-Time Profit Machine – What This Means for UK GDP**](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/overview/from-instinct-to-real-time-insight-transforming-steel-sales-with-ai?stcr=1060D887C6B543578A3B5DEBDED6BEBB&cid=mgp_opr-eml-alt-dna_mt-mgp-glb--&hlkid=be11f3d8a1b1447b996411c00726dbd9&hdpid=892a0fff-5eec-4e05-9c54-1c0685374add)**.** McKinsey’s case study with ArcelorMittal Brazil shows how a generative AI-powered sales companion transformed traditional steel selling. The tool gave sales teams real-time insights, automated quoting, captured customer conversations, and delivered data-driven recommendations. The pilot produced an estimated R$53 million (~$10.5 million) margin impact while cutting administrative work and increasing customer contacts.

The same approach can extend far beyond steel. Industries most likely to benefit from AI-enabled payments and sales include manufacturing, chemicals, construction materials, energy, logistics, and wholesale trade, sectors where complex B2B pricing, inventory, and credit decisions still rely heavily on human judgment. Then there is the potential of increasing a company’s profits introducing real time digital payments so as goods are delivered to a customer, manufactures get paid real time as opposed to 30-60days later.

For the UK, widespread adoption of AI sales and payment tools in these heavy and intermediate industries could lift productivity, improve working-capital efficiency, and strengthen export competitiveness. Even modest gains across manufacturing and wholesale could add several billion pounds annually to GDP through higher margins, faster transaction cycles, and better capital allocation.

[**Open USD Attacks Circle’s Profit Engine: Will Shared Stablecoin Infrastructure End the Rush to Issue Proprietary Dollars?**](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/30/circle-slides-8-as-stripe-coinbase-and-blackrock-back-rival-stablecoin-network?cd_traffic_source=hub-headline&utm_campaign=190949583-CoinDesk%20Headlines&utm_medium=email&_hsmi=139682974&utm_content=139682974&utm_source=hs_email)Open USD could reshape stablecoin competition by replacing the “launch your own coin” model with shared infrastructure. Backed by more than 140 financial and technology firms, it proposes free minting and redemption while returning most reserve income to participating distributors. That may reduce the incentive for companies to issue dollar stablecoins, because they can gain programmable-dollar functionality, distribution economics and network reach without carrying the regulatory and operational burden themselves. However, banks and retailers may still issue proprietary coins when control over customers, data and settlement is strategically important. Circle must respond by treating USDC as infrastructure rather than merely a token: sharing more reserve income, lowering distribution costs, expanding payment and treasury use cases, strengthening interoperability through Arc, and emphasising regulatory credibility, liquidity and redemption reliability. Open USD threatens Circle’s economics, but USDC’s scale and institutional trust remain defences. Wonder why Coinbase is supporting Open USD given it earnt over $1.3billion last year from its Circle partnership taking roughly **50% to 60%** of Circle's total gross reserve income.

[**The On-chain Cash Bottleneck: Why Tokenised Gilts Need Digital Sterling to Revolutionise Payments.**](https://www.industryevents.com/news/uk-digital-bond-plans-hinge-on-one-missing-piece-onchain-cash-20260723) Britain’s digital gilt pilot (DIGIT) aims to unlock trapped market liquidity and modernise sovereign debt issuance via blockchain infrastructure. However, industry experts caution that the full potential of tokenised bonds hinges on a vital missing component: regulated on-chain cash, such as systemic stablecoins or tokenised bank deposits. Without programmable sterling for atomic Delivery-versus-Payment (DvP) settlement, tokenised debt remains constrained by legacy payment rails. Once on-chain cash arrives, tokenised gilts will inevitably expand beyond standard trading. Driven by their near-zero counterparty risk profile and sovereign backing, tokenised gilts will increasingly function not only as investments but as high-quality liquid collateral and programmable payment media for automated, large-value transactions and agentic cross-border settlement. Furthermore, automated smart contracts will utilise these yield-bearing sovereign tokens to settle B2B obligations, minimizing liquidity drag while maintaining maximum capital efficiency across global financial networks.
