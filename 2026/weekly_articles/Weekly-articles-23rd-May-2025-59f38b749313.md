[Meta is looking to use stablecoins, which corporation will be next?](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/report-meta-considering-using-stablecoins-for-cross-border-payments/)- Meta is reportedly exploring the integration of stablecoins into its platforms, such as Facebook, WhatsApp, and Instagram, to facilitate cross-border payments, particularly for content creators. This initiative comes three years after the company shelved its Diem (formerly Libra) cryptocurrency project due to regulatory challenges. According to reports, Meta is in early discussions with crypto infrastructure firms to support these stablecoin transactions, considering options like USDC and USDT. The primary goal is to streamline small-scale international payouts under $100, by reducing transaction fees and delays associated with traditional banking systems. Leading this renewed effort is Ginger Baker, Meta's Vice President of Product, who brings experience from fintech firms like Plaid and the Stellar Development Foundation. This development reflects a shift in Meta's approach to digital currencies, emphasizing integration with existing stablecoin ecosystems to enhance user experience and expand the creator economy globally.

[Fireblocks 2025 state of stablecoins](https://www.fireblocks.com/report/state-of-stablecoins/?utm_campaign=13473551-The%20State%20of%20Stablecoins%202025&utm_medium=email&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-8P25BI6Iqpd2Vv65Ewdvcz6oqEMUC6aFLl6kLC7DMcVumsut0Rg1IoOZxDW1GdXoZO-YQOrTspgSuu-S4kZs4DNtOFU00TZ-eSjZQed5iKaTytc-M&_hsmi=362194835&utm_content=362194835&utm_source=hs_email) – We are seeing a rapid integration of stablecoins into global financial systems. In 2024, stablecoins constituted nearly half of all transaction volumes on the Fireblocks platform, underscoring their growing role in modernising payment infrastructures. Over 300 banks and payment providers are now leveraging stablecoins for faster, cost-effective, and 24/7 cross-border transactions, particularly in emerging markets like Latin America and Africa. The report identifies speed as the primary benefit of stablecoins, with 48% of institutions citing it as a top advantage, followed by cost savings and continuous availability. A significant 86% of respondents believe their infrastructure is ready for large-scale stablecoin adoption, and 90% are actively exploring or implementing stablecoin solutions. Regionally, Latin America leads in real-world stablecoin usage, with 71% utilizing them for cross-border payments. In Asia, 49% view market expansion as the primary driver for stablecoin adoption. North American firms see regulation as a catalyst, with 88% considering it a green light for adoption, while in Europe, 42% cite legacy system risks and 37% demand safer payment rails. The report concludes that enterprise-grade infrastructure is crucial for scaling stablecoin payments, with 41% emphasizing speed and 34% highlighting compliance as non-negotiable factors. Security remains a pivotal concern, as 36% believe enhanced protection would significantly boost adoption. Overall, the report portrays stablecoins as transitioning from experimental tools to essential components of the global financial ecosystem, driven by technological readiness, regulatory developments, and the demand for more efficient payment solutions.

[Circle’s founder building an AI native bank](https://fortune.com/crypto/2025/05/20/sean-neville-catena-labs-18-million-a16z-breyer-circle-coinbase-tom-brady/) – Could the future of finance belong not to banks or humans, but to **autonomous AI agents**. The founder of stablecoin Circle, Neville, envisions a world where bots not people negotiate, authorise, and execute programmable payments. In this model, your financial advisor isn’t a person, but an algorithm embedded across the interfaces you already use. Neville is creating a “AI-native bank,” one designed not to digitize legacy systems, but to **replace them entirely**. Identity, payments, compliance all reimagined for a machine-to-machine economy. Blockchain and stablecoins will be tools in this future, but not the centre of it. The true core is **autonomous intelligence negotiating trust, risk, and value in real time.** It’s a radical idea: that finance itself will no longer be human-facing, but **machine-mediated**, where value moves through invisible protocols governed by code, not clerks. In this world, the customer is a node, the advisor is synthetic, and trust isn’t spoken, it’s verified. Clearly for this to happen we will need digital money to enable programmable payments.

[Swiss Bankers Association alive to the need for Switzerland to have stablecoins but concerned about narrow banking](https://www.swissbanking.ch/en/media-politics/news/a-stablecoin-can-be-a-valuable-addition-under-clear-conditions?TimeFrameDays=1&ChartTimeFrameDays=7) - The Swiss Bankers Association (SBA) has released a comprehensive report analysing the potential role of stablecoins within Switzerland's financial ecosystem. The report posits that, under stringent conditions, a Swiss franc-denominated stablecoin could bolster the nation's digital economy by providing efficient, low-cost, and secure payment options. Such a stablecoin, ideally issued by regulated banks, would need to be backed by high-quality liquid assets to ensure value stability and public trust. The SBA highlights several benefits of introducing a stablecoin, including streamlined payment processes, reduced transaction times and costs, and enhanced support for digital asset trading and decentralized financial services. These advantages could position Switzerland as a global leader in financial innovation. However, the report also cautions against potential risks, notably the threat of disintermediation, where widespread adoption of stablecoins might diminish banks' traditional roles, potentially impacting their ability to extend credit and affecting monetary policy effectiveness.

[Coinbase looks to acquire Circle -](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/coinbase-and-ripple-vie-for-usdc-stablecoin-issuer-circle-report/) The bidding war between Coinbase and Ripple for Circle signals more than just a corporate acquisition. It marks a high-stakes battle for the financial plumbing of the digital US$. As stablecoins emerge as core infrastructure in the new internet-native economy, controlling their issuance isn’t just about revenue it’s about owning the rails of programmable money. Coinbase earnt over $920billion for Circle through a revenue-sharing agreement in 2024, sees consolidation as a path to vertical integration. Ripple on the other hand, envisions USDC as a potential complement or replacement for its native XRP in regulated cross-border payments. Either way, the winner doesn’t just gain a balance sheet boost—they gain influence over how dollars move, settle, and scale across the decentralised economy. What’s at stake isn’t just Circle’s profits it’s the architecture of a future where central banks, FinTechs, and AI agents may all transact in tokenised fiat. The question now is: Will stablecoins serve platforms, or become the platform themselves? Whoever controls USDC may help answer that. The funny thing is if interest rates fall because of the revenue share deals Circle has entered into it could well become a loss-making business. Circle isn’t just a tech company, but **an** [interest rate play](https://fortune.com/crypto/2025/04/01/crypto-giant-circle-just-filed-for-an-ipo-here-are-5-key-takeaways/) **wrapped in a stablecoin brand**, with a future dependant on the Fed’s monetary policy.
