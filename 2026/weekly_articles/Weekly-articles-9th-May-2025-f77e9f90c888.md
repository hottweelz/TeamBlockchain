1. [The Stablecoin Wars](https://www.forbes.com/sites/christiancatalini/2025/04/29/the-stablecoin-wars/) **-** The stablecoin wars are no longer a futuristic fantasy they’re unfolding now, and the outcome will shape the next generation of digital finance. A swirl of headlines PayPal working with Coinbase, Tether muscling in via SoftBank, Stripe and Visa teasing new plays masks a deeper power struggle: not over who mints the best stablecoin, but who owns the infrastructure through which money *actually moves*. The real prize lies in embedding stablecoins into global payments, banking rails, and consumer platforms before banks, FinTechs, or Big Tech close the gate. Stablecoins are splitting into two roles almost in real time mediums of exchange for cross-border payments and durable stores of value in inflation-ridden economies. But as tokenised Treasuries and smart contract enabled finance to mature, the store-of-value space is getting crowded. What we are seeing is a collision between software, regulation, banking muscle, and distribution scale. In this race, winners will be those whoever owns the rails, the checkout flows, and the wallets. Stablecoins, ironically, may fade into the background just like electricity reliable, essential, and invisible. The question is: who gets to control the plugs? Would you trust a coin minted by Amazon or Meta over your local bank?

[Can collaboration solve global payments challenges?](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2025/inside-u-s-banks-push-to-simplify-global-payments-in-an-era-of-co-opetition/) In the digital-first era, the path from starting a business to scaling it globally is no longer linear and neither are the expectations placed on payments and financial services providers, thriving in today's service economy requires more than the ability to process transactions. It demands a layered ecosystem of adaptable, value-added tools that evolve in lockstep with the ever-changing needs of businesses and consumers. What’s emerging is a new model of **“financial co-evolution”**—where banks, FinTechs, and platforms shift from siloed competitors to collaborative enablers. This co-opetition allows businesses to assemble customised financial stacks, combining tokenisation, fraud detection, embedded finance, loyalty tools, and cross-border FX capabilities from a marketplace of interoperable providers. The bank of the future may not own all the tools—but it must know how to orchestrate them. At the core of this evolution is the pursuit of **frictionless commerce**, not just for consumers at checkout but for enterprises behind the scenes. This means building payments infrastructure that can flex with scale, defend against increasing fraud threats, and accommodate real-time demands without sacrificing security. Tools such as tokenised identity, embedded point-of-sale financing, and multi-currency management aren’t simply upgrades—they are essential rails for survival in an economy that never sleeps. No longer are banks merely offering accounts—they are becoming operating systems for commerce. Wallets are morphing into dynamic, data-rich identity hubs. Payments are becoming **programmable and contextual**. And the rise of borderless transactions, amplified by digital wallets and stablecoins, is pushing providers to reimagine not just how money moves, but **who controls the rails. In a rapidly tokenised, AI-enabled economy where consumer expectations shift in milliseconds, can financial institutions transition from serving users to anticipating them—and will the true competitive advantage lie not in what you offer, but how intuitively you deliver it?**
3. [Are business and the law misunderstanding blockchain?](https://swenldn.substack.com/p/symbol-without-sense?triedRedirect=true) Applying existing laws to blockchain-powered platforms and tokenised assets without adapting to blockchain’s decentralised could well hold back mass adoption of blockchain technology. Do we need to rebuild legal understanding from the ground up, starting with how decentralized systems truly function? For example, **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** is being **symbolically applied** to blockchain systems in ways that often **miss the mark.** R**egulators need to reassess the way they are treating blockchains like conventional IT infrastructure,** rather than appreciating its foundational difference—**decentralisation.** Blockchain is **a consensus-driven, append-only ledger,** not a mutable, user-centric database. The assumption that data on blockchain can be “owned,” “controlled,” or “deleted” like it would be in a CRM system leads to a **disconnect between legal theory and technical reality.** Regulators often ignore—or sidestep—the fact that decentralization is a **design choice**, not a flaw. Attempts to impose centralized compliance obligations onto a decentralised network create **regulatory friction** that risks stifling innovation.
4. [Are stablecoins set to replace Eurodollars](https://www.coindesk.com/opinion/2025/05/06/stabledollars-the-third-act-of-dollar-reinvention)-

![A white text with black text  AI-generated content may be incorrect.](data:image/png;base64...)

The U.S. dollar is undergoing a profound reinvention one that may determine whether American financial leadership extends into the digital age or quietly erodes. Arguably there have been three transformation for the US Dollar. 1.Eurodollars, which moved dollars offshore in the 1950s; to 2.Petrodollars, which anchored global energy markets to U.S. currency in the 1970s; and now to 3. Stabledollars that represent the third great act of dollar hegemony. Unlike their predecessors, Stabledollars operate not through bank intermediaries or geopolitical arrangements, but through decentralised networks and smart contracts. They’ve already reached a $230 billion supply and, on many days, settle more value than legacy giants like PayPal or Western Union. Merchants in Lagos, hedge funds in Singapore, and gig workers in Bogotá are bypassing traditional banks, avoiding currency slippage, and transacting instantly using digital dollars. The dollar is becoming a kind of monetary API—programmable, always-on, and borderless. Yet with this transformation come serious questions. Who conducts monetary policy when much of the offshore float lives in smart contracts? What happens when access to dollar liquidity is gated not by banks, but by opaque code or blacklists? How will other powers respond if global commerce increasingly runs on a rails-level dependency governed by U.S.-regulated assets? These are not abstractions. The U.S. Treasury already models a $2 trillion stablecoin market by 2028—large enough to rival the Eurodollar system of the early 1990s. And unlike Eurodollars, which settled slowly and opaquely, stablecoins settle in seconds and transparently hold reserves in short-term U.S. debt. Foreigners are, in effect, holding digitized T-bills—offering the U.S. a strategic lever it cannot afford to ignore. This would not only solidify the dollar’s digital dominance but also build a regulatory moat no central bank digital currency from China or otherwise could cross. Stablecoins, in other words, are not simply digital assets. They are the next evolution of monetary power, and the question is whether the United States will steward the system it inadvertently birthed or be left chasing it.

[Visa invests in stablecoin startup BVNK](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danielwebber/2025/05/06/visa-invests-in-bvnk-in-powerful-validation-of-stablecoins-future/) – Yet more evidence of how seriously the credit/debit card companies are taking stablecoins Visa’s investment in BVNK is more than just another fintech partnership it’s a signal that the world’s largest payments network sees **stablecoins as the next global financial infrastructure.** BVNK abstracts the blockchain’s complexity, offering seamless APIs that allow businesses to settle instantly in digital dollars, bypassing the friction, fees, and delays of correspondent banking. What SWIFT was to the 20th-century payments ecosystem, **stablecoin infrastructure may become for the digital century** but faster, programmable, and always on. Currently most countries have e-Money licence what surely has to come are D-Money licences and the revolution in digital money is being rolled out by API calls inside payroll platforms, acquirers, and FinTech companies. **Stablecoins are not the end of banking. They’re the end of banking inefficiency.** Visa has seen the future. It's programmable, dollar-denominated, and settling in seconds. The only question left is: **Who else will own the rails?**
