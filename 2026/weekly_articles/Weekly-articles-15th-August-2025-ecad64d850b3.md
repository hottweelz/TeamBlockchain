**Weekly articles 15th August 2025**

[**1. The role of blockchain technology for real estate-**](https://tech.realtor/2025/08/04/the-future-of-real-estate-is-on-the-blockchain-why-real-estate-pros-should-pay-attention/)  Blockchain is poised to redefine real estate by transforming transactions from paperwork-heavy, delay-prone processes into secure, automated, and transparent workflows. Blockchain technology offers the potential to slash closing times, reduce fraud, and lower transaction costs by up to 30%, according to Deloitte, while enhancing trust through tamper-proof property records. Perhaps the most disruptive promise lies in tokenisation, which fractionalises property ownership into digital tokens tradable on secure platforms. This opens the door for smaller-scale investors to access real estate markets and build equity without full mortgages, potentially easing barriers to ownership in an era of housing unaffordability. Blockchains could help streamline escrow, title transfers, and cross-border property transactions, allowing real estate to move at the speed of the internet.

Yet adoption faces hurdles. Regulatory frameworks remain in flux, integration with legacy MLS and title systems is complex, and a knowledge gap persists among professionals and consumers. Like the early days of e-signatures, these challenges are more transitional than terminal, but they require education and openness from industry players. Crucially, blockchains won’t erase the human element trust, negotiation, and client guidance remain irreplaceable. Instead, roles will evolve, title officers may pivot toward risk analysis, escrow agents toward digital custody, and agents toward tech-savvy advisors who navigate clients through the blockchain-enabled marketplace.

[**2. Trump’s proposed Clarity act opens the door to digital assets and a Wall Street overhaul**](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/08/08/trump-crypto-wall-street-overhaul-00499464?utm_source=chatgpt.com) **-** Tokenised stocks, bonds real estate, commodities etc are shaping up to be the next big battleground between FinTech upstarts such as Coinbase, Robinhood, and Kraken, and Wall Street stalwarts such as Citadel and BlackRock. These blockchain-based versions of equities and other assets promise 24/7 global trading, lower costs, and faster settlement, but also challenge the decades-old regulatory framework underpinning markets. With Trump’s pro-digital asset stance and allies in top regulatory posts, the industry sees an opening to rewrite securities trading rules. TradFi warns that creating “loopholes” for tokenised assets could splinter markets and undermine investor protections, while crypto firms argue tokenization is inevitable and will eventually make traditional rails obsolete.

While there are concerns that tokenisation will fragment markets and potentially reduce liquidity, others believe that tokenisati9n will enable new investors in Asia, Africa and Latin America to again greater access to financial markets and this increase trading volumes and liquidity. The fight hinges on whether the SEC applies existing stock rules to tokenised versions or crafts new regulations either preserving Wall Street’s dominance or opening the door to a parallel, blockchain-driven securities markets. Stablecoins’ recent legislative win shows how quickly the balance of power can shift, but history suggests legacy finance may adapt, invest, and ultimately co-opt the disruption.

[**3. Structured Data at the Centre of CFO’s Forecasting Revolution**](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2025/how-better-structured-data-is-upending-the-traditional-cfo-playbook/) **-** Data’s true value in finance no longer lies in mere accumulation, but in refinement*.* Like crude oil transformed into high-octane fuel, raw figures when standardised, contextualised, and delivered in real time are powering a new breed of CFO: less ledger-keeper, more strategic operator. Clean, structured data integrated across payments, procurement, and supply chains enables unified dashboards that connect shipment statuses directly to working capital forecasts. The shift is profound finance is moving from reactive scorekeeping toproactive orchestration, where AI predicts future cash flow and tariff-induced uncertainty is met with agile pricing, smarter contracts, and tighter partner coordination. With centralised data lakes and finance-specific ML models, CFOs can forecast disruptions, optimise capital deployment, and even influence logistics routing. In a volatile world, data mastery is not just a competitive edge it’s the foundation of financial resilience.

[**4. Citibank roles out its blockchain- powered treasury management service**](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/payoneer-adopts-citi-blockchain-technology-for-real-time-treasury-transfers/) - Payoneer teaming up with Citi to run blockchain-enabled treasury transfers through Citi Token. This offers an alternative replacing traditional correspondent banking hops with tokenised deposits on Citi’s private blockchain, cross-border settlements that once took days can happen in minutes, with near-instant liquidity visibility for corporates. For Payoneer’s global SME clients, it means faster payouts, fewer intermediaries, and reduced FX friction. For Citi, it’s a live demonstration that tier-one banks can wield blockchain as infrastructure, not hype.

The deeper implication? If tokenised cash transfers become standard for treasury flows, banks stop being the bottleneck and start being the blockchain blurring the line between stablecoins, bank money, and central bank digital currencies. This could quietly shift the competitive ground under SWIFT, stablecoin issuers, and even the Fed’s settlement rails.

**5.** [**The fight against bank fraud. Tide removes card details from its cards**](https://thepaypers.com/fraud-and-fincrime/news/tide-to-remove-printed-numbers-on-cards-to-reduce-fraud)- In the battle against one of the fastest-growing threats in finance: card-not-present fraud Tide is removing card details. With UK losses from CNP fraud hitting nearly £400 million in 2024 (up 11% year-on-year), printed card details have become an open invitation for online theft, mail interception, and shoulder surfing. Partnering with Mastercard to launch numberless business cards, Tide effectively severs the link between a stolen physical card and the ability to use it for unauthorised transactions. Card data will now be accessed securely through the app, enabling tokenised payments and aligning with Mastercard’s ambitious goal to eliminate manual card entry in Europe by 2030. This is a step toward a cardless payments future where physical cards become nothing more than a branded authentication device. The decision to extend card life from three to four years adds an environmental layer to the innovation, cutting plastic waste while lowering replacement costs.

If adopted widely, numberless cards could accelerate the decline of traditional card fraud, shift more transactions toward tokenisation, and even reshape how we think about the physical payment card’s role in the digital economy. For banks and FinTechs still clinging to embossed numbers, Tide’s move is a reminder that the battle against fraud is as much about redesigning the payment experience as it is about patching vulnerabilities.

[**6. Tech enabled consumer credit**](https://www.forbes.com/sites/boazsobrado/2025/08/14/the-golden-age-of-tech-enabled-consumer-credit/)- Traditional lenders should be nervous Zilch’s credit revenue rocketed 96% to £75.7 million, while Chime’s surged 113% to $162 million in Q2 2025, even as U.S. household debt hit $18.39 trillion and 4.4% of it slid into delinquency. These FinTechs are thriving in a market where banks are weighed down by old models, surging student loan delinquencies, and eroding consumer trust. They’re winning on three fronts: **data, transparency, and AI-driven efficiency**. Zilch processes £1.9 billion in annual transaction volume; Chime handles $32.4 billion quarterly underwriting with real-time spending data instead of blunt FICO scores. Their pricing models reject compounding interest traps, replacing them with flat, upfront fees. Zilch claims UK customers have saved £750 million in interest and fees; Chime’s SpotMe feature is so trusted that users tip their bank.

Operationally, AI is rewriting the economics of small-dollar lending. Zilch keeps customer service costs flat despite growth; Chime’s GenAI voice bot doubled satisfaction scores and slashed acquisition costs. That efficiency lets them profitably serve customers banks avoid.

Both are converging from opposite ends Zilch from credit toward full-service finance, Chime from payments into lending meeting in the sweet spot of payments data + customer trust + intelligent credit.

[**7. AI powered digital payments-**](https://www.forbes.com/sites/digital-assets/2025/08/14/ai-growth-fueled-by-blockchaincom-privacy-and-tether-payments/)AI and digital payments offer a powerful attraction where abundance meets scarcity, and creation meets verification. AI generates infinite possibilities from data; blockchains locks those possibilities into trust, provenance, and value. That duality creator and protector is turning into a design principle for the next wave of Web3. For example, Blockchain.com’s offer a privacy-centric AI for 90M wallets, designed to give users real-time insights, transaction analysis, and coding support all without harvesting personal data. By embedding intelligence directly into the tools people already trust with their money. The bet is simple: make the wallet smarter, keep the user’s data sovereign, and loyalty follows. Meanwhile Tether’s play is **infrastructure focused.** Its decentralised AI let autonomous agents transact directly in USDT or Bitcoin, no central servers, no API choke points. That’s the scaffolding for a machine-to-machine economy where AI agents pay each other for compute, bandwidth, or data without human oversight.

Both strategies aim at the same convergence, where intelligence is baked into the rails of finance, not bolted on. For Blockchain.com, the challenge is monetising without betraying its privacy promise. For Tether, it’s building a developer community that actually ships on its new rails. 2025 could be the year it becomes infrastructure. What’s being built now will decide whether AI-driven blockchains empower users or just recreate the old power structures with smarter code.
