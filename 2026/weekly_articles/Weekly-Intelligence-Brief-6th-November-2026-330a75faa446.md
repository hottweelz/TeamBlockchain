**Weekly Intelligence Brief 6th November 2026**

**Tokenisation is moving from experimentation into core financial infrastructure, spanning $7.6 trillion of monthly repo transactions, digital government bonds, 24/7 settlement, tokenised equities and emerging-market debt. Yet as AI agents begin selecting assets and initiating payments, the decisive questions will concern interoperability, liquidity, legal ownership and who is liable when automated decisions go wrong.**

[**Broadridge’s $7.6 Trillion Repo Platform Shows Tokenization at Scale**](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/broadridge-financial-distributed-ledger-repo-processes-7-6-trillion-in-october-ce785fddde8cf727)**.** Broadridge says its Distributed Ledger Repo platform processed $7.6 trillion in October, averaging $345 billion daily. That scale makes tokenization a component of securities finance rather than a laboratory exercise. Shared records can reduce reconciliation, settlement failures and duplication across repo participants. AI could accelerate adoption by optimising collateral selection, forecasting margin needs and routing transactions across tokenized assets. It could also create correlated behaviour if many institutions use models to move collateral simultaneously.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** For banks and dealers the combination promises lower operating costs and more efficient balance-sheet use, while demanding stronger model governance and real-time controls. The economy benefits when funding markets operate reliably and collateral circulates efficiently, lowering financing costs. However, greater automation can make liquidity shocks travel faster. Tokenized repo succeeds only if legal finality, default procedures, cyber resilience and human intervention remain robust during stressed markets.

[**Britain’s Digital Gilt Will Test Whether Tokenization Can Escape Platform Silos**](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/uk-moves-closer-to-launching-first-digitally-native-government-bond)**.** HM Treasury has appointed Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, Morgan Stanley, NatWest and RBC Capital Markets to support DIGIT, Britain’s first digitally native government bond, expected by the first quarter of 2027. Regulatory approval for a link between HSBC and LSEG digital depositories will let investors access the gilt through either platform. That connection matters because tokenization without interoperability merely creates smaller pools of liquidity.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** For regulated companies, DIGIT offers experience in documentation, bookbuilding, custody and settlement of sovereign securities on distributed ledgers. It could eventually create high-quality digital collateral for automated treasury and AI-driven investment systems. The wider economy may benefit from faster settlement, improved collateral mobility and a UK digital-finance sector. Yet one pilot will not transform gilt markets. Success depends on competitive pricing, broad investor participation, legal certainty and integration with existing repo, clearing and monetary-policy infrastructure. **Could we see the DIGIT being used as a form of payment and so bypassing the need for a GBP stablecoin?**

[**Hong Kong Makes Tokenized Central Bank Settlement Available 24/7**](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2026/11/20261102-5/)**.** The HKMA has upgraded Project Ensemble so banks can settle tokenized-deposit transactions continuously using tokenized central bank money. Participation has expanded to 12 banks and 20 pioneers, covering corporate treasury and tokenized money-market-fund activity. The upgrade removes dependence on conventional settlement hours and provides a safer interbank cash leg for always-on digital assets.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** For banks, asset managers and multinational companies, this could improve liquidity management, reduce prefunding and support continuous trading. It also creates financial infrastructure that AI agents could use to allocate cash or rebalance tokenized portfolios under mandates. Economically, faster circulation of collateral and cash may improve capital efficiency and strengthen Hong Kong’s competitiveness. However, continuous settlement transfers operational demands into nights and weekends. Institutions will need resilient staffing, cyber controls and liquidity safeguards. Always-on markets are useful only if risk management, redemption capacity and support are equally available. How soon before other countries follow Hong Kong and offer the same?

[**AI Agents Could Trigger 5% of Cross-Border Payments, But Who Holds the Liability?**](https://www.pymnts.com/news/payments-innovation/2026/ai-agents-could-control-5percent-of-cross-border-payments-who-controls-them/?utm_source=Sailthru&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Main%20NL%2011/03&utm_term=Main%20Newsletter)Mural CEO Sinclair Toffa predicts AI agents could initiate 5% of cross-border payments within a year as artificial intelligence, stablecoins and real-time networks converge. The central question is not whether agents can move money, but who authorizes them, limits their discretion and bears losses when they make a mistake. Mural advocates agent-specific accounts, permissions, embedded compliance and transaction-level audit trails connecting banks, wallets and payment networks.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** This could automate supplier payments, payroll, foreign-exchange decisions and reconciliation while reducing conversion costs, in markets where dollar accounts are scarce. Banks and payment providers risk becoming invisible infrastructure unless they control the permissioning layer. The wider economy could benefit from cheaper trade, faster working-capital cycles and broader dollar access. Yet unclear liability, fraud and automated errors could transmit losses. Agentic payments will scale only when delegated authority becomes as programmable as money itself.

[**Europe’s €1 Billion Digital Bond Bridges Blockchain and Central Bank Money**](https://www.esm.europa.eu/articles-and-op-eds/strengthening-europes-financial-infrastructure-digital-age?utm_source=chatgpt.com)**.** The European Stability Mechanism has explained why its €1 billion digitally native bond, settled through the Eurosystem’s Pontes platform, matters beyond an issuance. Clearstream’s D7 ledger records the security while central bank money settles the cash leg, and the bond enters conventional infrastructure, preserving liquidity and collateral eligibility.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** For issuers and investors, this hybrid design lowers the operational barrier to testing tokenization without sacrificing protections or market access. Banks, custodians and trading venues gain a blueprint for connecting digital issuance with existing systems. The economic prize is a more integrated European capital market and a euro-denominated settlement alternative to infrastructure developed elsewhere. However, the transaction settled at T+5 to remain familiar, demonstrating that technology alone does not guarantee faster markets. Europe must standardise legal treatment, interoperability and operational processes before digital bonds can reduce costs or enhance the euro’s role.

[**NYSE Adds Shareholder Rights to Its Tokenized-Equities Ambition**](https://www.broadridge.com/press-release/2026/broadridge-to-bring-onchain-governance-and-digital-transfer-agency)**.** Broadridge will provide on-chain shareholder governance and transfer-agency services for the New York Stock Exchange’s tokenized-securities platform, subject to regulatory approval. Smart contracts will connect tokenized shares with meeting agendas, voting rules and results, while wallet authentication will establish voting entitlements. Investors could vote through several channels. This tackles a problem: trading a token is straightforward, but maintaining accurate ownership records and corporate rights is harder.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** For issuers, the arrangement could simplify proxy distribution, investor communications and share administration. Transfer agents and brokers can modernize services rather than disappear. The wider economy may benefit if lower administration costs encourage ownership and efficient capital formation. Yet digital governance can magnify errors if identity records, smart contracts or voting rules are wrong. Recognition, privacy protection and reconciliation with registers will determine whether tokenized equities represent genuine shares or attractive wrappers.

[**Schroders Takes Emerging-Market Debt On-chain, Giving AI More Than Treasury Bills**](https://centrifuge.io/blog/schroders-centrifuge)**.** Schroders and Centrifuge have brought the Schroders Emerging Market Bond strategy onto Solana, extending tokenization beyond money-market funds and government bills. The product offers tokenized exposure to a managed portfolio spanning sovereign, corporate and local-currency debt. For asset managers, public-blockchain distribution can widen access, enable programmable subscriptions and let fund interests interact with collateral and portfolio applications. AI could improve country-risk monitoring, currency analysis and allocation, making a complex asset class easier to navigate. However, automated recommendations may disguise political, liquidity and exchange-rate risks that require human judgment.

**Impact on business and the wider economy:** If emerging-market borrowers reach new investors and capital becomes diversified. Yet tokenization cannot make underlying bonds continuously liquid or eliminate settlement restrictions in local markets. Adoption will depend on accurate valuations, investor suitability controls and ensuring that always-on distribution does not imply always-available liquidity.
