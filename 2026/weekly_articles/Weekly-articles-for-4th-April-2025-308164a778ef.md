**Weekly articles 4th April 2025**

1. [How AI is impacting payments](https://www.pymnts.com/accounts-payable/2025/data-silos-meet-genai-the-next-frontier-for-cfos-seeking-ap-excellence/) - Despite decades of innovation, accounts payable (AP) remains stuck in the past—bogged down by paper invoices, manual data entry, and error correction. For many enterprises, automation has yet to displace the inefficiencies at the heart of AP. CFOs are standing at a digital crossroads—modernising AP with AI and automation while ensuring integration with legacy banking systems doesn’t compromise stability. Despite years of talk about innovation, 84% of AP tasks remain manual. But that’s changing. AI—especially generative AI—is making sense of unstructured invoice data, enabling 99.5% capture accuracy with minimal human input. Combined with real-time payments now possible using blockchain -powered programmable payments and predictive analytics, AP is evolving into a strategic lever for cash flow optimisation, supplier management, and fraud prevention. Will AI deliver true transformation or just tinkers at the edges. To unlock AI’s true potential, CFOs must pair the right tools with the right partners—and reimagine AP not as a cost centre, but as a catalyst for competitive advantage.
2. [Standard Chartered: Can Stablecoins Fill Payment Gap?-](https://fintechmagazine.com/articles/standard-chartered-can-stablecoins-fill-payment-gap) Standard Chartered’s digital asset arm, Zodia Markets, reports that stablecoins now process **$425 billion in monthly transactions**, filling gaps left by outdated cross-border payment systems. As G20-led reforms stall and traditional correspondent banking declines, stablecoins—primarily on Ethereum and Tron—have become a viable alternative, particularly in emerging markets and volatile currency regions. Stablecoins are proving effective for FX, remittances, and after-hours settlement. Regulatory clarity is expected in early 2025, especially under the Trump administration and hopefully buy the end of 2025 in the UK too. Meanwhile, real-world test trades using stablecoins demonstrate how programmable payments can overcome T+1 equity settlement challenges and support 24/7 liquidity.

4. [Crypto crime how big is it?](https://go.chainalysis.com/2025-Crypto-Crime-Report.html) A recent report from Chain analysis looked at the size of cryptocurrency crime and the results would appear to be not as large as many believe to be the case. United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime estimates up to [5% of global GDP](https://www.unodc.org/unodc/en/money-laundering/globalization.html) is laundered each year, maybe as much as $2 trillion. A recent report from Chain analysis estimates crypto hacks and heist to be 0.7% of transactions compared to up to 5%. Of note is historically the preferred crypto for nefarious activities had been Bitcoin potentially because it has the biggest and most liquid. However, Chain analysis reported that stablecoins have now taken over as the cryptos of choice, could this be due to the easy way it is to convert them into cash or the fact that criminals typically keep them exposed to US$ until then wish to use their proceeds of crime?

[Stablecoin surge: Here’s why reserve-backed cryptocurrencies are on the rise.](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/03/stablecoins-cryptocurrency-on-rise-financial-systems/) The World Economic Forum reported: “*The use of stablecoins has increased in recent years with the average supply of stablecoins in circulation increasing roughly 28% year-over-year. Total transfer volume, meanwhile, hit $27.6 trillion last year, surpassing the combined volume of Visa and Mastercard transactions in 2024*”. What’s driving this surge? Unlike volatile cryptocurrencies, stablecoins offer **price stability**—making them ideal for **cross-border payments, remittances, and savings**. The appeal is global: from PayPal’s PYUSD to Circle’s euro-pegged EURC, stablecoins are expanding across currencies and geographies. Even banks are entering the arena. Standard Chartered’s **HKD-pegged stablecoin** is just the latest signal that traditional finance is waking up to blockchain’s efficiency and bank of America is looking to launch a stablecoin. With over a **billion unbanked people globally**, could stablecoins finally bridge the inclusion gap—or will questions of **transparency and trust**, especially around USDT’s reserves, hinder their promise?
6. [Larry Fink 2025 letter to shareholders talks about unlocking the opportunities in private equities, tokenisation and Europe](https://www.marketsmedia.com/larry-finks-annual-investor-letter-unlocking-private-markets/)- Fink: “*Decentralized finance is an extraordinary innovation… But that same innovation may undermine America’s economic advantage.*” Larry Fink’s 2025 annual letter reads less like a typical investor update and more like a provocation—a challenge to the foundations of the current financial order. What happens when the world stops believing in the dollar? With national debt ballooning, interest payments outpacing defence spending, and confidence in the fiscal trajectory of the United States fading, the stage is being set for alternatives—namely, Bitcoin and other digital assets—to claim territory once thought unshakably American. Fink sees in Bitcoin not just a hedge or speculative asset, but a signal: that capital is seeking speed, transparency, and a new kind of trust. At the heart of his vision is tokenisation—not as a buzzword, but as a foundational shift in how value moves. If SWIFT is the postal service, then tokenization is instant messaging. Assets, from bonds to real estate, could become programmable and portable, tradeable 24/7, and available in fractional form to a far wider swath of the population. The implication is profound: financial inclusion isn’t just a moral imperative—it may become a competitive edge. But the promise of tokenisation collides with the friction of identity. Fink points to India’s Aadhaar system as a proof point that secure digital identity is not only possible but essential. Without it, the full realisation of tokenised capital markets remains out of reach.

Latin America is becoming a critical testbed for the future of cross-border payments. A surge in digital adoption—fuelled by fintech innovation, mobile penetration, and in some cases, government-led modernization—has opened the door to a new wave of alternative payment methods. These are transforming everything from ecommerce and SME trade to freelance gig economy payouts and remittances.

But doing business in Latin America is not without its challenges. Macroeconomic instability, currency volatility, and fragmented regulatory frameworks threaten to undercut efforts to build faster, more affordable, and transparent payment rails.
