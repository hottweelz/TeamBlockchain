**Weekly articles 20th June 2025**

**1.** [**BNP’s investment themes for 2025**](https://www.bnpparibas-am.com/en/thematics-barometer/) **-** BNP Paribas Asset Management’s 2025 Thematics Barometer is based on discussions with 180 institutions and found Overall, 71% of investors in private markets have thematic positions. Private markets are no longer niche they’re becoming core to how investors pursue thematic opportunities like AI, clean energy, and healthcare. Institutional focus is pivoting toward private market exposure, particularly in North America and Europe, where over 20% allocations are becoming the norm. Asian investors, by contrast, are doubling down on traditional equities, perhaps reflecting differing regulatory or liquidity preferences.

AI’s adoption in finance isn’t just speculative it’s already credited with boosting productivity by 30%, and 79% of respondents now hold AI-themed investments. On the sustainability front, European investors are pushing forward, with 63% prioritizing renewables and clean energy, reinforcing ESG’s central role in the next wave of thematic growth.

Thematic investing is becoming the convergence point of long-term conviction, risk-adjusted returns, and innovation exposure. And private markets once the domain of niche venture funds are emerging as the primary vehicles to ride these megatrends. We're witnessing the mainstreaming of disruption.

**2.** [Gen Z is rewriting the rules of banking](https://www.pymnts.com/supply-chain/2025/importers-face-mounting-tariff-bills-from-trade-policies/). With $12 trillion in projected spending power by 2030 and $84 trillion in assets by 2045 Gen Z i.e. born between 1990 and 2010 isn’t just the next generation of banking customers but the generation that will redefine what banking means. Born into a world of instant access, social awareness, and economic uncertainty, they demand more than apps and flashy branding. They expect financial institutions to align with their values, anticipate their needs, and integrate seamlessly into their digital lives. The traditional playbook doesn’t work. Gen Z is sceptical of legacy systems, are not loyal so will switch brands at the push of an App, allergic to friction, and intolerant of opacity. They trust experiences over institutions, and they’re not afraid to walk away from brands that don’t deliver relevance or purpose. Winning their loyalty isn’t about digitising old models; it’s about reimagining what financial empowerment looks like in a world where attention is scarce, and expectations are sky-high.

It reminds me of that old saying and banks need to adhere to it indeed we all do- “*Two ears one mouth, use them in this ratio*”. Banks that succeed won’t be the ones that shout the loudest but those that listen the closest, build trust early, educate meaningfully, and evolve with Gen Z’s fluid financial journeys. The question isn’t whether Gen Z will change banking it’s whether banks are ready to change with them and embrace digital services and programmable payments?

[APIs can enable programmable small payments](https://thepaypers.com/interviews/money2020-europe-2025-silverflow-and-bolt-direct-card-network-connectivity--1274092?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Daily+2025-06-16+Daily-Newsletter-Payments). APIs can help **bypass much of the traditional banking infrastructure,** especially when paired with modern payment networks and fintech platforms. **Silverflow and Bolt have joined forces to revolutionise ride-hailing payments through direct card network integration.** Announced at Money20/20, the partnership aims to cut costs, streamline infrastructure, and boost performance across Bolt’s 55+ markets. Silverflow’s API-based platform connects directly to networks like Visa and Mastercard, eliminating the need for multiple intermediaries and improving data control. For Bolt operating on tight margins and small-ticket transactions this offers greater control over payment flows and a path to faster market expansion. The roadmap includes rolling out across key regions and piloting innovations like tap-to-phone payments, which turn drivers’ phones into secure payment terminals.

By rethinking the traditional payments stack, Silverflow and Bolt are setting a new benchmark for operational efficiency and real-time innovation in global mobility with no use of CBDCs, stablecoins or tokenised deposits. Unfortunately most banks struggle to accept API instructions to trigger payments currently.

Shoppers can now pay by simply saying “Rokid, pay 10 RMB,” with payments completed in seconds dramatically faster than traditional phone-based QR methods. The glasses don’t just streamline checkout they introduce **contextual intelligence**, offering real-time overlays for price comparisons, deals, or loyalty points before payment. With voice controls and [haptic feedback](https://builtin.com/hardware/haptic-technology#:~:text=Metaverse,support%20more%20immersive%20training%20simulations.), the system also enhances **accessibility**, helping users with mobility challenges engage in commerce more independently. AR payments are pushing the frontier of FinTech, evolving from smartphone wallets to fully immersive, wearable interfaces. This shift empowers consumers with **greater speed, context, and choice** at the point of sale, redefining how we interact with the physical retail world.

5. [Wildcat banking US $ lessons from the past that stablecoins can learn from](https://www.omfif.org/2025/06/wildcat-banking-2-0-stablecoins-and-the-dollar/?mc_cid=c97f8fba73&mc_eid=8fd65fb32f) - The US$’s transformation from specie-backed notes to today’s fiat currency has been shaped by wars, commerce, and technology. Now, US$ denominated stablecoins are redefining how money moves in the digital age, offering new efficiencies in payments, settlements, and financial inclusion. Yet this innovation revives historical concerns from the 19th-century wildcat banking era particularly regarding monetary fragmentation and stability. Stablecoins today vary in structure, regulation, and transparency, echoing the diverse and often unstable currencies of pre-Fed America. To preserve the US$’s role as a reliable medium of exchange, history teaches the need for consistent standards, strong oversight, and clear regulatory frameworks. With market projections showing stablecoins potentially scaling to trillions, their impact on the monetary system is accelerating. The EU is pursuing tight control via regulation and a digital €. The U.S. is taking a more open approach promoting an ecosystem of private, well-regulated stablecoins as it looks to introduce innovation through laws such as the proposed Genius Act and blocking a retail CBDC.

Ultimately, the lesson is clear: to safeguard the dollar’s integrity in the digital era, policy must blend historical insight with modern technology, ensuring stability without stifling innovation.

7. [GENIS Act in USA set to impact interest rates](https://www.investopedia.com/the-genius-act-could-have-interest-rate-implications-here-s-how-stablecoins-fed-11757379?utm_source=chatgpt.com) - The U.S. Senate’s has passed the **GENIUS Act** marking a historic moment for digital finance by establishing the first clear regulatory framework for **dollar-pegged stablecoins**. The law mandates **1:1 reserve backing** with high-quality liquid assets such as **short-term U.S. Treasuries,** effectively legitimising stablecoins as dollar proxies within the financial system. But this isn’t just crypto regulation its **monetary policy in disguise.** According to **Citigroup,** stablecoins could attract **$1.2 trillion in U.S. federal debt** by 2030, surpassing any single jurisdiction in Treasury holdings. This demand could drive **short-term yields down,** lowering government borrowing costs at a time when **U.S. interest payments are on track to exceed $1 trillion annually.**

Yet this growing market comes with systemic risks. The **Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** warns that while inflows to stablecoins may lower yields slightly, **outflows during crises could spike yields sharply,** risking “fire sales” in the Treasury market. Concentrated holdings by stablecoin issuers could also **weaken the Fed’s control over interest rates,** complicating inflation management.

In short:
🔹 The U.S. is outsourcing dollar distribution to the private sector via stablecoins.
🔹 This strengthens dollar dominance globally but at the potential cost of **financial stability.**

The GENIUS Act could reduce Treasury costs, supercharge the stablecoin industry. But it also raises urgent questions: **Who controls money when code and capital move faster than central banks?**
