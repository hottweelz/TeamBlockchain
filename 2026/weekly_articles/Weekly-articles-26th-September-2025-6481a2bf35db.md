[**GENIUS act and MiCAR regulations, a transatlantic convergence on crypto?**](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/09/us-genius-act-eu-mica-convergence-crypto-rules/)The passage of the US GENIUS Act and the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) mark a pivotal moment in global cryptocurrency regulation, signalling a slow but crucial convergence between two leading jurisdictions. The global crypto ecosystem’s future hinges on such leadership, harmonizing legal perimeters, emphasising consumer protection, market integrity, and financial stability while trying to foster innovation. By mandating one-to-one reserve backing and redemption rights, they collectively restore trust in stablecoins, a concern that has haunted the sector following several high-profile collapses. Yet, the GENIUS Act goes beyond MiCA in its stringent prohibitions against bank-related risks and segregated balance sheet requirements for payment stablecoins, reflecting America’s unique banking structure and risk tolerance. It also empowers the Treasury to pursue regulatory harmonisation and passporting, potentially easing cross-border crypto operations. This transatlantic alignment challenges fears of regulatory arbitrage but also sets the stage for deeper cooperation, balancing state sovereignty with the need for a unified, trusted digital monetary system. Crucially, both frameworks reject big tech’s domination of digital money through strict guardrails, emphasizing the protection of monetary sovereignty and competitive markets. The global crypto ecosystem’s future hinges on such leadership, harmonising innovation with prudence to unlock an open, secure, and accessible digital economy that transcends jurisdictional boundaries.

[**UK FCA Cryptoasset consultation**](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp25-25-application-handbook-regulated-cryptoasset-activities) The FCA’s latest cryptoasset publication arguably marks a turning point in crypto regulation in the UK. With forthcoming Statutory Instruments to bring stablecoin issuance, custody, staking, trading platforms and other “qualifying cryptoasset activities” under the Financial Services & Markets Act (FSMA) regime, crypto firms must now seek formal authorisation for activities hitherto lightly regulated. At its core, it seeks regulatory parity: crypto firms should follow the same high-level principles, senior management standards, systems and controls, and operational resilience expectations as traditional financial firms. Notably, it extends SM&CR (Senior Managers & Certification Regime) to crypto-firms, making individuals accountable; it proposes that stablecoin issuers and custodians be subject to client assets regime (CASS, in certain cases); it mandates risk management, governance, and financial crime controls at the level expected of FSMA-authorised entities. But these ambitions raise profound tensions. Many crypto firms operate with lighter infrastructure, novel business models, and different risk profiles. The cost and complexity of compliance could stifle innovation or push firms offshore. Also, the paper is seeking feedback on how deeply to apply duties such as the Consumer Duty and Product Governance to cryptoasset services, and the balance between fostering innovation and enforcing robust protection is delicate. This consultation is more than policy drafting, it’s a roadmap shaping whether the UK will lead or lag in crypto-asset regulation. Will the new regime ensure consumer trust and market integrity without destroying the agility that makes crypto attractive?

[**Tokenising healthcare data to predict life expectancy using AI**](https://www.dkfz.de/en/news/press-releases/detail/ai-model-predicts-disease-risks-decades-in-advance)  In applying AI to predict life expectancy from health data, the process begins with tokenisation. Here, raw medical information is converted into structured formats, numerical codes or “tokens” that AI systems can interpret. Once tokenised, the data undergoes advanced processing, where machine learning models are trained on vast datasets of anonymised health records. These models detect patterns and correlations across variables such as genetic markers, lifestyle factors, and clinical history. From this foundation, AI builds predictive models capable of forecasting an individual’s health trajectory, highlighting potential risks, outcomes, and likely longevity. Using AI EMBL, DKFZ, and the University of Copenhagen trained on anonymised data from 400,000 UK Biobank participants and validated on 1.9 million Danish patient records, the system predicts the onset and timing of over 1,000 conditions, from diabetes to heart attacks, up to a decade ahead. By tokenising medical events into sequences, the model learns how lifestyle factors and prior illnesses interact to shape health trajectories. Unlike deterministic diagnoses, forecasts are expressed as probabilities, akin to weather predictions. While promising for personalised medicine and health system planning, limitations remain, underrepresentation of youth and ethnic minorities in training data, variable accuracy for rare or unpredictable diseases, and the absence of clinical deployment pathways. The research underscores AI’s potential to transform preventive healthcare but only with rigorous testing, transparency, and ethical safeguards. Then there is the opportunity to sell your healthcare data but that is another story!

[**Google and Paypal collaborate with enabling agentic shopping using Paypal’s stablecoin**](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46618/google-and-paypal-agree-multi-year-commerce-collaboration?ct=t(email_campaign_9_20_2025_21_6)&mc_cid=e8f3141643&mc_eid=562a6d0508)**.** PayPal and Google have entered a sweeping multiyear strategic partnership that includes deep integration of PayPal’s payment technologies across Google’s key products and services, and a shared push into AI-driven shopping and "agentic commerce" platforms. As part of this alliance, Google’s newly announced AI payment protocol incorporates stablecoin support, and both companies are actively advancing dollar-pegged crypto for seamless, programmable transactions between AI applications and human users. Specifically, PayPal’s stablecoin, PYUSD, is being expanded across multiple blockchains and is confirmed as a supported asset within Google’s AI payment system. PYUSD enables instant, transparent, and low-friction payments, making it attractive for both the next wave of digital commerce—where autonomous AI agents transact on users’ behalf—and for everyday user payments inside Google’s ecosystem. While both firms stress broader digital payments innovation—including credit cards, Hyperwallet, and P2P crypto links—the inclusion of [PYUSD positions PayPal’s stablecoin as a cornerstone](https://cointelegraph.com/news/google-ai-payment-protocol-stablecoin-integration) of their cooperation and a prominent player in Google’s move to blend cloud, AI, and crypto into future payment rails. This integration signals a major step towards mainstream stablecoin use in global tech platforms and hints at much wider adoption and experimentation with digital money in coming years.

[**UK and USA establish a Transatlantic Taskforce for Markets of the Future to enhance collaboration on capital markets, digital assets and other innovative financial activities.**](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/boosting-collaboration-between-uk-and-us-financial-systems-to-drive-innovation-and-growth-in-global-markets) The Chancellor of the Exchequer and the US Treasury Secretary have launched the Transatlantic Taskforce for Markets of the Future to deepen cooperation on capital markets, digital assets, and innovative financial activities. Tasked with reporting within 180 days, the group aims to streamline cross-border capital raising, align digital asset regulations while frameworks evolve, and explore long-term collaboration on wholesale digital markets. Chaired by officials from the UK and US finance ministries with regulator involvement, it seeks industry input to unlock growth opportunities, reduce regulatory burdens, and strengthen the financial ties of the two global centres. This initiative is critical to positioning the UK and US as leaders in the emerging digital economy and setting global standards amid the fast-evolving crypto landscape. This presents significant opportunities for the UK. by collaborating closely with the US, the UK can streamline cross-border capital flows, making London a premier hub for global digital asset fundraising and tokenised securities trading. It also enables the UK to influence evolving digital asset regulations on a global scale, promoting innovation-friendly standards that attract FinTech firms and institutional investors. Finally, the taskforce will hopefully encourage the UK to issue regulatory clarity in the same way the USA has with its GENIUS act.
