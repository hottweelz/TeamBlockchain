**Weekly articles 27th June 2025**

**1.** [**Programmable money but not using tokenisation**](https://cryptobriefing.com/technology-redefining-money-currency/) **-** Programmable money is possible with the need for tokenisation. While Ethereum and DeFi protocols pioneered self-executing transactions, banks and central banks are now embracing similar logic **without relying on public blockchains.** Through projects like the **Bank of England’s Project Rosalind**, firms like **Quant** are developing programmable systems for **traditional bank deposits,** enabling features like automated escrow, conditional bill payments, and smart compliance triggers.Unlike stablecoins, which mostly function as static digital cash, these “smart deposits” embed **business logic directly into conventional bank infrastructure** potentially reshaping how payments, payroll, and settlements work at scale.This raises critical questions: Are we entering an era where **money becomes a service** layered with automated rules? Will commercial banks, not crypto platforms, drive the mainstream adoption of programmable finance? And can such power be deployed responsibly, without sliding into surveillance or centralised control?

The next chapter in digital money isn’t just about tokenisation the real key is being able to offer programmable payments while being agnostic about the technology and it’s already possible in your bank’s back office.

2. [Musk’s X to launch payments and realise his ambition of a super App](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/musks-x-to-launch-trading-and-payments-in-push-toward-everything-app-report/?utm_source=FMnewsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=20.06.25)- Elon Musk’s X (formerly Twitter) is moving closer to becoming an “everything app” with the upcoming launch of **X Money**, a digital wallet and payments service set to debut in the U.S. in partnership with **Visa.** According to CEO Linda Yaccarino, users will soon be able to **trade, invest, tip creators, and make purchases directly within the platform**. The initiative mirrors China’s WeChat, blending finance, commerce, and social media. X is also developing **a physical or virtual card product** and plans to integrate AI-driven financial services following its acquisition by Musk’s **xAI**. If successful, X’s financial expansion could redefine the intersection of fintech, social media, and commerce in the West in the same way WeChat has in Asia.

3. [Revoult looks to issue a stablecoin](https://decrypt.co/326074/neobank-revolut-launching-stablecoin) Revolut’s rumoured entry into the stablecoin market is yet another example of a FinTech looking to issue its own form of money. This announcement follows [Amazon Expedia and Walmart](https://cryptobriefing.com/retailers-embrace-stablecoins/) all who are being tipped to launch their own stablecoins too. The key common attribute all these companies share are global brands and more importantly distribution i.e. potential users. Revolut has 55 million users and its stablecoin could quickly scale, leveraging its established infrastructure and global footprint. Are we about to enter an era of “branded money” where companies issue tokens that lock users deeper into their ecosystems bypassing Visa, Mastercard, and even traditional banks.

Supporters hail this as innovation; critics like Sen. Elizabeth Warren warn of corporate surveillance and anti-competitive risks. Meanwhile, banks like JP Morgan and Bank of America prepare their own responses, eyeing yield, efficiency, and control. With the GENIUS Act expected to become law by August, the next few months may define whether the future of money belongs to governments, banks, or brands. One thing is clear: stablecoins are no longer fringe they’re fast becoming financial infrastructure.

4. [Why Tokenised Bank Deposits Could Redefine Digital Payments (and Banking)](https://investors.fiserv.com/newsroom/detail/2848/fiserv-launches-new-fiusd-stablecoin-for-financial-institutions)- **Tokenised deposits** unlike stablecoins that sit outside traditional banking, tokenised deposits as they remain on the bank’s balance sheet but can enable programmability with the safety and utility of regulated money. If this model scales, it could challenge the very foundations of the current payment stack, rendering today’s real-time payment networks and settlement rails obsolete. Arguably the real disruption isn't in launching a stablecoin it's in reimagining the **core ledger of the banking system**. As infrastructure giants like Fiserv in the USA embrace programmable money, a new question emerges: will the next generation of financial infrastructure be built by today’s incumbents or born from entirely new networks, designed to settle value at the speed of software?

What if the most revolutionary use of blockchain isn’t in DeFi, but in quietly rewiring the plumbing of global finance?

5.[Mastercard expands stablecoin integration with new partners](https://thepaypers.com/online-mobile-banking/mastercard-expands-stablecoin-integration-with-new-partners--1274247) - Mastercard’s expanding embrace of stablecoins isn’t just a sign of innovation it’s a pre-emptive strike in a brewing power shift within the financial system. As digital US$ such as PYUSD, FIUSD, and USDC gain traction, and retailers like Walmart and Amazon eye their own stablecoins, the very foundation of card-based payments is being quietly challenged. Mastercard’s partnerships with Paxos, PayPal, Fiserv, and major crypto platforms reveal a calculated bid to remain indispensable not by resisting change, but by embedding itself into the architecture of programmable money.

Yet this move also raises uncomfortable questions: if stablecoins can be sent peer-to-peer, instantly and cheaply, what happens to the traditional fee-based model Mastercard is built on? And as stablecoin networks mature, will legacy financial infrastructure enhance innovation or stand in its way? In trying to bridge the old world and the new, Mastercard might not just be facilitating digital payments it may be racing to avoid being left behind by them.

6. [US and UK special relationship extends to digital assets including stablecoins](https://news.bitcoin.com/googles-quantum-breakthrough-quietly-inches-closer-to-breaking-bitcoin-nydig/) - The latest US-UK Joint it marks the consolidation of a transatlantic power bloc shaping the future of digital finance. By aligning legislative efforts on stablecoins, digital securities, and payment systems, Washington and London are building a shared financial architecture that could set global standards, sidelining less aligned jurisdictions. Behind the scenes, this coordination suggests a strategic recalibration: a move to pre-empt fragmented crypto governance and solidify Western influence over digital asset innovation, enforcement, and infrastructure. With AI regulation and accelerated settlement cycles also on the table, the scope extends beyond crypto into the very mechanisms that underpin global capital flows. But as these two financial superpowers integrate oversight and infrastructure, a question emerges: will this alliance foster healthy innovation or reinforce legacy gatekeepers under a digital veneer? As other nations pursue sovereignty through CBDCs or decentralised rails, the US-UK pact might represent not just cooperation but containment.

7.[The world’s biggest non Chinese bank launches programmable money for institution](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/jp-morgan-to-launch-jpmd-deposit-token-on-public-blockchain-base/)s- JPMorgan’s launch of the **JPMD token** on Coinbase’s Base blockchain is the first time a globally systemic bank moves high-volume institutional cash settlements onto a public blockchain. Thus representing the quiet institutionalisation of stablecoin infrastructure permissioned, precise, and purely for institutions By issuing a **tokenised deposit** not a stablecoin JPMorgan is asserting a future where **programmable money doesn’t disintermediate banks** but reinforces them with blockchain-powered liquidity rails. It replaces multi-day cross-border settlements with instant, on-chain internal transfers from London to Hong Kong. The implications are profound. With **real-time settlement** backed by this model could outcompete stablecoins, reshape money market infrastructure, and blunt the momentum of asset managers eyeing their own stablecoins. The Base blockchain a semi-decentralised, regulator-friendly Layer2 blockchain offers just enough openness without surrendering control.

In a post-GENIUS Act world, where regulation gives stablecoins legitimacy but also elevates competitive pressure, the next question: which bank copies them next, and how fast?
