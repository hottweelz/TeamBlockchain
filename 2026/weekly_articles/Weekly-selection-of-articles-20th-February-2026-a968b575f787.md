For the second time in two weeks, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission has declared tokenisation integral to the financial system's future. Commissioner Mark Uyeda's February 2026 remarks at the Asset Management Derivatives Forum crystallise the shift: properly implemented tokenisation enhances security, transparency, and immutability while reducing intermediary reliance, streamlining transaction lifecycles, and lowering costs without sacrificing investor protections. This isn't pilot-stage experimentation, the SEC is actively approving applications to tokenise investment funds and Treasury clearing, signalling that blockchain-based securities infrastructure is becoming operational reality. The implications for financial markets are seismic. Tokenisation collapses settlement from days to seconds, increases ownership visibility, and enables direct issuer-investor interactions on programmable rails. The SEC's approval of new Treasury clearing agencies (CME, ICE Clear Credit) alongside expanded cross-margining arrangements confirms that traditional multi-layered intermediation is being redesigned around instant, on-chain finality. This directly challenges legacy systems dependent on batch processing and correspondent banking, both structurally incompatible with tokenised assets that trade continuously and settle atomically. Here's why digital payments are no longer optional but foundational: tokenised securities demand tokenised money. When a Treasury bond exists as a blockchain token, settling the transaction requires blockchain-native currency, stablecoins or central bank digital currencies, not wire transfers that take days and introduce counterparty risk. The SEC's embrace of tokenisation implicitly mandates programmable payment rails. Without them, the efficiency gains collapse: atomic delivery-versus-payment, instant cross-margining, and real-time collateral optimisation all require money that moves at the speed of code, not correspondent banks. Financial markets are being rewired around a simple truth: if assets are digital and autonomous, so must be the money that buys them.

[**Cambridge University report of Tokenised Money, uses cases, regulation and interoperability**](200%20words%20synopsis%20and%20what%20does%20these%20mean%20for%20business%20https%3A/www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/2026-ccaf-tokenised-money-use-cases-interoperability-and-regulation.pdf)**.** The report identifies four primary use cases driving adoption: **cross-border payments**, **treasury management**, **trade finance**, and **capital markets infrastructure**. While stablecoins currently dominate for retail and DeFi payments, institutional players are increasingly adopting tokenised deposits for real-time liquidity management and 24/7 settlement. A critical barrier to scaling is interoperability, as fragmented networks risk creating "walled gardens". To address this, the report highlights major initiatives like Project Agorá and **the Regulated Settlement Network (RSN)**, which aim to unify commercial and central bank money on programmable platforms. Furthermore, programmability is emerging as a "catalytic" feature, enabling automated trade finance, AI-driven commerce, and "just-in-time" treasury optimization.

Regulatory landscapes remain fragmented but are accelerating. The U.S. GENIUS Act of 2025 is identified as a pivotal catalyst that provided federal legitimacy to payment stablecoins, prompting global competitors to refine their own frameworks to avoid "dollarisation" and preserve monetary sovereignty.

### **What This Means for Business**

* **Operational Efficiency**: Businesses can move from "just-in-case" to **"just-in-time" liquidity**, potentially eliminating the need for complex treasury forecasting by enabling instant global fund transfers.
* **Reduced Transaction Costs**: Tokenisation can drastically lower the cost of cross-border payments—from an average of $50 and five days in traditional banking to potentially **cents and seconds**.
* **Atomic Settlement**: Integrating tokenised money with trade finance allows for **Delivery versus Payment (DvP)**, where payments are automatically triggered by the digital transfer of trade documents, eliminating counterparty risk.
* **Embedded Compliance**: Businesses can utilize **smart contracts** to automate regulatory reporting and AML/KYC checks directly within the payment flow, reducing manual reconciliation and audit overhead.
* **Access to Yield**: Tokenised MMFs enable corporate treasurers to earn yield on "resting" balances while maintaining the instant liquidity typical of cash

1. **[Google's 100 duration Bond: Locking in AI Financing Before the Window Closes](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/alphabet-plans-tech-first-100-163611765.html).** Alphabet is issuing its first 100-year sterling bond, the first tech firm to sell such extreme-maturity debt since Motorola in 1997. The deal, part of a massive multi-currency fundraising alongside dollar and Swiss franc tranches, follows Alphabet's announcement that capital expenditures will reach $185 billion in 2026, double last year's spending, to finance AI infrastructure ambitions.

**The Paradox: Why 100-Year Debt When Stablecoins Offer Short-Term Liquidity?** While stablecoin markets provide instant, flexible short-term financing, Google is betting on century-long certainty for several strategic reasons. First, AI infrastructure represents generational capital commitments, data centres, chips, and energy grids with 30-50 year lifespans require matching long-term financing to avoid rollover risk. Second, current interest rate environments may represent a narrow window before costs rise; locking in rates now hedges against future volatility. Third, diversification across investor bases: pension funds and insurers seek ultra-long duration assets unavailable in stablecoin markets, providing stable capital that won't flee during market stress. Stablecoins optimize for operational velocity, paying suppliers, settling transactions instantaneously. But strategic infrastructure financing requires patient capital immune to redemption runs. Google's 100-year bond signals confidence that AI will dominate beyond current business models, essentially mortgaging future AI dominance against today's construction costs. The risk: technological obsolescence making century-long commitments absurd, as J.C. Penney's 1997 century bond demonstrated when bankruptcy arrived just 23 years later.
