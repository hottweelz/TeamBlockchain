[**Why blockchain’s confidentiality problem is holding back banking**](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/comment/why-blockchain-confidentiality-problem-is-holding-back-banking/?cf-view)Blockchain has proven its worth in finance with efficiency gains in settlement, tokenisation, and collateral management, as BlackRock and JPMorgan’s Tokenised Collateral Network recently demonstrated. Yet a fundamental flaw keeps banks from embracing the technology at scale: a lack of confidentiality. Public blockchains expose balances, counterparties, and transaction histories to anyone. While transparency is acceptable for retail crypto markets, it is untenable for private banks, hedge funds, or wealth managers, where discretion and client privacy are paramount. This tension has pushed many potential blockchain applications in finance into pilot stages or abandonment. However, growing interest in privacy-preserving technologies suggests a turning point. **Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE)** is emerging as the most promising fix, enabling data to remain encrypted even while being processed. This means transactions, risk models, and smart contracts could be executed on-chain without exposing sensitive details, combining blockchain’s composability with institutional-grade confidentiality. Historically, FHE faced barriers around speed, scalability, and usability. But advances in GPU acceleration, developer-friendly tools, and projections of 100x performance improvements are making it increasingly viable. Apple, IBM, and blockchain-native firms are now experimenting with integrating FHE into real-world systems, signalling momentum. If adopted, FHE could transform blockchains from transparency-first networks into privacy-preserving financial infrastructures suitable for global banking. The question is no longer whether blockchain can support finance, but whether confidentiality technology can scale fast enough to meet surging institutional demand. Whoever solves this paradox may define the next generation of financial infrastructure.

[**AI the rise of the new industrial order**](https://www.mike-walsh.com/blog/digital-keiretsu) As we see an increasing use of humanoid and bots in industry are we to see a rising demand for digital powered machine to machine payments? Are we seeing the dawn on Japan’s keiretsu? Hong Kong has long embodied the role of a crossroads, a living bridge between East and West, and today it finds itself at the heart of a deeper industrial transformation. The city and the wider Greater Bay Area remain central to global supply chains, yet the very concept of industrialisation is being redefined. What was once seen as the unglamorous machinery of progress is now recast as the foundation of resilience in an era of climate shocks, pandemics, and volatile geopolitics. The 2010s vision of Industry 4.0, factories filled with sensors and flexible automation, proved partial, creating islands of innovation rather than systemic change. The new wave is more ambitious: intelligent networks of suppliers, logistics hubs, and energy grids orchestrated by AI, digital twins, and real-time data. In this emerging “digital keiretsu,” trust is not rooted in equity or geography but in code. Case studies illustrate this shift. Walmart’s Scintilla integrates suppliers into a retail nervous system; Shein enforces algorithmic trust across thousands of micro-factories; Amazon federates intelligence through its fulfilment platform; and Tesla turns factories into living software ecosystems. Each builds networks behaving more like organisms than chains. The implications are profound. Industrialisation is moving from efficiency to resilience, from siloed factories to interconnected systems capable of responding collectively to disruption. These networks are not just about cheaper goods, they are about civilizational endurance. If the consumer internet shaped how we connect, the industrial internet may determine how societies survive. Thus, a future where industrial intelligence becomes a shared global network, reweaving supply chains into ecosystems of resilience rather than fragility.

[**FX prop traders’ thoughts on trading digital assets 24/7**](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/37-of-proprietary-traders-optimistic-in-247-debate-as-fx-crypto-tokenization-grow/?utm_source=FMnewsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=12.09.25) The proprietary trading firms are grappling with the realities and implications of a move toward 24/7 trading in traditional markets. While 37% of traders embrace it for its potential to increase revenues and responsiveness to unfolding news, 38% remain wary, citing staffing, operational fatigue, and infrastructure strains as dominant concerns. The optimism is highest among US-based participants and those deploying ultra-low latency strategies; conversely, firms with less advanced technology are sceptical. Support for continuous trading is reinforced by its ability to minimize overnight risk and facilitate global alignment. Still, more than 80% of respondents highlight the acute operational demands this model would impose, from round-the-clock risk management to liquidity and payment access challenges. Despite such reservations, **80% believe 24/7 trading is inevitable within a decade**. Exchange liquidity programs, regulatory headwinds, and talent scarcity, particularly the fusion of trading and AI expertise, are additional forces influencing sector performance. Transitioning to 24/7 trading necessitates a complete reimagining of operations. Firms must introduce round-the-clock staffing, upgrade surveillance and risk systems for continuous oversight, and strengthen technical infrastructure for near-zero downtime. Clearing and settlement processes must shift to real-time, often requiring prefunded margins and after-hours liquidity arrangements. Firms will also contend with fragmented liquidity, wider spreads, and more volatile pricing during off-peak hours, demanding new liquidity management strategies and robust investor protections. Regulatory adaptation, enhanced circuit-breakers, and transparent communication of overnight risks are critical. So as ever yes 24/7 looks attractive but the adoption and implications is not without its cost.

[**Deposit Tokens: The Banking Response to Stablecoins**](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5378363) (Written by ex-CEO of Fnality) - The cross-border payment system is burdened with inefficiency due to the outdated, fragmented correspondent banking model, creating costly, slow, and opaque "long payment corridors." This system requires payments to pass through multiple intermediaries bound by bilateral credit lines, adding fees and delays, compounded by asynchronous banking hours, batch processing, multiple FX conversions, and increasing regulatory compliance burdens. In response, stablecoins have emerged as a digital solution offering 24/7 global availability, near-instant settlement, lower costs via direct peer-to-peer transfers, and enhanced transparency using blockchain technology. Their programmability enables automation of escrow and conditional payments, streamlining cross-border transactions. However, stablecoins have introduced new intermediaries to manage yield distribution, complicating regulatory oversight and potentially undermining decentralisation goals. Traditional banks are responding by developing deposit tokens, digital representations of bank deposits on public blockchains, that preserve regulatory protections, offer native interest payments, and leverage existing client trust. Despite their promise, both stablecoins and deposit tokens face challenges including AML compliance, risk management, cybersecurity, and interoperability. Regulatory convergence, particularly in anti-financial crime measures like the FATF’s Travel Rule, is crucial to levelling the playing field. The future of cross-border payments lies in a hybrid ecosystem where digital assets and traditional finance co-evolve, fostering faster, cheaper, more transparent global money flows while navigating complex risks.

[**Adoption of AI globally and its impact on economies**](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)**.** This report has looked at the Claude.AI (one of the leading AI large language model services) conversations and how businesses are actually using AI. It confirms the unprecedented speed and the striking unevenness of global AI adoption. AI’s rapid uptake, 40% of U.S. employees now use it at work, far outpaces historical adoption of other technologies such as electricity or the internet, largely thanks to its ease of use and continual capability improvements. However, the geography of AI adoption remains highly concentrated: wealthy, technologically advanced countries such as Singapore, US, and Israel exhibit far higher per-capita usage, while emerging economies lag behind. Within the U.S., this unevenness is mirrored at the state level; DC and Utah outpace even California, reflecting both local economies and sectoral specialization. AI usage is not just about automation versus augmentation, it’s also about the diversity of economic activity. As AI adoption deepens, the mix of tasks evolves from simple coding or automation to more complex, collaborative, and knowledge-driven applications, suggesting a possible bifurcation in how societies and firms leverage AI: less affluent or early-stage adopters rely more on automating discrete tasks, while advanced economies harness AI for augmentation and collective problem-solving. Enterprise data supports this, businesses overwhelmingly use Claude for automation (77% of API usage), especially coding and administrative workflows, yet weak price sensitivity signals that capability and context, not cost, drive transformative deployment.

This uneven diffusion carries profound implications. If productivity gains remain concentrated in already prosperous regions, AI could exacerbate global economic inequality, potentially reversing decades of progress in growth convergence. Policymakers and business leaders are thus urged to tackle these divides, not only to realise AI’s full productivity potential but to promote a broader, more equitable diffusion. Open-sourcing the data aims to foster rigorous independent research and evidence-based decisions. AI revolution is not only a story of soaring adoption rates, but one of who benefits, where, and for which tasks, and the collective choices still ahead for inclusive prosperity.

[**Banks navigating digital assets**](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2025/09/16/banks-that-own-crypto-navigating-the-new-frontier-of-digital-assets/?kuid=4f11d912-1456-4af3-ba0d-cbe059339ae9-1758020400&kref=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.disruptionbanking.com%2F)- The rapid integration of cryptocurrencies into traditional finance marks a paradigm shift. Major banks, including JPMorgan Chase and Morgan Stanley, have transitioned from scepticism to strategic crypto investment, primarily leveraging Bitcoin ETFs for exposure. Direct Bitcoin ownership offers control but entails operational risks, pushing many banks toward regulated ETFs for safer client access. This trend reflects a broader banking strategy to diversify portfolios, hedge inflation, and meet growing client demand for digital assets amid volatile markets. Despite regulatory uncertainties, banks are quietly accumulating crypto holdings and investing in custody solutions, signalling a shift toward mainstream acceptance rather than speculative hype. Future growth will likely involve expanding digital asset offerings, including Ethereum and stablecoins, shaping a diverse institutional crypto ecosystem. As regulatory clarity improves, banks’ active roles in crypto could redefine capital markets, fuelling innovation while posing new risk management challenges in the evolving digital asset era.

[**Stablecoins: adoption, optimism and regulatory clarity**](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/financial-services/cost-savings-and-speed-drive-stablecoin-adoption) - The EY report highlights the accelerating adoption of stablecoins, driven predominantly by cost savings, transaction speed, and the recent regulatory clarity established through the GENIUS Act. The GENIUS Act, enacted in July 2025, lays foundational standards for USD stablecoins, including alignment between issuers and regulators, formal approval processes, clear reserve requirements, tax guidance, and custodial rules. This regulatory certainty has significantly increased industry optimism, prompting financial institutions and businesses to invest in stablecoin solutions at record levels. The survey results show universal awareness among respondents and a strong expectation for stablecoins to deliver transformative efficiency and value within payments, treasury management, and cross-border transactions. Firms anticipate stablecoins will lower operational barriers and costs, while enabling real-time transfers in a compliant and transparent manner. EY emphasizes that successful adoption requires multidisciplinary approaches—robust risk management, strategic deployment, and ongoing adaptation to regulatory evolution. The GENIUS Act’s regulatory roadmap is credited for unlocking stablecoins’ potential as resilient infrastructure for digital money, suggesting both the private and public sectors will harness these innovations to streamline fund flows and modernize financial services in the coming years.
