**Weekly articles 4th July 2025**

**1.** [**Why is digital asset adoption accelerating by Goldman Sachs**](https://www.goldmansachs.com/what-we-do/investment-banking/insights/articles/why-digital-asset-adoption-is-accelerating/why-digital-asset-adoption-is-accelerating.pdf) - Far from being a passing trend, digital assets ranging from tokenised securities to blockchain-powered programmable payment are being actively embraced by the traditional finance sector. This evolution is not driven by hype but by a growing recognition of the operational efficiencies, transparency, and programmability that blockchain technology can offer. At the heart of this transformation is the convergence between traditional finance (TradFi) and decentralised finance (DeFi). Institutional players are no longer just observers they are participants building bridges between legacy systems and emerging digital ecosystems. The growing clarity in regulation, particularly around stablecoins and digital securities, is accelerating this shift, offering firms a clearer roadmap to engage with tokenisation, on-chain settlement, and smart contract automation. Goldman argues that as the market infrastructure matures, barriers to entry are lowering. Innovations such as programmable money, tokenised money market funds, and real-time trading and settlement environments promise not just cost savings, but a reimagining of how capital is mobilised, and value is exchanged. This isn't just about making existing systems more efficient it’s about reengineering them to be more inclusive, global, and real-time.

The report forces us to rethink the boundaries of financial innovation. If tokenised assets can be traded instantly, without intermediaries, and settled 24/7, then why should we continue to operate within legacy frameworks constrained by time zones, batch processing, and central clearinghouses? As central banks, financial institutions, and regulators grapple with these questions, the decisions they make now will shape the next generation of capital markets. The real provocation lies in the quiet but fundamental question this report raises: if the future of finance is programmable, permissionless, and decentralised, who will control it and will legacy institutions be ready to cede that control or reshape it in their own image?

**2.** [**Stablecoins are quietly reshaping global financial infrastructure.**](https://mmc.vc/research/stablecoins-entering-their-untethered-growth-era/)**-** Stablecoins are quietly reshaping global financial infrastructure and are increasingly becoming embedded in real-world financial services, international commerce, and even monetary policy discussions. This is not just an evolution of payment technology it is a redefinition of how digital value is stored, transferred, and trusted across borders. What makes this era “untethered” is not merely the scale of adoption, but the decoupling of stablecoin innovation from its original, anarchic crypto roots. We are witnessing a transition from wildcat experimentation toward institutional integration. As banks, FinTechs, and even central banks explore programmable, permissioned stablecoins, the stablecoin market is bifurcating into a dual-track system: one part optimized for global liquidity and high-frequency use in decentralised markets, the other designed for compliance-first, regulated environments.

But this hybrid evolution prompts deeper questions about power and purpose. If stablecoins become embedded into e-commerce, payroll, and savings products, what happens to central bank control over monetary supply? As U.S. dollar-backed stablecoins dominate global flows, what implications does this have for emerging market currency sovereignty? If corporations or Big Tech entities issue branded stablecoins, will money itself become a privatized, competitive service?

Arguably the institutions that embrace this shift intelligently will shape not only payment infrastructure but the very meaning of money in the digital age.

**4.** [**Blockchain and Tax: A Comprehensive Guide to the Intersection of Technology and Finance**](https://themerex.net/wp/blockchain-and-tax-a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-intersection-of-technology-and-finance/) **-** As blockchain technology continues to redefine global finance, its convergence with taxation introduces both opportunities and regulatory challenges. At its core, blockchain’s decentralisation, transparency, and immutability challenge the traditional tax infrastructure, which has historically relied on intermediaries and siloed data systems. In a world where digital assets transcend borders and are exchanged peer-to-peer, conventional tax regimes struggle to keep pace with new forms of wealth and transactional complexity. However, blockchain technology may be the very key to solving this conundrum. Its transparent ledger system can automate tax reporting, reduce evasion, and establish trust between taxpayers and authorities. Smart contracts can embed tax logic directly into transactions, allowing instant, automatic compliance. Imagine a DeFi yield protocol or NFT sale that calculates and remits taxes at the moment of execution, with a verifiable trail for auditors and regulators alike.

However, this transformation demands a shift in mindset and legal infrastructure. Most jurisdictions still treat crypto like an exotic anomaly, applying inconsistent rules or none at all. The borderless nature of blockchain underscores the urgent need for harmonised international frameworks. While some governments have begun to explore blockchain for tax enforcement (like Brazil’s blockchain-based e-invoicing or Estonia’s digital tax systems), many are playing catch-up. The ultimate question isn’t whether taxation will adapt to blockchain but how soon governments can harness this technology without stifling innovation. Will regulators embrace transparency, or will they be intimidated by a system that displaces their control? Will taxpayers gain more agency and automation, or face a more intrusive and opaque form of surveillance finance?

Taxation in the blockchain era isn’t just a compliance issue—it’s a test of how modern governance responds to decentralised power. Those who embrace this shift early may not only close tax gaps, but also build fairer, more efficient systems for the digital economy.

**7.** [**BIS Annual Economic Report 2025**](https://www.bis.org/publ/arpdf/ar2025e.pdf) - Global growth briefly steadied, with 2024 GDP rising ~3% and inflation cooling. But the calm shattered in early 2025 as sweeping U.S. tariffs rattled markets. The BIS warns this shake-up deepens existing fractures in trade networks, supply chains, and productivity dragging economies into an unstable era

Household inflation expectations, scarred by the post-pandemic shock, remain stubbornly high. With core inflation above target in many economies, central banks are caught in a delicate balancing act: controlling price growth without choking recovery

Fiscal pressures are rising too. Public debt has surged to peacetime record levels, heightening vulnerability to rate hikes and limiting governments’ ability to respond to future crises

Moreover, the financial system’s backbone is shifting. Non-bank financial intermediaries such as hedge funds and asset managers now dominate bond and derivatives markets. Their ill-regulated leverage exposes markets to sharp liquidity shocks particularly in times of stress

Amid these stresses, technological innovation stands out as both a risk and a solution. The BIS highlights **tokenisation and unified ledger frameworks** such as its **Project Promissa** and **Project Agorá** as ways to streamline securities, correspondent banking, and payments by integrating central bank money, commercial deposits, and bonds into a single, programmable platform

But the BIS is clear: innovation must be built on trusted foundations. Stablecoins, while useful for low-value transactions, fail to meet the standards required for a global monetary backbone. Instead, token-based systems must centre on central bank currency to preserve stability, integrity, and public confidence.
