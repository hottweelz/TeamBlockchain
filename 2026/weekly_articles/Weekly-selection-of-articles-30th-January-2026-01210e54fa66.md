[**When monetary innovation makes money obsolete**](https://www.omfif.org/2026/01/when-monetary-innovation-makes-money-obsolete/)Tokenisation threatens to solve money out of existence by eliminating the frictions that give it value. Money exists because converting assets into purchasing power is costly and slow. These transaction frictions create a liquidity premium that makes holding non-interest-bearing cash rational despite its opportunity cost. When any securities portfolio converts instantly into settlement medium without delay or cost, the cash-in-advance constraint disappears. Money becomes something obtained just-in-time for transactions and immediately reconverted to yield-bearing form. It persists as a unit of account and transient settlement instrument but vanishes as a store of value held across time. Required transaction balances equal payment flow multiplied by settlement time. As settlement time approaches zero, so does the money stock needed. The same unit intermediates arbitrarily large transaction volumes through near-instantaneous recycling. This is not barter; prices remain quoted in a single unit of account with centralized valuation. For banks, the implications are existential. The deposit-taking model rests on frictions that make maintaining balances rational. Remove those frictions and deposits evaporate. Bank balance sheets would increasingly resemble investment funds rather than traditional deposit-funded lending institutions. The boundary between banking and capital markets would blur into irrelevance. Money's perfection may eliminate the need to hold it.

[**Breaking the Blockchain Bottleneck: How IoT Networks Learn to Move Faster**](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366637702/Chiba-University-unveils-algorithm-to-reduce-blockchain-delay-in-IoT-networks)Blockchain technology promises secure, tamper-proof data sharing for the Internet of Things, but it has struggled with a critical weakness: latency. When autonomous vehicles, industrial sensors, and smart city infrastructure need split-second decisions, traditional blockchain networks are simply too slow. The culprit is not the blockchain protocol itself but the chaotic way peer-to-peer nodes communicate with each other. Researchers, at Chiba University in Japan, discovered that decentralised IoT networks suffer from redundant data transmission that causes exponential increases in duplicate copies, creating network congestion and queuing delays. The disorganised network topology, the shape and structure of connections between nodes, was causing the slowdown. Their solution, called Dual Perigee, is a lightweight algorithm that allows each node to intelligently score its peers based on delivery speed for both individual transactions and complete blocks. Slow neighbours get automatically disconnected and replaced, allowing the network to self-organize into optimal configurations without central control. Testing on a simulated fifty-node IoT environment showed remarkable results: block-related delays dropped by nearly half compared to standard Ethereum approaches, with minimal computational overhead. The system enables blockchain to support time-sensitive IoT applications from smart cities and healthcare systems to industrial monitoring and supply-chain tracking, where secure, real-time data sharing is essential for mission-critical operations.

As stablecoins like Tether (USDT) scale, they are displacing traditional FX settlement rails, moving the industry from a two-day settlement window (T+2) toward near-instantaneous (T+0) peer-to-peer transfers. This transition allows corporate treasuries to unlock significant liquidity by reducing capital buffers and bypassing the high costs associated with correspondent banking, where remittances currently average 6%. To address these risks, the upcoming **Bessant-Reeves standards**, anticipated in June 2026, are expected to mandate stricter transparency and reserve disclosure for stablecoin issuers.
