[**Digital Asset insurance is difficult due to a lack of historic data**](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/crypto-insurers-face-a-data-deficit-as-defi-exposure-grows-561662.aspx)the structural parallels between the early property market and the burgeoning digital asset space are becoming undeniable. Historically, the property market "took off" largely because **mortgage indemnity and title insurance** allowed lenders to provide high-percentage loans (often over 80%) with the security that their capital was protected against default or title defects. Before insurance was a standard requirement in the mid-to-late 1900s, homeownership was a luxury; insurance effectively expanded the "underwriting envelope," making the dream of ownership a mass-market reality. Today, digital assets are facing a similar "bottleneck". While $120 billion to $160 billion in assets sit in decentralized finance (DeFi), a staggering **95% to 98% remain uninsured**. This gap exists because, unlike property with 300 years of loss data, crypto has barely a decade. Carriers are hesitant to quote because this thin data profile makes pricing confidence impossible. However, the market is shifting. Traditional carriers are increasingly being forced into the space by client pressure from banks and asset managers who now require on-chain liability coverage for their operations. To compete, insurers are moving toward **"**code-level" underwriting, scrutinising smart contracts to gauge claim likelihood rather than relying on marketing claims. Just as title insurance became essential for a successful loan transaction, regulated insurance for DeFi vaults is beginning to emerge, signalling the start of a more resilient digital financial model.
